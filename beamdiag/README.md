# Beam diagram source notes

These facts came from comments that `scripts/check.sh` bans. Each one waits for a name, a constant, a check or a test in the code.

## Conventions

- Units are SI: m, N, N/m, N·m, Pa, m², m⁴. `UNIT_IDS` lists the consistent unit systems. `to_units` and `from_units` convert at the edge, and the solver sees only SI. A system gives the SI size of one unit of every quantity. Stress is force per length squared, so E·I/L³ stays in the system. `ascii` names the system where text must stay ASCII.
- Axes: x along the beam from its left end, +y up. Rotations and couples are counter-clockwise positive (+z out of the page). Point force F is positive up, couple C positive counter-clockwise, distributed load q varies linearly from x1 to x2 (N/m, positive up).
- Supports: a pin restrains deflection. A fixed support restrains deflection and slope. With only pins and fixed supports the structure is stable exactly when it has a fixed support or two separate pins.
- V(x) is the sum of the upward forces left of the section. M(x) is positive when the beam sags. dM/dx = V and dV/dx = q. Section shape codes are 0 none, 1 rect, 2 circle, 3 tube, 4 custom. Bending is about the horizontal axis, with the depth along y.
- The edited beam stays in SI. Values are converted only where typed or shown, so a change of unit convention re-expresses what was entered and never changes the beam or its results. The solver measures x from the left end. The origin option (mid-span) only changes the typed and shown positions, which then run from −L/2 to +L/2, still positive to the right.
- Degree of static indeterminacy for transverse loading: reaction components minus the two equilibrium equations.

## engine.goose

- Method: linear-elastic Euler–Bernoulli beam, direct stiffness. Two-node Hermite elements run between the supports and the beam ends only. Consistent (work-equivalent) nodal loads come from every point force, couple and distributed load inside each element. Nodal deflections and reactions are then exact, and indeterminate supports need no equilibrium-only shortcut. V and M are recovered exactly by statics from the loads and reactions. Deflection comes from integrating M/EI exactly from the nearest event on the left.
- The arithmetic follows the JavaScript engine it replaced, operation for operation, so the numbers are the ones the page has always shown. Powers are correctly rounded because JavaScript's `**` takes the platform `pow`, whose last bit varies. `dd_pow` computes x^n in double-double arithmetic, so its rounding to one double is x^n correctly rounded (x·x for a square, as everywhere). The reciprocal 1/(h + l) is correctly rounded as q plus the remainder of 1 − q·(h + l).
- The beam being worked on is in globals: the model (`g_m`, `g_sup`, `g_loads`) and its solution (`g_rea`, `g_disp`, `g_eq`, `g_ext`). A failure leaves its message and field in `err_msg` and `err_field`; `fail` returns false so a caller can `return fail(...)`.
- An unchecked model comes from the page or a test: NaN stands for a missing or non-numeric value, and a `has_` flag false for a value left out, which takes its default. `validate` checks and normalises `in_x`, `in_sup` and `in_loads` into `g_m`, `g_sup` and `g_loads`; the unit only sets how lengths are written in messages. A bad load kind from the page is −1; fields a kind does not use are ignored. A whole number is written as JavaScript's String, else six significant figures.
- `distinct` equals `[...new Set(xs)].sort((a, b) => a - b)` (−0 is 0). `events` lists ends, supports, point actions and load ends, and fails with the error set when two are too close. `intensity` takes the load starting at x (RIGHT) or ending there (LEFT).
- The band: beam DOFs couple only within one element, so a symmetric banded row keeps K[i][i..=i+BAND] with BAND = 3, stored flat, four to a row. Dropping constrained DOFs keeps the free system banded with the same band.
- `solve_banded` solves K u = f for symmetric positive definite banded K by LDLᵀ elimination. It first scales the system to a unit diagonal, which makes the pivot test independent of units and element lengths. A vanishing scaled pivot means a mechanism.
- Hermite shape functions on an element of length l at ξ ∈ [0, 1] are ordered v_i, θ_i, v_j, θ_j.
- Distributed-load nodal loads: ∫ N q dx over each element's share. N is cubic and q linear, so 5-point Gauss is exact.
- Deflection at every event: stations take the solved values. Other events integrate M/EI exactly from the previous event, and nothing happens strictly between two events. x = 0 is a station, so every other event has one before it. Between events M is at most cubic, so 3-point Gauss integrates M and (x − t)M exactly (θ' = M/EI, v' = θ).
- The equilibrium check is the resultant force and moment about x = 0 of all loads and reactions, which should vanish. A trapezoid is two triangles, q1 peaking at x1 and q2 peaking at x2. For V and M from the left free body at x, a distributed load is a rectangle q1 plus a triangle (qb − q1), where qb is the intensity at the end of the loaded part, each about the section at x.
- `diagram` makes mesh-independent samples: about `count` regular points plus per-segment critical points and event limits. Each segment runs from its right limit at the start to its left limit at the end, so jumps are two points at the same x. The diagrams close to zero at the free ends. Inside a segment q is linear and V quadratic: V(s) = C + B s + A s².
- The NASTRAN mesh (`mesh`) put `divisions` elements between consecutive events. Nothing on the page uses it now; see REQUIREMENTS.md, which removed NASTRAN.

## hand.goose

The stiffness solver in engine.goose is authoritative. The hand calculation writes the same answer as it is worked by hand, so a reader can follow and check it on paper.

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| reactions | Statically determinate: equilibrium, the sum of forces and of moments about a support. Indeterminate: compatibility (the force method) through Macaulay's double integration of EI v'' = M(x): two equilibrium equations, plus v = 0 at every support and v' = 0 at every fixed support, in the reactions and the two integration constants. |
| segments | Between consecutive supports and load points: V(x) and M(x) by the method of sections, then EI θ and EI v by integrating M, continuous from segment to segment. |
| a point | V, M, θ, v and the bending stress at any chosen x. |
| checks | Peak bending stress, and the sums of forces and moments of loads and reactions. |

- Every number shown is the solver's value at that step, written with the page's formatters, in the page's unit convention and origin of x. Where a written-out system would have more than 8 unknowns, the set-up and equations are stated generally and the solver's values are listed.
- The solved beam is the engine's globals. `hand_derive(u, mid)` reads them into the `hand_*` globals. Call `hand_invalidate()` when the solution changes and `hand_ensure(u, mid)` before reading. The steps are built on first use. One ordered list feeds the page and the PDF. An equation step holds its LaTeX and its Unicode text; any other step holds text.
- A load is in display units, positions from the display origin; n is the 1-based place in the model's list. Unknown kinds: 0 reaction R, 1 support moment M, 2 constant C_1, 3 constant C_2; r is the reaction it belongs to or −1. Equation kinds: 0 sum of forces, 1 sum of moments, 2 v = 0 at support `sup`, 3 θ = 0 at support `sup`. A Macaulay term is a coefficient, a position and a power.
- Terms: "k s" is added to a TeX sum with zero coefficients left out and 1 written as the symbol. A polynomial in s drops terms that are round-off at s = h. The bracket ⟨x − a⟩ⁿ is in the display coordinate and is zero left of a. The k-fold integral of the terms is taken at x.
- The load resultants give each load's vertical force and its moment about the display coordinate, counter-clockwise positive. A trapezoid is split into two triangles so a load whose ends have opposite signs needs no special case. A sum of formatted terms is written out when short, then totalled.
- Unknowns: each reaction force and fixed-support moment, then the integration constants. The solver's values of the constants are EI θ and EI v at the left end, re-expressed for brackets measured in the display coordinate. The model is in display units; only its length, rigidity and loads are used. Equilibrium: vertical, then moments about the left end.
- Segment polynomials in s = x − a come from the solver's values at a⁺. Zero shear inside the segment makes the bending moment stationary there, and only those inside are kept, in increasing order. Terms at the right end vanish everywhere on the beam and are left out. Start values are what the previous segment ends with plus the jumps at x = a. The end values come from the solver.
- The frame for a chosen x holds paragraphs and equations only; blocks are (eq, start, length) into `hand_pt_buf`. The result is in `hand_pt` and `hand_pt_title`.

## num.goose

JavaScript's number to text conversions, digit for digit: `String(x)`, `toPrecision`, `toExponential` and `toLocaleString("en-GB")` with fixed fraction digits. The page's formatters were written with them, so every number reads the same as it always has.

- Digits come from the C library's exact conversions (`shim.h`). JavaScript rounds the exact binary value, and the C library rounds half to even, so a tail that reads as exactly one half is settled from the exact digits. Every double has at most 767 significant digits, so 780 are exact. Only the first p + 1 digits of the exact expansion are needed.
- A digit string has a decimal exponent: x = d.ddd × 10^e. `read_sci` reads "d.ddde+XX". The shortest digits that read back as x (x finite and positive) come with n such that x = 0.d1d2… × 10^n. At a power of two the interval of numbers that read as x is lopsided, so a digit string next to the nearest one may be the shortest that reads back, as JavaScript finds it.
- `toLocaleString("en-GB", { minimumFractionDigits: f, maximumFractionDigits: f })`: ICU rounds the shortest decimal that reads back as x, half away from zero, then groups the thousands with commas. When all of x·10^f is below one, it rounds to 1 only from a first digit of 5 or more.
- A text of digits, sign, point and exponent is read as the correctly rounded number; it is 0 when it is too long.

## app.goose, state.goose, jv.goose, wasm.goose, host.c

- The program owns every number, word and drawing. The host in `page_host.js` only moves events in and applies the commands: set an element's HTML, value or class, save a file, copy text. `wasm.goose` is the WebAssembly interface: the host writes an event's JSON into memory (`host.c` `alloc` gives it room), calls `bd_handle` (`handle` in C) and reads the command JSON it returns (`out_len` is its length).
- `jv.goose` is a small JSON reader because the standard library's json tree reads back wrong on WebAssembly (its nodes hold slices behind relative offsets). It keeps only integers and numbers in its nodes and the decoded text in two pools. A node is an index; −1 is none. `raw.json` is read once (nodes below `j_mark`, text in `jp_raw`) and kept for the life of the program. Each event is read after it into `jp_ev`, and the previous event's nodes and text are dropped. `jm_rtext` reads raw.json text separately so that what its users keep never points into the pool an event drops.
- `data.goose` reads raw.json: presets, materials, sections, the method, conventions and sources. Strings are slices of the embedded file or its decoded text, which live as long as the program. A missing or mistyped string is "", a number NaN. Every preset names one of the materials. Shape code s (1..4) owns `FIELD_AT[s − 1]` up to `FIELD_AT[s]` of the field table (key, label, quantity, note). A dimension key is "b", "h", "d", "t", "A", "I", "Iy", "J" or "c".
- `state.goose`: one beam on the page. `st_preset` is "" for none. The section shape code is 1 rect, 2 circle, 3 tube, 4 custom, 0 none, and `st_shape_name` is as the page names it. `app_u` is an index into `UNIT_IDS`; the page opens on N, mm and MPa with the centre as origin. `app_attach` is the last valid length, to which positions are attached while the length field holds something else. `app_has_sol` means the engine globals hold a solved beam. `app_has_error` means `err_msg` and `err_field` hold why the edited beam is not solved. `app_active` is the handle being dragged ("s0", "l2"), or "". Typed lengths drop conversion noise (2300 mm is 2.3 m, not 2.3000000000000003 m).
- Rust parity helpers in app.goose and figure.goose copy the Rust the program replaced: `total_cmp` puts −0 before 0 and NaN after every number; `f64::min` and `f64::max` let a NaN give way to the other number; `str::parse::<f64>` accepts decimal text, "inf", "infinity" and "nan"; `Math.round` rounds halves up; JavaScript's `Number(s)` of "" is 0; a number field "" is NaN; text lengths are counted in UTF-16 units as JavaScript does, and in figure.goose in Unicode scalars.
- `+x.toPrecision(10)` is how a field holds a number, "" for NaN or infinity. A position or length typed lands exactly on any other position on the beam within 1e-9·L, because fields show 10 significant figures and typing back a shown value must give the position it shows. `me` says which field is being edited: 0 the length, 1 a support's x, 2 a load's x, 3 its x1, 4 its x2. An `Attach` records where a support or load end sat when a length edit began: `key` 0 is x, 1 is x1, 2 is x2; `end` is −1 for none, 0 or 1 at an end.
- E typed in psi only matches a preset to rounding, so presets are recognised within 1e-9.
- A beam solved in one convention can overflow when shown in another (10³⁰⁶ N·m is Infinity in N·mm), so a solved beam is copied and put back when the new solution fails. A re-solve waiting on paused typing is applied before an export or a drag. Re-deriving a long beam on every drag step is wasted work, so the view catches up when the drag ends. The hand calculation starts over for a new solution.
- Solver error messages quote left-end positions, and `at_message` rewrites them in the chosen convention. It reads three message shapes: "between 0 and (\S+) \S+\.$", "position x = (\S+) \S+\." and "^Positions (\S+) \S+ and (\S+) \S+ are" (the last splits its six space-separated words as `splitn(6, ' ')`). A failure in the section is not the beam's error, so the page keeps the message it shows.
- From mid-span, supports and loads at either end stay attached to it. Everything else keeps the position it was given, so it moves with the middle of the beam; a left-end entry keeps its place but reads differently, so it is refreshed too.
- A new support or load tries the usual spots first, then the middle of the widest gap between supports and ends. A default in SI is rounded to one significant figure in the current convention (−10 kN is −2000 lbf).
- Steps and snapping work in the current length unit, so a dragged support lands on 24 in, not 0.6 m. A zero length makes the exponent −∞: Rust's cast gives `i32::MIN` and its 10^MIN is 0.1 (the negation of MIN wraps). 10·p is at least raw, so a step is always found. A handle key ("s0", "l2") names a position slot.
- Round-off far below the largest value of the same quantity on this beam reads as zero in the readout. The readout at the cursor shows x, V, M, v and θ, with a jump as "left → right". The aria text of the readout is "k v, k v": the name and value joined by a space, the pairs by ", ".
- The selected point's hand calculation frame follows the cursor on the diagrams.
- Saving: the image and the PDF are drawn from the same display list as the page, entirely in the page, and nothing is uploaded. Saved figures always use the site's light colours, which print well. The saved SVG uses the print palette and the embedded fonts, so it looks the same anywhere. The A4 report keeps each row and diagram on one page. Its table has two columns, the model and the results. The result columns are x, what, V x⁻, V x⁺, M x⁻, M x⁺, v, θ, with numbers right-aligned at fixed edges and "what" from its left. The cell for "what is here" is empty when nothing is there.
- Fonts arrive as three non-empty base64 strings from the host (sans, heading, maths), and `font_data` holds their bytes one after the other. Base64 decoding skips anything but letters, digits, + and /.
- The page as it opens is a solved beam in N, mm and MPa with the centre as origin. Every region is filled in as the script would, so the page reads before the script runs.
- Text wrapping breaks lines of at most n characters at spaces and returns them separated by newlines.
- JSON string output escapes as `JSON.stringify` does.

## draw.goose and figure.goose

- A figure is a display list: lines, paths, rectangles, circles and text in CSS pixels, y down, with colours as the site's colour tokens (indexes into `PALETTE_NAMES`). The page draws it as SVG. A saved figure is the same SVG in the print palette, or a vector PDF (`pdf.goose`).
- The list is flat and is a `DOp[>..]` that the caller owns. An ITEM op is one shape. An OPEN op starts a group (its attributes are the text of a `<g>` tag) and a CLOSE op ends it, so a group is the ops from its OPEN to its matching CLOSE and a copy of a range of ops is a copy of those groups. A PAGE op separates the pages of a PDF report; a list has one more page than its page breaks. All strings (text, class names, group attributes) live in one builder, `draw_text_pool`, and the path segments in one array, `draw_segs`. An op holds (start, length) into them. `draw_reset()` empties both: call it before a new figure, when no older list is needed.
- Path segments are absolute: k is 0 M(a,b), 1 L(a,b), 2 C(a..f), 3 Z. Paint is a stroke colour token (−1 none) and width, and a fill token (−1 none) and its opacity. A line is (a, b)–(c, d). A rect is x a, y b, width c, height d, corner radius e. A circle is centre (a, b), radius c. A text has its baseline origin (a, b); `s` is the text of a text op or the attributes of an OPEN op, and `cls` is the class of an item. A group opens with its attributes, such as ` class="axes"`, or "" for none. A path op names a run of segments. An arrowhead has its tip at (x, y) and points `deg` degrees clockwise from up, as a closed path. `draw_node(list, k)` gives (0, 0) when there is none.
- Text styles by class: monospace, size (px), colour token, letter spacing (em), halo. An unknown class is styled as "val". Report headings use the site's sans at the weight of its page headings (600), and the rest of the report 400. The figure carries its own CSS so a saved SVG looks as the page does. An SVG coordinate has at most two decimals. The SVG root takes `attrs`, and `defs` (styles, fonts) come first inside it.
- The page's figure is interactive: supports and loads are handles (a slider to drag or move with the arrow keys), and the diagrams are a slider with a cursor. A saved figure is the same drawing without them. `figure()` appends the groups: the beam, the diagrams and, on the page, the cursor line. The caller supplies `readout_text(c)`, the aria text of the readout at section c.
- A diagram panel has a quantity key: 0 for V, 1 for M, 2 for v. Its title is `fig_text[t0..t0 + t1]`; its ticks and labels are the entries k0.. of `fig_ticks`. The figure's x scale is separate from the page's own (`lay_*` in state.goose) so a saved figure never touches it.
- `f64::max` and `f64::min` of Rust ignore a NaN argument. The tick step search needs 10·p at least raw, so a step is always found. Tick labels on one axis share a format: plain below 10⁵, otherwise a power of ten. An 11px monospace axis label width and an 11px Inter panel title width are rounded up. A character count counts Unicode scalars.
- Layout: the right margin holds the x axis's arrowhead and its label. The beam panel is first, from the top. Spans are halved before subtracting so loads near the double limit cannot overflow. The left margin widens only when a tick label would not fit the usual one. The moment's title would run past the right edge on a figure as narrow as a phone's: it drops "Bending", and starts further left if it still does not fit. The title starts just right of the value axis.
- Distributed loads draw arrows scaled to the largest intensity. With many close supports only those whose text has room get a reaction label; every reaction is still listed below the figure and in the table. A point force or couple is capped because, while an edit is unsolved, F can exceed the largest force of the solved model. Axes: x along the beam from the chosen origin, y up through it. A value axis is labelled by the panel title at its tip, with x along the zero line. The extremes are labelled.
- The x axis is an arrow to the right, labelled past its tip. Position ticks are numbered in the chosen convention and drawn at their place on the beam. An arrowhead points down (dir 1) or up (−1).

## tex.goose

- The small TeX subset of the hand calculations is drawn as HTML without a maths library: text, fractions, superscripts and subscripts. The Rust built a node tree and flattened it. Here one recursive pass writes the flat text directly (mode `TEX_TEXT` for accessible labels, `TEX_UNICODE` for the PDF and the page's first draw). Both end by closing up all runs of white space, so merging neighbouring strings first is not needed. `tex_i` is the read position in the source being parsed. The source is read byte by byte; only a character consumed as one unit (after ^ _ \frac \left \right or a backslash) is stepped over whole.
- HTML text and attribute escaping. White space is measured as Rust's `char::is_whitespace` and collapsed as `s.split_whitespace().collect::<Vec<_>>().join(" ")`. The next character is set as TeX sets maths: `-`, `'` and `=` become their maths forms. A symbol command not known gives false. The text written since a start becomes superscript or subscript characters (digits, - + i n) in place. As in TeX, a control word swallows the spaces after it. The parser reads to the end character (a code, or −1 for none), which it consumes.
- Forms: plain text for accessible labels; Unicode maths for the PDF, from the same equation as the page; text with inline $maths$; and text with inline $maths$ and every ×10ⁿ number typeset, where the digits, points, commas and minus signs just before the ×10ⁿ are typeset with it. The length of the ⁰¹²³⁴⁵⁶⁷⁸⁹⁻ characters that start s is in bytes.

## pdf.goose

- A display list becomes a vector PDF in the print palette, one page per run of ops between page breaks. Text is set in the embedded subsets of the site's font, Inter at 400 and at 600 for headings (TrueType), and Fira Math (OpenType CFF) for maths. It is addressed by glyph ID with a ToUnicode map, as the site's PDFs are. A character missing from a font falls back to Inter 400, then Fira Math. Nothing reads a clock, so equal figures give equal bytes.
- The three fonts (sans, heading, maths) are OpenType bytes. A character's glyph is read from the font's cmap on demand and remembered in `pdf_cache`: per font and 16-bit character, 0 not looked up, 1 not in the font, else the glyph ID + 2. A second table holds, per font and glyph ID, the character set with it (0 when unused). A font record holds where it sits in `pdf_data`, its tables and metrics (bbox, ascent, descent, units per em). Reads are big-endian of n bytes at offset o, and give 0 when they are not all in the font. In the cmap later format 12 groups replace earlier ones, and a format 4 segment fills only a character not yet mapped.
- A run of text is split into pieces, each in the first font that has the character (the style's, then sans, then maths); the glyph IDs of the pieces are in `pdf_glyphs`. Level text sets superscripts and subscripts at a size and rise in ems. The subsets lack most script digits, so these are base digits made small and moved.
- Content streams: classes drawn only on the page (grips, focus rings, the plot frame and the cursor) are skipped, with everything in a group of such a class. A transform group is a saved graphics state with that translation. The page's CTM flips y, so each run's matrix flips it back. A figure in CSS px (96 to the inch) becomes PDF pages of the same size in points; the list holds the pages separated by page breaks (`draw_page`), and the fonts are the bytes of sans, heading and maths.
- Objects: `pdf_add_from` adds an object whose body is in `pdf_ob` from an offset and gives its number; stream objects are `<<dict/Length n>>stream\ndata\nendstream`. The opacities used anywhere are collected in order of first use. ToUnicode maps each glyph used back to its character, in blocks of at most 100. The four fixed objects come last.

## page_host.js and page_style.css

- The toast, after Sonner (https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-toast-component): the newest toast is in front. The timer stops while the pointer is on the stack or the tab is hidden. A loading toast stays until the next toast takes its place. The toaster is fixed and each toast is absolute: the toasts are a stack of layers at the same place. A toast's `::after` starts inside the 1 px border, and it covers the border, the 14 px gap and a subpixel margin so the pointer does not leave the stack.
- Saved PNG pixels are doubled for a sharp print, within what every browser's canvas allows. A focused element that was drawn again keeps the focus.
- Some sizes follow their parent text, so a rem value is wrong there.

## reference.py

`reference.py` writes the exact Macaulay answers (`reference.json`) from `fixtures.json`; `--check` exits 1 if `reference.json` is out of date. Unknowns are R_i for each support, M_i for each fixed support, then C1 and C2. Known load terms of M(x) are (coef, a, power) triples meaning coef·⟨x − a⟩^power. The equations are the sum of vertical forces and the sum of moments about x = 0. The support conditions come from EI v = Σ coef ⟨x − a⟩^(p+2) / ((p+1)(p+2)) + C1 x + C2. The NASTRAN bulk data reader reads the subset BEAMDIAG wrote: an exponent may lack the E (1.0+5), blank CP, CD and PS fields on a GRID take their values from GRDSET, and the bars must form one chain from x0 to x0 + L.
