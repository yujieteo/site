Physics and field theory

  • Fermionic antisymmetry is encoded by anticommuting geometry: repeated states vanish, reflecting the Pauli exclusion principle.
  • Independent, Gaussian, and noninteracting systems are the free cases; this is where parallel decomposition is most natural.
  • Gauge theories describe field configurations as differential-cohomology cocycles, with vector bundles and connections as basic examples.
  • Deformation quantisation studies families of noncommutative algebras parametrised by admissible values of Planck's constant.
  • The cobordism hypothesis says framed cobordism is freely generated by a fully dualisable object. Noncompact variants lead to Calabi–Yau objects; a closed TQFT is determined by its value on circles. A conformal field theory is a functor on conformal cobordisms.
