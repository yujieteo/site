This is the same exercise as [the Hacker News ranking](/essays/hn-forecasters/), run on LessWrong. It looks for people who have been right several times, and it counts what they got wrong as well.

It covers 3,843 comments and posts with forecast wording, written between 2005 and 2021 by 545 authors. 566 held a real forecast about the world. 247 came true, 78 partly came true, 140 failed and 101 cannot be checked yet. The rest were not real forecasts: bets about other commenters, thought experiments and claims with nothing to check.

The text comes from [a public crawl of LessWrong ↗](https://huggingface.co/datasets/x65617379/lesswrong_260509) made in May 2026. That crawl leaves out quick takes, so forecasts made there before 2022 are missing. A large share of the forecasts come from the yearly prediction threads, where people listed forecasts with odds.

The open forecasts are also on [one page](/forecasts/), with the Hacker News ones.

Names appear as each person shows them on LessWrong. Nobody was traced beyond what they published.

## The ranking

The order is a judgment. It weighs how many calls came true, how often the person missed, and how hard the calls were. A forecast that the status quo will hold counts for less than a dated call that others doubted. A stated chance of 70% or more for something that happened counts as right, and 30% or less counts as wrong. A person needs three calls that came true to be listed.

## How this was made

1. A pattern search found comments and posts up to 2021 with forecast wording, such as "I predict", "I would bet" or a stated chance with a date. It also took the comments under prediction threads.
2. An AI model read each one. It kept real forecasts about the world, restated each in one line, and judged the outcome from what it knew in November 2026.
3. I read the verdicts for the people near the top, removed forecasts that were counted twice, and set the order.
4. For the 20 people, the same search ran over their comments and posts from 2025 and 2026. Each open forecast shown in quotes was checked against the source text.

The model judged most outcomes from memory. Treat a single verdict as likely, not certain, and follow the link before you rely on one.
