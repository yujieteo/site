Many people on Hacker News have been right once. This post looks for the people who have been right several times, and it counts what they got wrong as well.

It covers 3,048 forecasts made between 2007 and 2019 by 227 frequent or notable forecasters. 277 came true, 49 partly came true, 237 failed and 147 cannot be checked yet. The other 2,338 turned out not to be real forecasts.

The open forecasts are also on [one page](/forecasts/), where an AI agent adds new ones and marks outcomes.

There is a [ranking of LessWrong writers](/essays/lw-forecasters/) made the same way.

Names appear only where a person gives one in their own Hacker News profile. Nobody was traced beyond what they published.

## The ranking

The order is a judgment. It weighs how many calls came true, how often the person missed, and how hard the calls were. A bet that a hyped product will flop counts for less than a dated call that others doubted. A count of 3 right and 0 wrong is a small sample, so it can rank below a longer record with a few misses.

## How this was made and what it misses

- The data is the [open-index/hacker-news dataset ↗](https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-index/hacker-news). Forecasts from 2007 to 2019 have at least seven years of hindsight. Recent comments run through August 2026.
- Only comments with a personal commitment were kept, using phrases such as "I predict" or "I'd bet". Forecasts worded any other way are missing, so these records undercount everyone.
- The pool holds 227 of 8,135 people with such a comment. It includes people with at least eight such comments, plus people who stood out in an earlier pass.
- Claude Sonnet scored each forecast with the author's name hidden. Claude Opus then read the top records and set the final order. No formula decided the ranking.
- Outcomes came from the model's own knowledge. A few headline facts were checked against sources, but most were not, so some verdicts will be wrong.
- Repeats of one forecast were counted once, and quoted forecasts were removed. Comments may have been deleted or edited since.
- Backgrounds come from each person's profile or comments. "Not stated" means the profile does not say.
- Open forecasts are selected quotes. They have not been tested, and a few are casual remarks.
