## Before you start

These 129 pages borrow from operator algebras, modular curves, statistical mechanics and Arakelov geometry at once. Few readers know all four, so you may feel like an outsider in every chapter. Let that be. Marcolli says she aims at the general picture and leaves many proofs to the references. If you want to see class field theory appear as a phase transition, Chapter 3 is the place. Read the chapter that pleases you. The rest can wait.

## The story

Number theory is full of quotients that are bad spaces. Take the real projective line modulo SL<sub>2</sub>(ℤ), or lattices modulo commensurability. Orbits are dense, so the only continuous invariant functions are constants. Connes' idea is to keep functions on the graph of the equivalence relation and multiply them by convolution, like matrices. The algebra is noncommutative, and it remembers the quotient. Marcolli runs this idea through three cases. The boundary of a modular curve becomes a space of noncommutative tori and still knows modular symbols. Lattices with labelled torsion points give a quantum system whose partition function is ζ(β), and whose zero-temperature states are permuted by a Galois group. The fibre of an arithmetic surface over an Archimedean prime gets a Dirac operator whose zeta function gives the local L-factor.

## Main definitions

**Noncommutative space.** A C\*-algebra, thought of as the algebra of functions on a space. For a quotient X = Y/∼ one uses functions on the graph of ∼ with the convolution product. Two such algebras describe the same space when they are Morita equivalent.
- Example: the irrational rotation algebra A<sub>θ</sub>, generated by unitaries U and V with UV = e<sup>2πiθ</sup>VU, stands for the circle modulo rotation by θ.

**Spectral triple (Definition 3.1 of Chapter 1).** A triple (A, H, D): a C\*-algebra A acting on a Hilbert space H, and a self-adjoint operator D on H with compact resolvent, such that [D, a] is bounded for a in a dense subalgebra. It plays the role of a Riemannian metric. Its zeta function is Tr(|D|<sup>−z</sup>).

**ℚ-lattice.** A pair (Λ, φ) of a lattice Λ ⊂ ℝ<sup>n</sup> and a homomorphism φ : ℚ<sup>n</sup>/ℤ<sup>n</sup> → ℚΛ/Λ that labels torsion points. It is invertible if φ is an isomorphism. Two ℚ-lattices are commensurable if ℚΛ<sub>1</sub> = ℚΛ<sub>2</sub> and φ<sub>1</sub> = φ<sub>2</sub> modulo Λ<sub>1</sub> + Λ<sub>2</sub>.

**KMS state.** Given an algebra A with a time evolution σ<sub>t</sub>, a state φ is KMS at inverse temperature β if φ(a σ<sub>t</sub>(b)) continues analytically to the strip 0 &lt; Im z &lt; β and equals φ(σ<sub>t</sub>(b) a) on the far edge. These are the equilibrium states.
- KMS<sub>∞</sub> states are weak limits of KMS<sub>β</sub> states as β → ∞.

## Main results

Theorem numbers restart in each chapter.

**Theorem (Bost–Connes; quoted in Chapter 3, Section 2.1).** The algebra of 1-dimensional ℚ-lattices up to scale and commensurability is C\*(ℚ/ℤ) ⋊ ℕ<sup>×</sup>, and its partition function is ζ(β). For β ≤ 1 there is a unique KMS<sub>β</sub> state. For 1 &lt; β ≤ ∞ the extremal KMS<sub>β</sub> states form a copy of Ẑ<sup>\*</sup>, which acts freely and transitively.
- At β = ∞ the states take values in ℚ<sup>cycl</sup> on a rational subalgebra.
- The class field theory isomorphism Gal(ℚ<sup>cycl</sup>/ℚ) ≅ Ẑ<sup>\*</sup> turns the Galois action on these values into the symmetry action.

**Theorem (GL<sub>2</sub> system, Connes–Marcolli; Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 of Chapter 3).** For 2-dimensional ℚ-lattices there are no KMS states for β ≤ 1. For β > 2 the extremal KMS states are the points of the Shimura variety GL<sub>2</sub>(ℚ)\GL<sub>2</sub>(𝔸)/ℂ<sup>\*</sup>, that is, the invertible ℚ-lattices. For a generic invertible lattice (ρ, τ), the zero-temperature state sends an arithmetic subalgebra into the modular field evaluated at τ, and Galois acts through ℚ<sup>\*</sup>\GL<sub>2</sub>(𝔸<sub>f</sub>).

**Theorem (limiting modular symbols, Manin–Marcolli; Theorem 4.2 of Chapter 2).** Follow a geodesic in the upper half plane towards an irrational point β and divide its homology class on the modular curve by the length travelled. For β in a level set of the Lyapunov exponent of the continued fraction map, with value c, the limit equals the limit of (1/cn) Σ φ(T<sup>k</sup>(t<sub>0</sub>)), an average of ordinary modular symbols along the continued fraction shift T.

**Theorem (Archimedean L-factor, Consani–Marcolli; Theorem 7.1 of Chapter 4).** For an arithmetic surface whose complex fibre has genus at least 2, the local factor L(H<sup>1</sup>(X), s) at an Archimedean prime is a zeta-regularised determinant. It is computed from a zeta function of a spectral triple built from a Schottky uniformisation of the fibre. The exact normalisation is left out here.

## Note, then ignore

- Proofs. Many are left out on purpose, with pointers to the original papers. Safe to skip the hunt on a first reading.
- KMS<sub>∞</sub> versus ground states. Marcolli's definition by weak limits is stronger than the usual one. It matters only for making the Galois statement exact. Safe to skip.
- The range 1 &lt; β ≤ 2 for GL<sub>2</sub>. The text gives evidence for a single KMS state there, and states no theorem. Safe to skip.
- Dixmier trace. Its definition needs a choice of generalised limit, which drops out for measurable operators. Safe to skip.
- Not quite a spectral triple. In the last section the algebra is an enveloping algebra and not a C\*-algebra, so one axiom fails (Theorem 8.2 of Chapter 4). Safe to skip.
