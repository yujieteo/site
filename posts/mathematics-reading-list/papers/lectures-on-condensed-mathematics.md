## The story

How do you do algebra when groups and modules carry a topology? Topological abelian groups do not form an abelian category. The identity map from ℝ with the discrete topology to ℝ with the usual topology is a bijection and not an isomorphism, yet it has no kernel or cokernel to explain the gap. The idea, due to Clausen and Scholze, is to record a space T by the sets of continuous maps S → T for all profinite sets S. Such a record is a sheaf on profinite sets, a condensed set. Now the map above has a non-zero cokernel Q: its value on a point is 0, but its value on S is continuous functions modulo locally constant ones. Completion is then handled by a second idea, solid modules, which gives a well-behaved completed tensor product.

## Main definitions

**Condensed set (Definition 1.2).** A sheaf of sets on the category of profinite sets, where covers are finite families of jointly surjective maps. Concretely, a functor T from profinite sets to sets, contravariant, with T(∅) a point, that turns disjoint unions into products and satisfies gluing along surjections. Condensed groups and rings are defined in the same way.
- Example 1.3: a topological space T gives the condensed set S ↦ {continuous maps S → T}.

**Extremally disconnected set (Definition 2.4).** A compact Hausdorff space S such that every surjection onto S from a compact Hausdorff space splits. The Stone–Čech compactification of a discrete set is one. These supply the projective generators.

**Solid abelian group (Definition 5.1).** For a profinite set S = lim S<sub>i</sub> put ℤ[S]<sup>□</sup> = lim ℤ[S<sub>i</sub>]. A condensed abelian group A is solid if every map S → A from a profinite set extends uniquely to a map ℤ[S]<sup>□</sup> → A.

## Main results

**Theorem (topological spaces embed; Proposition 1.7).** Fix an uncountable strong limit cardinal κ. The functor from topological spaces to κ-condensed sets is faithful, and fully faithful on κ-compactly generated spaces. It has a left adjoint T ↦ T(point) with a quotient topology.

**Theorem (a good abelian category; Theorems 1.10 and 2.2).** Condensed abelian groups form an abelian category with all limits and colimits, in which products, direct sums and filtered colimits are exact (Grothendieck's axioms AB3 to AB6, AB3\* and AB4\*). It is generated by compact projective objects.
- Step 1: condensed sets are also sheaves on extremally disconnected sets, where the sheaf condition reduces to turning finite disjoint unions into products.
- Step 2: so limits and colimits are computed pointwise there, and the free objects ℤ[S] on extremally disconnected S are compact and projective.

**Theorem (cohomology agrees; Theorems 3.2 and 3.3).** For a compact Hausdorff space S, condensed cohomology of S with ℤ coefficients agrees with sheaf cohomology. With ℝ coefficients, H<sup>0</sup> is the continuous real functions on S and H<sup>i</sup> = 0 for i > 0.

**Theorem (solid groups; Theorem 5.8).** Solid abelian groups form an abelian subcategory of condensed abelian groups, closed under limits, colimits and extensions. The products of copies of ℤ are compact projective generators. The inclusion has a left adjoint, solidification, which extends ℤ[S] ↦ ℤ[S]<sup>□</sup>. The derived category of solid groups embeds fully faithfully, and a complex is solid exactly when its cohomology groups are.
- Step 1: C(S, ℤ) is a free abelian group for profinite S (Theorem 5.4), so ℤ[S]<sup>□</sup> is a product of copies of ℤ.
- Step 2: a formal lemma (Lemma 5.9) reduces everything to one Ext computation between such products.

**Theorem (coherent duality; Theorem 11.1).** Let f: X → Spec R be separated, smooth, of finite type and of dimension d. There is a functor f<sub>!</sub> on solid derived categories that agrees with derived global sections when f is proper, and a trace map f<sub>!</sub> ω[d] → R that induces duality. No properness is needed.

## Note, then ignore

- Cardinal bounds. Profinite sets form a large category, so the notes fix a cut-off κ and later take a colimit over κ. This is set-theoretic care. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Non-T1 spaces. Warning 2.14 says a space such as the Sierpinski space does not give a condensed set once the bounds are removed. Safe to skip.
- Light condensed sets. A footnote mentions the choice κ = ℵ<sub>1</sub>, used in later work on analytic stacks. These notes do not use it. Safe to skip.
- Pyknotic sets. Barwick and Haine set up a closely related theory with universes. The difference is foundational. Safe to skip.
- ℝ is not solid. The derived solidification of ℝ is 0 (Corollary 6.1), so the solid theory suits non-archimedean situations. The notes say so and move on. Safe to skip.
