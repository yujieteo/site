basis: the introduction and the main theorem statements

## Before you start

This book is about 700 pages of charts, tables and differentials. The likely feeling is overwhelm, plus a fear that spectral sequences are a craft you never learned. That feeling is reasonable, and you can have it and still look. If you are curious how modular forms show up in the stable homotopy groups of spheres, the introduction tells the whole plot. Look at the charts as pictures first. Nobody reads this book cover to cover.

## The story

The spectrum tmf of topological modular forms sits between the sphere and the ring of integral modular forms ℤ[c<sub>4</sub>, c<sub>6</sub>, Δ]/(c<sub>4</sub><sup>3</sup> − c<sub>6</sub><sup>2</sup> = 1728Δ). Away from the primes 2 and 3 its homotopy is just that ring. At 2 and 3 it carries a rich pattern of torsion that detects much of the known torsion in the stable stems.

Hopkins, Mahowald, Bauer and Henriques had computed most of this with a descent spectral sequence, but many details lived as folklore. Bruner and Rognes redo it with the classical Adams spectral sequence. The key input is that the mod 2 cohomology of tmf is A//A(2), so the starting page is Ext over the finite algebra A(2), which a computer can resolve. They then find every differential and extension, with proofs, and get the ring structure in all degrees.

## Main definitions

**tmf.** The connective cover of Tmf, which is the global sections of a sheaf of E<sub>∞</sub> ring spectra on the compactified moduli stack of elliptic curves. Inverting Δ gives the periodic spectrum TMF. There is an edge homomorphism e from π<sub>\*</sub>(tmf) to the ring of integral modular forms above.

**A(2).** The subalgebra of the mod 2 Steenrod algebra A generated by Sq<sup>1</sup>, Sq<sup>2</sup> and Sq<sup>4</sup>. It has dimension 64 over 𝔽<sub>2</sub>. By a theorem of Mathew, H<sup>\*</sup>(tmf; 𝔽<sub>2</sub>) ≅ A//A(2) = A ⊗<sub>A(2)</sub> 𝔽<sub>2</sub>.

**Adams spectral sequence.** For a bounded below spectrum X of finite type mod p, a spectral sequence with E<sub>2</sub><sup>s,t</sup> = Ext<sub>A</sub><sup>s,t</sup>(H<sup>\*</sup>(X), 𝔽<sub>p</sub>) that converges strongly to π<sub>t−s</sub> of the p-completion of X.
- For tmf at p = 2: a change of algebra gives E<sub>2</sub>(tmf) ≅ Ext<sub>A(2)</sub>(𝔽<sub>2</sub>, 𝔽<sub>2</sub>).

**Bott and Mahowald elements.** Classes B in π<sub>8</sub>(tmf) and M in π<sub>192</sub>(tmf), taken after completion at 2. M is detected by Δ<sup>8</sup>, and the 2-local homotopy of TMF repeats with period 192.

## Main results

**Theorem (the E<sub>2</sub>-term; Shimada–Iwai, reproved as Theorem 3.46).** Ext<sub>A(2)</sub>(𝔽<sub>2</sub>, 𝔽<sub>2</sub>) is generated as an algebra by 13 classes, h<sub>0</sub>, h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>2</sub>, c<sub>0</sub>, α, β, d<sub>0</sub>, e<sub>0</sub>, γ, δ, g, w<sub>1</sub>, w<sub>2</sub>, subject to 54 relations. It is free as a module over 𝔽<sub>2</sub>[w<sub>1</sub>, w<sub>2</sub>].
- Step 1: a spectral sequence of Davis and Mahowald computes Ext over A(2) from Ext over A(1).
- Step 2: a Gröbner basis and a counting argument show the presentation is complete.

**Theorem (collapse; Theorem 5.27).** In the mod 2 Adams spectral sequence for tmf, E<sub>5</sub> = E<sub>∞</sub>. So only the differentials d<sub>2</sub>, d<sub>3</sub> and d<sub>4</sub> are nonzero.
- Step 1: the E<sub>∞</sub> ring structure of tmf gives Steenrod operations on E<sub>2</sub>, and these force many differentials.
- Step 2: a few known facts about the sphere in low degrees supply the rest.

**Theorem (homotopy of tmf at 2; Theorem 9.27).** After completion at 2, π<sub>\*</sub>(tmf) is a split extension of ℤ[B, M]-modules. The submodule is the B-power torsion, listed in a table for degrees 0 to 191 and then repeated by powers of M. The quotient is a sum of eight pieces, each tensored with ℤ[M]. I did not read the precise description of those pieces, called ko[k] in the book. The introduction says the product structure is in Theorem 9.54.

**Claim (applications; from the introduction).** The book also computes the full additive and multiplicative structure of the stable stems π<sub>n</sub>(S) for n ≤ 44, treats duality, and gives the corresponding results at the prime 3. At primes p ≥ 5 the edge homomorphism e is an isomorphism.

## Note, then ignore

- Two unresolved coefficients. One sign in a product in π<sub>246</sub>(tmf) at 2, and one coefficient in π<sub>112</sub>(tmf) at 3, are left open. They do not affect the rest. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- tmf, Tmf and TMF. Three spectra with the same letters: connective, compactified and periodic. The book is about the first. Safe to skip the difference at first.
- The prime 3 uses another tool. There the authors use an Adams spectral sequence built inside tmf-modules, because it is more convenient. Safe to skip.
- Implicit completion. Statements at 2 are about the 2-completed groups, even when the notation does not say so. Safe to skip.
- Clashing names. The Ext classes c<sub>0</sub> and c<sub>i</sub> are unrelated to the modular forms c<sub>4</sub> and c<sub>6</sub>. The book warns about this itself. Safe to skip.
