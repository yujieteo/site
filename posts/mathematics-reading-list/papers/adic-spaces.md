## Before you start

These are 188 pages of course notes, and the first 90 are valuation theory and topological rings before a single adic space appears. The likely feeling is impatience shading into boredom: you came for perfectoid spaces and got totally ordered groups. That reaction is honest, and nothing is wrong with it. The notes do give you a complete route from the definition of a valuation to almost purity, with proofs. You do not have to walk the whole route. Pick the one stop that interests you and enjoy it.

## The story

Tate's rigid geometry made p-adic analytic spaces work, but with a Grothendieck topology in place of a real topological space, and with no room for formal schemes or non-Noetherian rings. Huber's fix is to take more points. A point of Spec is a prime ideal. A point of Huber's space is a valuation, of any rank, that is continuous and bounded by 1 on a chosen subring. The result is an honest spectral space with a structure presheaf. The price is that the presheaf is not always a sheaf, so one needs a theorem saying when it is. Morel's notes build all this from scratch. Their final chapter spends the investment. Perfectoid rings satisfy the sheaf condition, and tilting is a homeomorphism from a characteristic 0 space to a characteristic ℓ one that preserves finite étale covers.

## Main definitions

**Valuation spectrum.** A valuation on a ring A is a multiplicative map |.|: A → Γ ∪ {0} with |0| = 0, |1| = 1 and |x + y| ≤ max(|x|, |y|), where Γ is a totally ordered abelian group. Spv(A) is the set of equivalence classes of valuations, with topology generated by the sets where |f<sub>1</sub>|, …, |f<sub>n</sub>| ≤ |g| ≠ 0.

**Huber pair.** A Huber ring (or f-adic ring) is a topological ring A with an open subring A<sub>0</sub> whose topology is I-adic for a finitely generated ideal I of A<sub>0</sub>. It is Tate if it also has a topologically nilpotent unit. A Huber pair (A, A<sup>+</sup>) adds an open, integrally closed subring A<sup>+</sup> contained in the power-bounded elements A<sup>0</sup>.
- Example: an affinoid algebra over a complete non-Archimedean field is a Tate ring.

**Adic spectrum.** Spa(A, A<sup>+</sup>) is the set of continuous valuations x on A with |a|<sub>x</sub> ≤ 1 for all a ∈ A<sup>+</sup>. A rational subset is {x : |f<sub>i</sub>|<sub>x</sub> ≤ |g|<sub>x</sub> ≠ 0 for all i}, where f<sub>1</sub>, …, f<sub>n</sub> generate an open ideal.
- Adic space: a space with a sheaf of topological rings and a valuation at each point, locally isomorphic to some Spa(A, A<sup>+</sup>) whose structure presheaf is a sheaf.

**Perfectoid Tate ring.** A complete uniform Tate ring A with a pseudo-uniformiser ϖ such that ϖ<sup>ℓ</sup> divides ℓ in A<sup>0</sup> and Frobenius A<sup>0</sup>/ϖ → A<sup>0</sup>/ϖ<sup>ℓ</sup> is bijective. Its tilt A<sup>♭</sup> is a perfectoid Tate ring of characteristic ℓ.

## Main results

**Theorem (spectrality; Theorem I.2.6.1 and Corollary III.2.4).** For every commutative ring A, Spv(A) is a spectral space. For every Huber pair, Spa(A, A<sup>+</sup>) is a spectral space, and the rational subsets are open, quasi-compact and form a base of the topology.
- Step 1: the continuous valuations form a spectral subspace of Spv(A) (Corollary II.2.2.3).
- Step 2: Spa(A, A<sup>+</sup>) is a pro-constructible subset of it.

**Theorem (when the presheaf is a sheaf; Theorem IV.1.1.5).** Let X = Spa(A, A<sup>+</sup>). Suppose that the completion of A is discrete, or that it has a Noetherian ring of definition, or that A is a strongly Noetherian Tate ring, or that (A, A<sup>+</sup>) is Tate and stably uniform. Then O<sub>X</sub> is a sheaf, and H<sup>i</sup>(U, O<sub>X</sub>) = 0 for every rational domain U and every i ≥ 1.
- Cases: the second covers Noetherian formal schemes and the third covers rigid analytic varieties.
- Case: the fourth covers perfectoid algebras. The notes prove all but the second.

**Theorem (tilting and Spa; Theorem V.1.6.1).** For a perfectoid Huber pair (A, A<sup>+</sup>), the map X = Spa(A, A<sup>+</sup>) → X<sup>♭</sup> = Spa(A<sup>♭</sup>, A<sup>♭+</sup>) is a homeomorphism that matches rational domains. For each rational domain U, the pair (O<sub>X</sub>(U), O<sub>X</sub><sup>+</sup>(U)) is perfectoid, and its tilt is the corresponding pair on X<sup>♭</sup>. So O<sub>X</sub> is a sheaf (Corollary V.1.6.2).

**Theorem (almost purity; Theorem V.3.1.3).** Let (A, A<sup>+</sup>) be a perfectoid Huber pair and X = Spa(A, A<sup>+</sup>). Finite étale maps of adic spaces Y → X are equivalent to finite étale A-algebras. Each such algebra is perfectoid. Tilting gives an equivalence between finite étale Huber pairs over (A, A<sup>+</sup>) and over (A<sup>♭</sup>, A<sup>♭+</sup>).
- Step 1: prove it in characteristic ℓ, where Frobenius does the work.
- Step 2: deform almost finite étale algebras, and treat perfectoid fields separately.

## Note, then ignore

- The prime is ℓ. Morel writes ℓ for the residue characteristic where most sources write p. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Additive or multiplicative. Some algebra references use additive valuations. The notes fix multiplicative ones, so "small" means close to 0. Safe to skip.
- Higher rank points. Valuations with value group bigger than ℝ are what make the space spectral. The closed unit ball example (III.5.2) shows them. Safe to skip.
- Why A<sup>+</sup>. Different choices of A<sup>+</sup> give different spaces for the same A. The largest choice is A<sup>0</sup>. Safe to skip.
- Not always a sheaf. The notes state that the structure presheaf can fail to be a sheaf, which is why Chapter IV exists. Safe to skip.
