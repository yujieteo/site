## Before you start

At 53 pages, with five earlier papers in the same series behind it, this one can raise a fear of arriving late: everyone else seems to know the notation already. That fear makes sense, and you can keep it and still read. The introduction is self-contained and states both main theorems in words. If you like how affine Kac–Moody algebras come from loops in a simple Lie algebra, here you see the next step up. Read for the pattern. The tables are for looking up.

## The story

Kac showed that every affine Lie algebra, after taking the derived algebra and dividing by the centre, is a loop algebra of a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra twisted by a diagram automorphism. Simple algebras have "nullity 0" and affine ones "nullity 1". People wanted nullity 2 and beyond. Three constructions compete: loop in several variables at once (multiloop), loop again and again (iterated loop), or write axioms (extended affine Lie algebras). In nullity 1 all three agree. In nullity 2 they do not. The authors show that the multiloop class M<sub>2</sub> is the well-behaved core. It consists of loop algebras of untwisted affine algebras by diagram automorphisms. That reduces classification to affine Dynkin diagrams and their symmetries, and gives a list of 14 infinite families and 9 exceptional algebras.

## Main definitions

Throughout, k is an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

**Multiloop algebra, and the class M<sub>n</sub>.** Let σ = (σ<sub>1</sub>, …, σ<sub>n</sub>) be commuting automorphisms of g with σ<sub>i</sub> of period m<sub>i</sub>. The multiloop algebra L(g, σ) is the subalgebra of g ⊗ k[z<sub>1</sub><sup>±1</sup>, …, z<sub>n</sub><sup>±1</sup>] spanned by all u ⊗ z<sup>k</sup> where σ<sub>j</sub> acts on u by the k<sub>j</sub>-th power of a fixed primitive m<sub>j</sub>-th root of unity. M<sub>n</sub> is the class of such algebras with g finite dimensional and simple.
- n = 1: this is the usual twisted loop algebra.

**Iterated loop algebras, I<sub>n</sub>.** I<sub>0</sub> is the class of finite dimensional simple Lie algebras. I<sub>n</sub> is the class of loop algebras L(g, σ) with g ∈ I<sub>n−1</sub> and σ of finite order. Always M<sub>n</sub> ⊂ I<sub>n</sub>.

**Extended affine Lie algebras, E<sub>n</sub>.** An EALA is a Lie algebra with an invariant form and a Cartan-like subalgebra satisfying axioms (EA1) to (EA6), modelled on simple and affine algebras. Its nullity is the rank of the group generated by its isotropic roots. E<sub>n</sub> is the class of algebras "core modulo centre" of EALAs of nullity n.

**Relative and absolute type.** An algebra L in M<sub>2</sub> becomes finite dimensional and simple over the fraction field F of its centroid. The absolute type is its root system type over the algebraic closure of F. The relative type is the type seen by a maximal diagonalisable subalgebra over F itself. L is anisotropic when that subalgebra is 0.

## Main results

Write g′ for the derived algebra of an affine algebra g, taken modulo its centre.

**Theorem (characterising M<sub>2</sub>; Theorem 10.1.1).** For a Lie algebra L these are equivalent: (a) L ∈ M<sub>2</sub>. (b) L ≅ L(g′, σ) with g untwisted affine and σ a diagram automorphism. (c) L ≅ L(g′, σ) with g affine and σ a finite order automorphism of first kind. (d) L ∈ I<sub>2</sub> and the centroid of L is isomorphic to k[t<sub>1</sub><sup>±1</sup>, t<sub>2</sub><sup>±1</sup>].
- Step 1: an "erasing" theorem (Theorem 6.2.2) lets you replace any finite order automorphism by an outer one without changing the loop algebra.
- Step 2: the kind of σ is read off from the centroid of L(g′, σ).

**Theorem (how the classes sit; Corollaries 10.1.3, 10.1.4 and 10.1.6).** M<sub>2</sub> ∩ E<sub>2</sub> = I<sub>2</sub> ∩ E<sub>2</sub>, and this is the set of isotropic algebras in M<sub>2</sub>.
- Case 1: σ not transitive on the affine diagram. Then L is isotropic and lies in E<sub>2</sub>.
- Case 2: σ transitive. Then L is anisotropic, lies outside E<sub>2</sub>, and is sl<sub>1</sub> of a quantum torus at a root of unity θ ≠ 1.

**Theorem (classification; Theorem 13.3.1).** Take an untwisted affine matrix A and a diagram automorphism σ from the paper's Table 2. Then L(g(A)′, σ) lies in M<sub>2</sub>, and every algebra in M<sub>2</sub> is isomorphic to exactly one of these. The introduction counts 14 infinite families and 9 exceptional algebras.

**Corollary (invariants suffice; Corollary 13.3.3).** Two algebras in M<sub>2</sub> are isomorphic if and only if they share absolute and relative type, except in one infinite family where both types are of type A.

## Note, then ignore

- Derived algebra modulo centre. The theorems concern g′ and not g, because Kac's realisation theorem describes g′ modulo its centre as a loop algebra. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Zero counts as a root. The paper includes 0 in every root system, which shifts some statements slightly. Safe to skip.
- First and second kind. This splits finite order automorphisms of g′ into two sorts. You only need that first kind is the good one. Safe to skip.
- Two labelling systems. The paper uses Kac's labels for affine types and notes that Moody–Pianzola labels differ. Safe to skip.
- Graded or ungraded. Isomorphism here means ungraded isomorphism, even though the algebras carry gradings. Safe to skip.
