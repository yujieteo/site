## Before you start

This article asks for two languages at once: knot groups and coverings on one side, Galois groups and étale cohomology on the other. You may feel that you know neither well enough. That worry is allowed, and it need not be solved today. The article works as a dictionary, and one half of each entry explains the other. If you would like quadratic reciprocity to look like a picture of two linked circles, Section 2 gives that. Read for the pleasure of the matches. Skip any entry freely.

## The story

Number theorists long compared number fields to function fields of curves. That comparison misses something: a number ring has no constant field, and its étale cohomology has dimension 3, with a Poincaré-type duality (Artin–Verdier). Mazur noticed in the 1960s that Spec of a number ring then behaves like a 3-manifold. A finite field is homotopically a circle, since its fundamental group is the profinite completion of ℤ, generated by Frobenius. So a prime is an embedded circle: a knot. Morishita tests this at the level of groups. The Galois group of the maximal extension unramified outside a set S of primes plays the role of a link group. Their presentations match, linking numbers match power residue symbols, and Alexander polynomials match Iwasawa polynomials. The primes 13, 61, 937 even behave like Borromean rings mod 2.

## Main definitions

**The basic dictionary.** A 3-manifold corresponds to Spec(O<sub>k</sub>) for a number field k. A knot S<sup>1</sup> → M corresponds to a prime Spec(𝔽<sub>p</sub>) → Spec(O<sub>k</sub>). The 3-sphere corresponds to Spec(ℤ) with the infinite prime added.
- Locally: a tubular neighbourhood corresponds to Spec of the p-adic integers, and its boundary torus to Spec of the p-adic field.
- Meridian and longitude correspond to a generator τ of tame inertia and a Frobenius lift σ.

**Link group and its analogue.** For a link L in S<sup>3</sup>, G<sub>L</sub> = π<sub>1</sub>(S<sup>3</sup> ∖ L). For a finite set S of primes, G<sub>S</sub> = π<sub>1</sub>(Spec(ℤ) ∖ S), the Galois group of the maximal extension of ℚ unramified outside S and ∞. One works with its maximal pro-l quotient G<sub>S</sub>(l).

**Mod 2 linking number of primes.** For primes p, q ≡ 1 mod 4, lk<sub>2</sub>(p, q) is the image of Frobenius at q in the Galois group ℤ/2 of the double cover of Spec(ℤ[1/p]) given by ℚ(√p).

## Main results

**Claim (Legendre symbol as linking; formulas (2.1), (2.2)).** For primes p, q ≡ 1 mod 4, (−1)<sup>lk<sub>2</sub>(p,q)</sup> equals the Legendre symbol (p/q). Quadratic reciprocity for such primes is the symmetry lk(K, L) = lk(L, K).
- Reason: Frobenius at q acts trivially on √p exactly when p is a square mod q.

**Theorem (Milnor and Koch presentations; Theorems 3.1, 3.2, 3.4).** After pro-l completion, a link group of n components in a homology 3-sphere has generators x<sub>1</sub>, …, x<sub>n</sub> and relations [x<sub>i</sub>, y<sub>i</sub>] = 1, with x<sub>i</sub> a meridian and y<sub>i</sub> a longitude. For primes p<sub>1</sub>, …, p<sub>n</sub> ≡ 1 mod l, the group G<sub>S</sub>(l) has generators x<sub>1</sub>, …, x<sub>n</sub> and relations x<sub>i</sub><sup>p<sub>i</sub>−1</sup>[x<sub>i</sub>, y<sub>i</sub>] = 1, with x<sub>i</sub> a monodromy and y<sub>i</sub> a Frobenius lift.
- Both sets of relations come from the local groups: the torus group on one side, the tame local Galois group on the other.
- Modulo commutators, the longitude is a product of meridians with linking numbers as exponents. For primes the exponents are l-th power residue symbols.

**Theorem (arithmetic Milnor invariants; Theorem 4.5, Example 4.7).** Expanding the Frobenius words y<sub>i</sub> by a pro-l Magnus expansion defines invariants μ<sub>m</sub>(I) of G<sub>S</sub>(l), for multi-indices I up to a bounded length. For length 2 they give power residue symbols. For l = 2 and three primes ≡ 1 mod 4, the Rédei triple symbol equals (−1)<sup>μ<sub>2</sub>(123)</sup>.
- Example: for S = {13, 61, 937} all pairwise mod 2 linking numbers vanish and μ<sub>2</sub>(ijk) = 1, as for the Borromean rings.

**Claim (Alexander and Iwasawa; Section 7, formulas (7.1), (7.2)).** The Alexander polynomial is the characteristic polynomial of the meridian acting on H<sub>1</sub> of the infinite cyclic cover. The Iwasawa polynomial is the characteristic polynomial of γ − 1 acting on the inverse limit of p-class groups in a ℤ<sub>p</sub>-extension, up to a power of p. Both control growth in the tower of finite covers.

## Note, then ignore

- Congruence conditions. p ≡ 1 mod 4, or p<sub>i</sub> ≡ 1 mod l, ensures the needed covers ramify only at the chosen primes. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Asymmetry for l > 2. The mod l linking number of primes is not symmetric, so some link theorems have no direct analogue. Safe to skip.
- The infinite prime. It is treated as the end of a non-compact 3-manifold. This affects cohomology with compact supports only. Safe to skip.
- Tame quotient. The local analogy with the torus group uses the maximal tame quotient of the local Galois group. Safe to skip.
- An analogy, not a functor. Nothing here turns a knot into a prime. The theorems on each side are proved separately. Safe to skip.
