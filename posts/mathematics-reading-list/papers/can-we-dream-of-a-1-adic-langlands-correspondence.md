## Before you start

The title sounds like a joke, and section 1 drops you into Breuil-Mézard, Kisin varieties and Serre weights with little warm-up. The likely feeling is doubt: is this serious, and can I tell? You can hold that doubt without settling it. The authors themselves call it a dream. If you like seeing a research idea before it is a theory, here are 17 light pages of that. Start with the appendix if you like. It needs only sets and monoids.

## The story

The p-adic Langlands programme links Galois representations of a p-adic field to representations of groups like GL<sub>n</sub>. A prime p is fixed at the start. Yet in computations p keeps dropping out. Lists of Serre weights are indexed by strings of 0s and 1s. Kisin varieties sit in products of projective lines, with equations whose coefficients are 0 or 1. Deformation rings look like ℤ<sub>p</sub>[[X, Y]]/(XY − p) for every p. The authors propose one reason: a "1-adic" correspondence over 𝔽<sub>1</sub>, the field with one element, of which each p-adic correspondence is a base change. Tits's observation is the model: the order of GL<sub>n</sub>(𝔽<sub>q</sub>), divided by (q − 1)<sup>n</sup>, tends to n! as q → 1, the order of the Weyl group. Nothing is proved. The paper collects clues.

## Main definitions

**Serre weight.** An irreducible representation of GL<sub>n</sub>(k) over the algebraic closure of 𝔽<sub>p</sub>, where k is the residue field of the p-adic field K. The Breuil-Mézard conjecture counts these weights with multiplicities.

**Gene.** For K unramified of degree f over ℚ<sub>p</sub>, a 2-dimensional irreducible mod p Galois representation ρ and a reducible tame inertial type t, the gene is a sequence of period 2f with values in {A, B, AB, O}. It is read off from the base p digits of one integer built from ρ and t.

**𝔽<sub>1</sub>-vector space.** A set. Its dimension is its cardinality, and a linear map is a partially defined function. Direct sum is disjoint union and tensor product is cartesian product.
- Consequence: GL<sub>d</sub>(𝔽<sub>1</sub>) is the symmetric group S<sub>d</sub>.
- Algebras: an 𝔽<sub>1</sub>-algebra is a commutative monoid, so 𝔽<sub>1</sub>(X) is the monoid of powers X<sup>ℤ</sup>.

**𝔽<sub>1<sup>n</sup></sub>-vector space (Definition A.1).** Again a set, but a linear map V → W is now a list of n partially defined functions. This is built so that GL<sub>d</sub>(𝔽<sub>1<sup>n</sup></sub>) = (S<sub>d</sub>)<sup>n</sup>.

## Main results

**Conjecture (Breuil-Mézard; Conjecture 1).** There are integers μ<sub>ρ</sub>(σ), one for each mod p representation ρ of the Galois group of K and each Serre weight σ, such that the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of the special fibre of each potentially crystalline deformation ring of ρ with Hodge type λ and inertial type t equals Σ<sub>σ</sub> n<sub>λ,t</sub>(σ)·μ<sub>ρ</sub>(σ). Here n<sub>λ,t</sub>(σ) is a multiplicity on the GL<sub>n</sub> side. The paper recalls that Kisin proved this for n = 2 and K = ℚ<sub>p</sub>.

**Claim (the gene decides the weights; quoted from an earlier paper of the authors).** In the setting of the gene, with Hodge type (0, 0), each gene X has a set W(X) of sequences in {0, 1}<sup>f</sup>, and W(X) is in canonical bijection with the set of Serre weights common to t and ρ. So that set depends on ρ, t and p only through the gene.

**Conjecture (Conjecture 2).** In the same setting, the generic fibre of the deformation ring is determined by the Kisin variety with its shape stratification, and the ring itself is determined by the gene. The paper reports that this is known in most cases where the Kisin variety is a point, and in one example where it is a projective line.

**Proposition (cyclic Galois groups in characteristic 1; Propositions A.2 and A.4).** View 𝔽<sub>1<sup>n</sup></sub> over 𝔽<sub>1</sub> as the set ℤ/nℤ with the shift i ↦ i + 1 as Frobenius. Its automorphisms commuting with Frobenius form a cyclic group of order n generated by Frobenius. With a naive model of ℚ<sub>1</sub> as the monoid of powers of a uniformiser ϖ, adjoining an n-th root of ϖ over the degree n unramified extension gives a further cyclic group of order n.
- Step 1: a bijection f of ℤ/nℤ with f(x + 1) = f(x) + 1 is a translation.
- Step 2: for the root extension, a monoid automorphism fixing the base is fixed by where it sends the root.

## Note, then ignore

- p > 2 and K unramified. Section 1 fixes these so the formulas stay short. They are working assumptions, safe to skip on a first reading.
- Two versions of 𝔽<sub>1<sup>n</sup></sub>. The usual one in the literature is the monoid of n-th roots of unity, with automorphism group (ℤ/nℤ)<sup>×</sup> and no Frobenius. The appendix replaces it for that reason. Note the clash and move on.
- Two restrictions of scalars. The appendix has an additive one (disjoint union of n copies) and a multiplicative one (n-fold product). Both are adjoint to extension of scalars. You need only the additive one at first.
- The lost factor p − 1. The tame Galois group over ℚ<sub>p</sub> has order p<sup>n</sup> − 1, which is 0 at p = 1. The authors drop the factor p − 1 and keep 1 + p + … + p<sup>n−1</sup> → n. Remark A.5 admits this is not justified. Ignore it for now.
- Many models of 𝔽<sub>1</sub>-geometry. Deitmar, Toën-Vaquié and Connes-Consani give different definitions of schemes over 𝔽<sub>1</sub>. The note does not choose one, and you need not either.
