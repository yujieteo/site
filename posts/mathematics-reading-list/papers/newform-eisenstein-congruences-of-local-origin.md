## Before you start

This paper is 20 pages, but its main conjecture carries two characters, two levels, three primes and two numbered conditions in one sentence. The likely feeling is irritation at bookkeeping: too many letters before any idea. That reaction is fair, and you can let it sit there. Underneath is a clean question you may care about: why does 691 appear in Ramanujan's congruence, and what replaces it at higher level? Read for that one question.

## The story

Ramanujan saw that τ(n) ≡ σ<sub>11</sub>(n) mod 691. The reason is that the cusp form Δ and the Eisenstein series E<sub>12</sub> agree modulo 691, and 691 divides the constant term of E<sub>12</sub>. Varying weight, level and character gives new moduli. Some divide values of Dirichlet L-functions. Others divide only a single Euler factor at a prime p of the level. These are the congruences "of local origin". Known results produce some eigenform with the congruence, but it may be an old form lifted from lower level. The authors ask when a newform does the job. They conjecture an exact criterion in two conditions. They prove one direction always, and both directions when the extra level M is 1 or a prime. The obstacle for composite M is gluing: each prime p dividing M gives a form new at p, but nobody knows how to get one form new at all p at once.

## Main definitions

**New Eisenstein series.** Let ψ and φ be Dirichlet characters of conductors u and v, with uv = N square-free and χ = ψφ. For weight k > 2, the series E<sub>k</sub><sup>ψ,φ</sup> has n-th coefficient Σ<sub>d|n</sub> ψ(n/d) φ(d) d<sup>k−1</sup> and constant term involving L(1 − k, ψ<sup>−1</sup>φ). It is a Hecke eigenform of level N and character χ.
- Eigenvalues: T<sub>q</sub> acts by ψ(q) + φ(q)q<sup>k−1</sup>.

**Eisenstein congruence.** A normalised eigenform f of level NM and character χ (lifted to modulus NM) satisfies the congruence if a<sub>q</sub>(f) ≡ ψ(q) + φ(q)q<sup>k−1</sup> mod λ for all primes q not dividing NM. Here λ is a prime above l in the ring generated by the coefficients of f and the values of ψ and φ.

**The two conditions.** Let M be square-free and coprime to N, and let λ′ be a prime above l in ℤ[ψ, φ].
- Condition (1): λ′ divides L(1 − k, ψ<sup>−1</sup>φ) times the product over primes p dividing M of (ψ(p) − φ(p)p<sup>k</sup>).
- Condition (2): for each prime p dividing M, λ′ divides (ψ(p) − φ(p)p<sup>k</sup>)(ψ(p) − φ(p)p<sup>k−2</sup>).

**Local origin.** A congruence has local origin when its modulus divides an Euler factor ψ(p) − φ(p)p<sup>k</sup> at a prime p of M, and need not divide the complete L-value.

## Main results

**Conjecture (newform criterion; Conjecture 1.1, restated as 3.1).** Let N, M be coprime and square-free, k > 2, and l > k + 1 a prime not dividing φ(N)NM. A newform f of level NM and character χ with the Eisenstein congruence modulo some λ above l exists if and only if Conditions (1) and (2) hold for some λ′ below λ.

**Theorem (newform implies conditions; Theorem 3.5).** With k > 2, l > k + 1 and l not dividing NM, if a newform of level NM satisfies the congruence, then Conditions (1) and (2) hold.
- Step 1: the congruence makes the mod λ Galois representation of f reducible, with pieces ψ and φ times the (k−1)-st power of the cyclotomic character.
- Step 2: at each p dividing M, a newform has a known local shape. Comparing the two descriptions at Frobenius forces one of the two factors in Condition (2) to vanish mod λ.

**Theorem (an eigenform exists; Theorem 3.6).** Let k > 2 and let λ′ not divide 6NM. If Condition (1) holds, then some normalised eigenform of level NM and character χ satisfies the congruence. It may be old.
- Step 1: build an Eisenstein series of level NM whose constant term at every cusp is divisible by λ′, so that its reduction is a cusp form mod λ′.
- Step 2: lift that reduction to characteristic zero and apply the Deligne–Serre lemma to get an eigenform.

**Theorem (prime M; Theorem 3.7).** If M = p is prime, the conjecture is true. The case M = 1 also holds, since there the eigenform from Theorem 3.6 is automatically new.
- Step 1: if the eigenform from Theorem 3.6 is old, it comes from a newform f<sub>1</sub> of level N.
- Step 2: Condition (2) gives a<sub>p</sub>(f<sub>1</sub>)<sup>2</sup> ≡ χ(p)p<sup>k−2</sup>(1 + p)<sup>2</sup>, which is the hypothesis of Diamond's level-raising theorem. That theorem supplies a congruent form new at p.

## Note, then ignore

- The bound l > k + 1. It keeps the Galois representation well behaved at l (crystalline, in the Bloch–Kato section). Safe to skip on a first reading.
- λ against λ′. One prime lives in ℤ[ψ, φ], the other in the larger ring containing the coefficients of f. The second lies above the first. Safe to skip.
- Section 4 on Bloch–Kato. It reads the two conditions as statements about Selmer groups, as motivation and not as part of the proofs above. Safe to skip.
- Weight 2. Section 3.5 explains that the conjecture fails in general for k = 2, with Mazur's theorem at prime level as the warning case. Safe to skip.
