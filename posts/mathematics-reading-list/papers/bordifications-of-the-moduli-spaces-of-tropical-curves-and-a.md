## Before you start

This paper is long and it touches tropical curves, quadratic forms, blow-ups, motives and group cohomology at once. That mix can raise overwhelm: the sense that you would need five backgrounds before page one. You are allowed to feel that. The introduction is self-contained and tells the whole story in ten pages. If you want to see where the cohomology of GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ) comes from, in pictures of cones, this is a good place. Read what you enjoy. Finishing is optional.

## The story

Let P<sub>g</sub> be the space of positive definite real symmetric g × g matrices. The quotient P<sub>g</sub>/GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ) computes the real cohomology of GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ). Borel found this cohomology in the stable range, where the degree is small against g. Outside that range almost nothing was known beyond computer tables for g ≤ 7. There are obvious candidate classes: the forms tr((X<sup>−1</sup>dX)<sup>4k+1</sup>). The trouble is that they blow up where det X → 0, so you cannot tell if they have compact support or are non-zero. Brown's idea is to repair the boundary. He blows up the loci of forms with rational kernel and gets a space with corners, a "bordification". He proves that the forms extend smoothly to it. Then the tables fill in: infinitely many unstable classes, and a new proof of Borel's theorem.

## Main definitions

**Canonical forms.** For X ∈ P<sub>g</sub> put ω<sup>4k+1</sup> = tr((X<sup>−1</sup>dX)<sup>4k+1</sup>), k ≥ 1. It is a closed form, invariant under GL<sub>g</sub>(ℝ) on both sides, so it descends to P<sub>g</sub>/GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ). For odd g > 1, Ω(g) is the exterior algebra on ω<sup>5</sup>, …, ω<sup>2g−1</sup>.
- Compact type: Ω<sub>c</sub>(g) is the ideal generated by the top generator ω<sup>2g−1</sup>.
- Non-compact type: Ω<sub>nc</sub>(g) is the subalgebra generated by 1, ω<sup>5</sup>, …, ω<sup>2g−5</sup>. So Ω(g) = Ω<sub>c</sub>(g) ⊕ Ω<sub>nc</sub>(g).

**Link of the moduli space of tropical abelian varieties, |LA<sub>g</sub><sup>trop</sup>|.** Take positive semi-definite matrices with rational kernel, up to GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ) and positive scaling. It is glued from Voronoi's perfect cones σ<sub>Q</sub>, each divided by a finite group. The cone σ<sub>Q</sub> is spanned by the rank one matrices λλ<sup>T</sup>, for λ a minimal vector of a positive definite form Q.
- Interior: the part with det ≠ 0 is the locally symmetric space P<sub>g</sub>/(ℝ<sub>&gt;0</sub> × GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ)).
- Example g = 2: one triangle modulo the symmetric group on three letters.

**Bordification |LA<sub>g</sub><sup>trop,B</sup>|.** In the projective space of quadratic forms on V = ℚ<sup>g</sup>, blow up the subspaces of forms with kernel K, for all rational K ≠ 0, in increasing order of dimension. Take the closure of the positive definite locus and divide by GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ). It maps onto |LA<sub>g</sub><sup>trop</sup>| and has the same interior.
- Boundary faces: one for each chain 0 &lt; K<sub>1</sub> &lt; … &lt; K<sub>n</sub> &lt; V.

## Main results

**Theorem (forms extend; Theorem 1.3).** Let g > 1 be odd. Every form of compact type ω ∈ Ω<sub>c</sub>(g) extends to a smooth form on |LA<sub>g</sub><sup>trop</sup>|. The resulting map Ω<sub>c</sub>(g) ⊗ ℝ → H<sub>dR</sub>(|LA<sub>g</sub><sup>trop</sup>|) is an injective map of graded algebras.
- Step 1: study the forms on each boundary face of the bordification, where they turn out to be finite.
- Step 2: use a de Rham theorem for spaces glued from polyhedra modulo finite groups.

**Theorem (unstable classes; Theorem 1.1).** Let g > 1 be odd. There are injections Ω<sub>nc</sub>(g) ⊗ ℝ → H<sup>•</sup>(GL<sub>h</sub>(ℤ); ℝ) for all h ≥ g. There are also injections of Ω<sub>c</sub>(g) ⊗ ℝ into compactly supported cohomology: into H<sub>c</sub><sup>•+1</sup>(GL<sub>g</sub>(ℤ); ℝ) and into H<sub>c</sub><sup>•+2</sup>(GL<sub>g+1</sub>(ℤ); ℝ). Matching statements for SL follow.
- Consequence: the first injection contains Borel's injectivity in the stable range, and goes far past it.
- New case: the statement for the even rank g + 1 is new, and uses the acyclicity of an "inflation complex" from earlier work.

**Theorem (the determinant stays away; Theorem 1.6).** The strict transform of the determinant hypersurface does not meet the bordification. The bordification minus its boundary is the locally symmetric space. An appendix shows the bordification is homeomorphic to the Borel–Serre compactification.
- Why it matters: the forms have poles only along the determinant, so they are finite on the whole bordification.

**Theorem (volumes of cones; Theorem 1.5).** Let Q be positive definite of rank g and ω a canonical form of degree dim σ<sub>Q</sub>. If σ<sub>Q</sub> meets the positive definite locus, then ∫<sub>σ<sub>Q</sub></sub> ω is finite. For cones coming from graphs under the tropical Torelli map, these integrals are Feynman integrals.

## Note, then ignore

- Link versus space. The letter L means "divide by scaling". It removes one dimension and shifts some degrees by one. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Even and odd g. For odd g the quotient is orientable and compact supports are Poincaré dual to ordinary cohomology. For even g they are not. This is why the theorem starts from odd g. Safe to skip.
- Finitely many blow-ups. The informal definition uses infinitely many subspaces K. The paper works cone by cone, in a category of diagrams of varieties, so only finitely many are needed. Safe to skip.
- Tropical curves. The paper also bordifies the moduli space of tropical curves and extends the tropical Torelli map (Theorem 1.4). It is a parallel story. Safe to skip at first.
- Motives. Section 1.8 and Theorem 1.7 attach objects in a category of realisations to these integrals. Nothing above depends on it. Safe to skip.
