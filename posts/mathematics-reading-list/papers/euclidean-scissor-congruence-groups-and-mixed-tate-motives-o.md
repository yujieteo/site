## Before you start

This is a thirteen-page paper in which most numbered statements are conjectures. The likely feeling is unease: you may ask what you are meant to learn when so little is proved, and whether you have missed the theorem. You have not missed it, and the unease is reasonable. If you like Hilbert's third problem, here it is placed inside a much larger frame in a few pages. Read for the pleasure of the frame. There is no proof to get through.

## The story

Dehn showed that volume alone does not decide when two polyhedra in ℝ<sup>3</sup> can be cut into each other. A second invariant is needed. Sydler showed that volume and the Dehn invariant together suffice. Goncharov had earlier defined scissor congruence groups S<sub>n</sub>(F) for spherical and hyperbolic geometry over any algebraically closed field F, and argued that they form the Hopf algebra of mixed Tate motives over F. Euclidean geometry was missing. Here he defines Euclidean groups E<sub>n</sub>(F) and a Dehn invariant that makes them a comodule over S<sub>•</sub>(F). The idea is that Euclidean space is a degenerate limit of curved space, so E should be the "ε-part" of the same story over the dual numbers F[ε]/ε<sup>2</sup>. The cohomology of the Euclidean Dehn complex should then be Kähler differentials of F.

## Main definitions

**Euclidean scissor congruence group E<sub>n</sub>(F).** Let A be an affine space of dimension 2n − 1 over F with a non-degenerate quadratic form Q. E<sub>n</sub>(F) is the abelian group generated by symbols (x<sub>0</sub>, …, x<sub>2n−1</sub>; vol<sub>Q</sub>), a simplex with Q non-degenerate on every face plus a choice of volume form.
- Relations: degenerate simplices are zero, the symbol is skew-symmetric, and the alternating sum over the faces of any 2n + 1 points is zero (the scissor axiom).
- Invariance: affine maps g send (x; vol<sub>Q</sub>) to (gx; g vol<sub>Q</sub>). Dilations give an action of F<sup>×</sup>, and length gives E<sub>1</sub>(F) ≅ F.

**Non-Euclidean group S<sub>n</sub>(F).** The same recipe for 2n hyperplanes through the origin in a 2n-dimensional space with a non-degenerate form Q. The sum S<sub>•</sub>(F) is a commutative graded Hopf algebra whose coproduct is the Dehn invariant, and S<sub>1</sub>(F) = F<sup>×</sup> for algebraically closed F.

**Euclidean Dehn invariant.** A homomorphism D<sup>E</sup> : E<sub>n</sub>(F) → ⊕ E<sub>k</sub>(F) ⊗ S<sub>l</sub>(F), summed over k + l = n with k, l > 0.
- Step 1: for a set I of 2l faces, intersect them to get a Euclidean affine space of dimension 2k − 1. The other faces cut out a simplex in it, an element of E<sub>k</sub>(F).
- Step 2: the quotient by that intersection is a 2l-dimensional Euclidean vector space with 2l hyperplanes, an element of S<sub>l</sub>(F). Sum over I.

**Dual numbers.** F<sub>ε</sub> = F[ε]/ε<sup>2</sup>. The paper combines the two kinds of group into Q<sub>•</sub>(F<sub>ε</sub>) = Q<sub>•</sub>(F) ⊕ E<sub>•</sub>(F)·ε, where Q<sub>•</sub>(F) is the Lie coalgebra of indecomposables of S<sub>•</sub>(F).

## Main results

**Theorem (comodule structure; Lemma 3.3).** The Dehn invariant D<sup>E</sup> makes E<sub>•</sub>(F) a comodule over the Hopf algebra S<sub>•</sub>(F). The reduced version makes it a graded comodule over the Lie coalgebra Q<sub>•</sub>(F). So Q<sub>•</sub>(F<sub>ε</sub>) is a Lie coalgebra in a tensor category of objects V<sub>0</sub> ⊕ V<sub>1</sub>ε.

**Conjecture (cohomology of the Euclidean Dehn complex; Conjecture 1.1).** Let F be algebraically closed, or F = ℝ with the classical groups. Then the i-th cohomology of the degree n part of the cobar complex E → E ⊗ S → E ⊗ S ⊗ S → … is the space of Kähler differentials Ω<sup>i−1</sup><sub>F/ℚ</sub>, with a twisted F<sup>×</sup>-action.
- Known case: for F = ℝ and n = 2 this is Sydler's theorem, the exactness of 0 → ℝ → E<sub>2</sub>(ℝ) → ℝ ⊗ S<sup>1</sup> → Ω<sup>1</sup><sub>ℝ/ℚ</sub> → 0. Volume and Dehn invariant determine a polyhedron in ℝ<sup>3</sup> up to scissor congruence.

**Conjecture (motives over dual numbers; Conjectures 1.3 and 4.2).** The category of finite-dimensional graded comodules over Q<sub>•</sub>(F<sub>ε</sub>) is equivalent to a subcategory of mixed Tate motives over F<sub>ε</sub>. The inclusion of Q<sub>•</sub>(F<sub>ε</sub>) into the conjectural motivic Lie coalgebra of F<sub>ε</sub> should induce an isomorphism on cohomology.
- Related: Conjecture 1.2 says the part of the dual Lie algebra in degrees ≤ −2 is free, with generators B<sub>n</sub>(F) ⊕ β<sub>n</sub>(F)·ε, the Bloch groups and their additive versions.

**Theorem (K-theory of dual numbers; Theorem 4.1).** For any field F, gr<sup>γ</sup><sub>n</sub>K<sub>2n−i</sub>(F<sub>ε</sub>) ⊗ ℚ = gr<sup>γ</sup><sub>n</sub>K<sub>2n−i</sub>(F) ⊗ ℚ ⊕ Ω<sup>n−i</sup><sub>F/ℚ</sub>. The paper quotes it from Goncharov's earlier work, where it is deduced from Goodwillie's theorem. It is the reason differentials appear in Conjecture 1.1.

## Note, then ignore

- The twist. The F<sup>×</sup>-module carrying the volume is written with twist n in the introduction and n − 1 in Lemma 3.2. It only records how dilations scale. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- "n + 2 points". The scissor axiom is printed for "n + 2 points x<sub>0</sub>, …, x<sub>2n</sub>", which is a list of 2n + 1 points. Read it as 2n + 1. Safe to skip.
- ℝ is not algebraically closed. The classical groups use positive definite forms only, so part b) of Conjecture 1.1 is a separate statement. Safe to skip.
- A non-rigid tensor category. Since ε·ε = 0, objects V<sub>0</sub> ⊕ V<sub>1</sub>ε have no duals. The paper needs this category only to state freeness. Safe to skip.
- Additive versus tangent. The additive groups β<sub>n</sub>(F), built from Cathelineau's groups, are not the tangent Bloch groups TB<sub>n</sub>(F). Section 2.3 explains the gap. Safe to skip.
