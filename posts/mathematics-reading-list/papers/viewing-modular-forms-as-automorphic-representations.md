## Before you start

These are 17 pages of seminar notes, written for people who had just read ten sections of Jacquet and Langlands. The likely feeling is shame at missing that background: adeles, (𝔤, K)-modules, principal series. You can feel that and still read, because the notes start from the upper half plane you already know. If you want to see why people say "Hecke eigenvalues are local data", this is a short road to it. Enjoy the dictionary first. Completeness can wait.

## The story

Classical modular forms are functions on the upper half plane. The modern theory speaks of representations of GL<sub>2</sub> over the adeles of ℚ. Booher asks how one becomes the other, with all details shown.

There are two moves. First, the upper half plane is GL<sub>2</sub>(ℝ)<sup>+</sup> modulo rotations and the centre, so a cusp form f lifts to a function F on the group. Second, strong approximation writes every adelic matrix as a product of a rational one, a real one and one in a compact open subgroup K<sub>0</sub>(N). So F extends to a function φ<sub>f</sub> on GL<sub>2</sub>(𝔸). Each classical property of f turns into a property of φ<sub>f</sub>. When f is an eigenform, φ<sub>f</sub> generates one irreducible representation π<sub>f</sub>. It factors over the places of ℚ, and the factor at p remembers a<sub>p</sub>.

## Main definitions

**The lift to the real group.** For a cusp form f of weight k, level N and character χ, set F(g) = (f|<sub>k</sub>g)(i) = (ad − bc)<sup>k/2</sup>(ci + d)<sup>−k</sup> f((ai + b)/(ci + d)) for g in GL<sub>2</sub>(ℝ)<sup>+</sup>.
- Rotations: F(gκ) = e<sup>−ikθ</sup>F(g) for a rotation κ by θ. The weight becomes a character of SO<sub>2</sub>(ℝ).

**The adelic lift.** Write g = γ g<sub>∞</sub> k<sub>0</sub> with γ in GL<sub>2</sub>(ℚ), g<sub>∞</sub> in GL<sub>2</sub>(ℝ)<sup>+</sup> and k<sub>0</sub> in K<sub>0</sub>(N). Set φ<sub>f</sub>(g) = F(g<sub>∞</sub>) λ(k<sub>0</sub>). Here λ is a character of K<sub>0</sub>(N) built from the adelic character ω attached to χ.

**Spherical representation.** An irreducible admissible representation of GL<sub>2</sub>(ℚ<sub>p</sub>) with a nonzero vector fixed by GL<sub>2</sub>(ℤ<sub>p</sub>). The spherical Hecke algebra is commutative, generated by T<sub>p</sub>, R<sub>p</sub> and the inverse of R<sub>p</sub>. The fixed vectors form a line, and the action of T<sub>p</sub> and R<sub>p</sub> on that line determines the representation (Theorems 4.2 and 4.4).

## Main results

**Proposition (two pictures of one space; Proposition 1.2).** For every positive integer N, the inclusion of the real group gives a natural bijection between Γ<sub>0</sub>(N)\GL<sub>2</sub>(ℝ)<sup>+</sup> and GL<sub>2</sub>(ℚ)\GL<sub>2</sub>(𝔸)/K<sub>0</sub>(N).
- Step 1: strong approximation gives surjectivity. It uses that ℚ has class number one.
- Step 2: two real matrices with the same image differ by an element of GL<sub>2</sub>(ℚ) ∩ K<sub>0</sub>(N) with positive determinant, which is Γ<sub>0</sub>(N).

**Proposition (φ<sub>f</sub> is a cusp form; Proposition 2.3).** The function φ<sub>f</sub> is well defined. It is an automorphic form on GL<sub>2</sub>(𝔸) with central character ω, and it is cuspidal.
- Dictionary: holomorphy becomes an eigenvalue (k/2)(1 − k/2) for the Laplacian Δ (Corollary 3.3). Vanishing at cusps becomes the vanishing of an integral over unipotent matrices.

**Theorem (the representation; Theorem 5.1).** For a cuspidal Hecke eigenform f, the form φ<sub>f</sub> lies in a unique irreducible admissible automorphic representation π<sub>f</sub> inside the cusp forms with central character ω.
- Step 1: for p ∤ N, the adelic T<sub>p</sub> acts on φ<sub>f</sub> as p<sup>1−k/2</sup> times the classical T<sub>p</sub> (Proposition 5.2). So the local factors at these p are fixed by f.
- Step 2: multiplicity one (Theorem 5.4, quoted) says two irreducible cuspidal subrepresentations that agree at almost all places are equal.

**Theorem (local factors; Theorem 5.6).** Write π<sub>f</sub> as a restricted tensor product of local factors. At ∞ the factor is a discrete series of weight k. For p ∤ N it is an unramified principal series, and its Satake parameters are the roots of x<sup>2</sup> − a<sub>p</sub>x + χ(p)p<sup>k−1</sup>.
- At p dividing N: the factor can be a principal series with one unramified character, a twist of the Steinberg representation (for example when p divides N exactly once and ω<sub>p</sub> is unramified), or supercuspidal. The notes point to an algorithm of Loeffler and Weinstein for the last case.

## Note, then ignore

- Direction of rotation. Booher's rotation matrix differs from Bump's, so e<sup>−ikθ</sup> here is e<sup>ikθ</sup> there. It is a convention. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The square root in det(g)<sup>k/2</sup>. It makes negative scalars act by χ(−1). The classical theory has the same wrinkle. Safe to skip.
- The power p<sup>1−k/2</sup>. Adelic and classical Hecke operators differ by this normalising factor. It disappears in the final polynomial for the Satake parameters. Safe to skip.
- Class number one. Strong approximation in this simple form needs it. The notes work only over ℚ. Safe to skip.
- Maass forms. The same story holds for them, and the notes say so, but treat only holomorphic forms. Safe to skip.
