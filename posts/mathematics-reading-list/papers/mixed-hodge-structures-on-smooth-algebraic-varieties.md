## Before you start

These are 27 pages of notes that fill in Deligne's proof, with spectral sequences and three filtrations on one page. The likely feeling is fatigue at index bookkeeping, and a worry that you will lose the thread. That worry is reasonable, and you can carry it along. If you want to know what "weight" means for an open variety such as a plane minus a curve, the examples in Section 7 show it with numbers. Go there for fun first. Finish the proof only if you feel like it.

## The story

A smooth projective variety has a Hodge decomposition on each cohomology group. An open variety X\* does not: the cohomology of ℂ minus a point already has a class, dz/z, that fits no pure pattern. Deligne's answer is to compactify. By Hironaka, X\* sits in a smooth projective X whose boundary D is a normal crossings divisor. Forms on X\* with at worst logarithmic poles along D compute the cohomology of X\*. Count how many factors dz/z a form has. That count is a filtration, the weight. Its graded pieces are the cohomology of the intersections of boundary components, which are smooth and projective, so they carry pure Hodge structures. A spectral sequence assembles them, and it stops early.

## Main definitions

**Logarithmic complex.** For X smooth projective and D ⊂ X a normal crossings divisor, Ω<sup>•</sup><sub>X</sub>(log D) is the complex of holomorphic forms on X\* = X \ D such that the form and its differential have at most a simple pole along D. Locally it is generated by ordinary forms and the forms dz<sub>i</sub>/z<sub>i</sub>, where z<sub>1</sub>⋯z<sub>k</sub> = 0 defines D.
- Smooth version: A<sup>•</sup><sub>X</sub>(log D), defined the same way with smooth forms.

**Weight filtration W<sub>ℓ</sub>.** A logarithmic form has weight at most ℓ if locally it can be written with at most ℓ factors dz<sub>i</sub>/z<sub>i</sub> in each term. This is an increasing filtration with W<sub>0</sub> the ordinary forms. On H<sup>m</sup>(X\*) the weight filtration is the induced one shifted by m.

**Hodge filtration F<sup>p</sup>.** The decreasing filtration by forms of type (p', q) with p' ≥ p, as in the compact case.

**Mixed Hodge structure.** A vector space with an increasing filtration W and a decreasing filtration F such that F induces a pure Hodge structure of weight k on each graded piece Gr<sup>W</sup><sub>k</sub>.

## Main results

**Theorem (log forms compute cohomology; Theorem 2.1).** The inclusions of Ω<sup>•</sup><sub>X</sub>(log D) into A<sup>•</sup><sub>X</sub>(log D), and of both into the pushforward j<sub>\*</sub>A<sup>•</sup> of all smooth forms on X\*, are quasi-isomorphisms of complexes of sheaves. So the hypercohomology of the log complex is H<sup>•</sup>(X\*, ℂ).
- Step 1: write each log form locally in terms of the dz<sub>i</sub>/z<sub>i</sub> (Lemma 2.2).
- Step 2: prove a Dolbeault lemma with logarithmic poles on a polydisc.

**Proposition (graded pieces are residues; Proposition 3.1).** The ℓ-th graded piece of the weight filtration on the log complex is quasi-isomorphic to the holomorphic de Rham complex of the disjoint union of the ℓ-fold intersections of components of D, shifted by ℓ. The map is the Poincaré residue.

**Theorem (Deligne's mixed Hodge structure; Section 5).** Let X\* be a smooth complex variety with a smooth projective compactification X and normal crossings boundary. The weight spectral sequence of the log complex has d<sub>r</sub> = 0 for all r ≥ 2. Hence Gr<sup>W</sup><sub>ℓ</sub> H<sup>m</sup>(X\*) has a pure Hodge structure of weight m + ℓ, the weights of H<sup>m</sup>(X\*) lie between m and 2m, and the lowest piece W<sub>m</sub> is the image of H<sup>m</sup>(X).
- Step 1: E<sub>1</sub> is cohomology of the boundary strata, d<sub>1</sub> is a Gysin map, so E<sub>2</sub> is pure.
- Step 2: by the lemma of two filtrations (Theorem 4.1), each later d<sub>r</sub> is a map of pure Hodge structures from higher to lower weight, hence zero.

**Theorem (functoriality; Theorem 6.1).** The mixed Hodge structure does not depend on the compactification, and a holomorphic map between smooth quasi-projective varieties induces a morphism of mixed Hodge structures on cohomology. The notes state this and refer to Deligne for the proof, which uses resolution of singularities.

## Note, then ignore

- Three filtrations on E<sub>r</sub>. A second filtration induces a first direct, a second direct and a recurrent filtration on each page. Theorem 4.1 says they agree here. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Shift conventions. The notes write W[n] and turn increasing filtrations into decreasing ones by a sign. This is bookkeeping for the spectral sequence. Skip it.
- Projective compactification. The notes assume X is projective, so that classical Hodge theory applies to X and to the boundary strata. Skip.
- Order on components. The notes fix a total order on the components of D. It only pins down signs in the residue maps. Skip.
- Appendix A. It relates d<sub>1</sub> to Gysin maps. You can take that on trust and skip it.
