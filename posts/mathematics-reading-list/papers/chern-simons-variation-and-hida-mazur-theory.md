## Before you start

This note is eight pages and reads like a dictionary with two columns. The likely feeling is a mild shame at knowing one column and not the other, knots without Hida theory or the reverse. That is the normal state of every reader of this note, and you can simply notice it. If you want one case where the knots-and-primes analogy produces a theorem and not just a table, this is it. Enjoy the table first. The theorem can come later.

## The story

A knot in the 3-sphere behaves like a prime in Spec ℤ. The knot group matches the Galois group of the maximal extension unramified outside p. In rank one, the Alexander polynomial matches the Iwasawa polynomial, and both describe how cohomology varies over a family of one-dimensional representations. Number theory has a rank-two version, due to Hida and Mazur: deform a modular Galois representation and watch a p-adic L-function vary over the deformation space. Morishita and Terashima ask what the knot side of this is. Their answer: for a hyperbolic knot, deform the holonomy representation into SL<sub>2</sub>(ℂ). The deformation space is a curve, as on the prime side. The function that varies over it is the complex Chern-Simons invariant, built from volume and the classical Chern-Simons number. They show it is a flat section of a line bundle, as the p-adic L-function is a section of one.

## Main definitions

**Character variety.** For a knot K with group G<sub>K</sub> = π<sub>1</sub>(S<sup>3</sup> ∖ K), the space X<sub>K</sub><sup>n</sup> of n-dimensional complex representations of G<sub>K</sub> up to conjugation. It is a complex affine variety.
- n = 1: X<sub>K</sub><sup>1</sup> ≅ ℂ<sup>×</sup>, the home of the Alexander polynomial.
- n = 2, K hyperbolic: use SL<sub>2</sub>(ℂ) and take the component X<sub>K</sub><sup>2,o</sup> through a lift ρ° of the holonomy of the hyperbolic structure.

**Universal deformation space.** On the prime side the group G<sub>p</sub> = π<sub>1</sub><sup>ét</sup>(Spec ℤ[1/p]) is profinite, so one fixes a residual representation ρ̄ over 𝔽<sub>p</sub> and studies its lifts to complete local rings. Mazur's universal deformation ring R exists when ρ̄ is absolutely irreducible. The space is the set of ℤ<sub>p</sub>-algebra maps R → ℂ<sub>p</sub>, seen as a rigid analytic space.
- Ordinary case: keep only deformations that are upper triangular on the decomposition group at p, with one diagonal character unramified.

**Meridian and longitude functions.** The peripheral group of the knot is abelian, generated by a meridian and a longitude. A representation ρ restricted to it is upper triangular with character χ<sub>ρ</sub>. Set m(ρ) = χ<sub>ρ</sub>(meridian) and l(ρ) = χ<sub>ρ</sub>(longitude).
- Analogy: meridian ↔ inertia at p, longitude ↔ Frobenius at p.

**Complex Chern-Simons invariant.** L<sub>K</sub>(ρ) = −2π<sup>2</sup> CS(ρ) + i Vol(ρ), for ρ near ρ° in X<sub>K</sub><sup>2,o</sup>.

## Main results

**Theorem (both spaces are curves; Theorems 2 and 5).** Knot side (Thurston): ρ ↦ tr ρ(meridian) is bianalytic near ρ°, so X<sub>K</sub><sup>2,o</sup> is a complex curve. Prime side: for p > 2, with ρ̄ absolutely irreducible and coming from an ordinary modular elliptic curve, the map φ ↦ tr ρ<sub>φ</sub>(γ) is bianalytic near the modular point, for a suitable γ in inertia.
- Hypothesis on the prime side: the ordinary deformation ring equals Hida's Hecke ring. The authors assume this and cite Wiles and others.

**Theorem (matching derivative formulas; Theorems 3 and 6).** Knot side (Neumann-Zagier): with x = log m(ρ) and the boundary torus written as ℂ<sup>×</sup>/q<sup>ℤ</sup>, one has dl/dx at x = 0 equal to (1/2)(log q)/(2πi). Prime side (Greenberg-Stevens): if E is split multiplicative at p with Tate period q, the derivative of the Frobenius eigenvalue a<sub>p</sub> at weight 2 is −(1/2) log<sub>p</sub>(q)/ord<sub>p</sub>(q).
- Reading: the modulus of the cusp torus plays the role of the Tate period, and both formulas give an ℒ-invariant.

**Theorem (Chern-Simons as a flat section; Theorem 7).** On a small affine open X of X<sub>K</sub><sup>2,o</sup> containing ρ° there is a holomorphic line bundle with holomorphic connection for which L<sub>K</sub>(ρ) is given by a flat section.
- Step 1: the quotient H(ℤ)∖H(ℂ) of the 3 × 3 Heisenberg group is a ℂ<sup>×</sup>-bundle with connection over ℂ<sup>×</sup> × ℂ<sup>×</sup>. This is Bloch's construction of the tame symbol.
- Step 2: pull it back along ρ ↦ (l(ρ), m(ρ)<sup>2</sup>). The bundle is then the Beilinson regulator of the symbol {l, m<sup>2</sup>} in K<sub>2</sub>.

**Conjecture (a two-variable L-function).** The authors expect a function L<sub>K</sub>(ρ, s) whose value at s = 0 gives L<sub>K</sub>(ρ). Their candidate is a spectral zeta function of a Dirac operator twisted by ρ. They say the needed equality is known only for closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds, by Jones-Westbury, and is open for knot complements.

## Note, then ignore

- The scan. The PDF is an image with poor text recognition, so formulas must be read from the page itself. Safe to skip once you know.
- Lifting to SL<sub>2</sub>. The holonomy lands in PSL<sub>2</sub>(ℂ), and the note lifts it to SL<sub>2</sub>(ℂ) without loss. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- m<sup>2</sup> instead of m. The pull-back uses the square of the meridian function. This is a normalisation. Safe to skip.
- Mixed Hodge structure. Theorem 8 repackages L<sub>K</sub> as a variation of mixed Hodge structure on ℤ<sup>3</sup> with graded pieces ℤ(0), ℤ(1), ℤ(2). It is a restatement of the Heisenberg picture. Safe to skip.
- Other bundles. Kirk and Klassen built a related line bundle earlier. The note does not compare the two. Safe to skip.
