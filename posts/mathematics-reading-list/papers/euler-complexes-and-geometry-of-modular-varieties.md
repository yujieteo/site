## Before you start

The introduction leans on mixed Tate motives, Bloch groups and motivic fundamental groups. The likely feeling is of arriving late to a conversation. That feeling fits the paper and says nothing about you. If you want to see a tiling of the hyperbolic plane turn into relations between special values, Figure 2 and the page around it give the whole plan. Enjoy the diagram first. The motives can come later, or never.

## The story

Double logarithms at N-th roots of unity are sums like Li<sub>1,1</sub>(a, b). In earlier work Goncharov found that relations between their motivic versions copy the triangulation of the modular curve for Γ<sub>1</sub>(N). Why a modular curve should appear was a mystery. This paper gives a reason. On the modular curve there are modular units θ(a), one for each torsion section a of the universal elliptic curve. For three torsion sections with a + b + c = 0 he builds a "double modular unit" θ(a, b, c) in the Bloch group of the curve. Triangles of the tiling go to these elements, and edges go to wedge products of units. Specialise at a cusp and you recover the cyclotomic picture. Specialise at a CM point instead and you get a new link to hyperbolic 3-folds.

## Main definitions

**Bloch complex.** For a field F, B<sub>2</sub>(F) is the free abelian group on symbols {x}, with x in the projective line over F, modulo the five-term relations of the dilogarithm. The map δ : B<sub>2</sub>(F) → Λ<sup>2</sup>F<sup>×</sup> sends {x} to (1 − x) ∧ x.
- Cokernel of δ: K<sub>2</sub>(F).
- Kernel of δ, after ⊗ℚ: the indecomposable part of K<sub>3</sub>(F).

**Modular complex for GL<sub>2</sub>(ℤ).** The chain complex of the tiling of the hyperbolic plane by ideal triangles, the images of the triangle with vertices 0, 1, ∞. It has triangles in degree 1 and geodesic edges in degree 2. Taking coinvariants under a subgroup Γ gives the chain complex of the triangulated modular curve.

**Double unit θ<sub>E</sub>(a, b, c).** For torsion points a, b, c with a + b + c = 0 on an elliptic curve E over a field k, an element of B<sub>2</sub>(k′) ⊗ ℚ, with k′ the field generated by the coordinates of a and b. Its key property is δθ<sub>E</sub>(a, b, c) = θ<sub>E</sub>(a) ∧ θ<sub>E</sub>(b) + θ<sub>E</sub>(b) ∧ θ<sub>E</sub>(c) + θ<sub>E</sub>(c) ∧ θ<sub>E</sub>(a).
- Over a modular curve: θ<sub>E</sub>(a) is a modular unit.
- At a CM curve: θ<sub>E</sub>(a) is an elliptic unit.

**Euler complex.** The subcomplex of the Bloch complex of the modular curve Y(N) spanned by the θ(a, b, c) in degree 1 and the θ(a) ∧ θ(b) in degree 2, over all N-torsion sections of the universal elliptic curve.

## Main results

**Proposition (modular to Euler; Proposition 2.16).** For every positive integer N there is a canonical surjective map of complexes from the modular complex to the Euler complex on Y(N). It factors through the coinvariants under Γ(N).
- Its second cohomology recovers the Beilinson–Kato elements in K<sub>2</sub> of modular curves, according to the introduction.

**Theorem (specialisation at a cusp; Theorem 3.4 a).** Specialising at the cusp ∞ of Y<sub>1</sub>(N) gives a surjective map from the Euler complex on Y<sub>1</sub>(N) to the level N cyclotomic complex C<sub>2</sub>(N) → Λ<sup>2</sup>C<sub>1</sub>(N). Here C<sub>1</sub>(N) is spanned by cyclotomic units and C<sub>2</sub>(N) by motivic double logarithms. The map is compatible with the maps from the modular complex to both.

**Theorem (CM points and hyperbolic 3-folds; Theorems 5.7 and 1.2).** Let K be the Gaussian or the Eisenstein field, P a prime ideal of its ring of integers O<sub>K</sub>, and Γ<sub>1</sub>(P) the subgroup of GL<sub>2</sub>(O<sub>K</sub>) fixing the row (0, 1) mod P. Let C<sub>2</sub>(P) be the span of the θ<sub>E</sub>(a, b, c) over P-torsion points of the CM curve, and C<sub>1</sub><sup>un</sup>(P) the units of the field K<sub>P</sub> generated by that torsion, tensored with ℚ. Then the cokernel of C<sub>2</sub>(P) → Λ<sup>2</sup>C<sub>1</sub><sup>un</sup>(P) is isomorphic to the cuspidal cohomology H<sup>2</sup><sub>cusp</sub>(Γ<sub>1</sub>(P), ℚ).
- Step 1: the Bianchi tessellation of hyperbolic 3-space (octahedra for Gaussian integers, tetrahedra for Eisenstein integers) gives a chain complex for Γ<sub>1</sub>(P).
- Step 2: a surjective map from that complex to the complex of double elliptic units is an isomorphism in two of the three degrees.

## Note, then ignore

- Tensor with ℚ. All complexes are rational vector spaces, so torsion is ignored throughout. Safe to skip.
- Sign of d. The introduction writes d = −1, −3 and Section 5 writes d = 1, 3 for the same two fields. Safe to skip.
- Two Bloch groups. Section 2.1 defines two versions of B<sub>2</sub>(F) and cites a result that they agree modulo torsion for number fields. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Other fields. For imaginary quadratic fields other than these two, and for non-prime ideals, the author calls the relation "elusive" or gives weaker statements. Note it and move on.
- Promised sequel. The Galois side for CM curves is deferred to a later paper. Nothing in this paper depends on it. Safe to skip.
