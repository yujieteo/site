## Before you start

The paper is ten pages, but it leans on Deligne's construction of Galois representations and on the language of motives. The likely feeling is shame at not knowing that background. You are allowed to feel it and read anyway. If you want to know where the Galois representation of a modular form "lives" as geometry, the first two pages answer that. Enjoy the trick with the sign character first. The blow-up sections can wait.

## The story

Deligne attached l-adic cohomology groups to cusp forms of weight at least 2. He found them inside the cohomology of Kuga-Sato varieties, which are compactified families of products of elliptic curves over a modular curve. Grothendieck's idea of motives suggests more: there should be an algebraic projector that cuts these groups out in every cohomology theory at once. People had guessed such motives exist.

Scholl finds the projector, and it is simple. The fibre E<sup>k</sup> has obvious symmetries: translate by points of order n, invert each factor, permute the factors. Take the part of cohomology where inversions act by −1, permutations act by sign, and translations act trivially. That part is exactly the cohomology of cusp forms of weight k + 2. The boundary contributes nothing, and that is the main work.

## Main definitions

**Kuga-Sato variety.** Fix n ≥ 3 and k ≥ 1. Let M be the modular curve over ℚ of elliptic curves with level n structure, with smooth compactification M̄, and let X̄ → M̄ be the universal generalised elliptic curve. The Kuga-Sato variety is Deligne's canonical desingularisation of the k-fold fibre product of X̄ over M̄. It is smooth and projective over ℚ.

**The group Γ<sub>k</sub> and the character ε.** Γ<sub>k</sub> is the wreath product of (ℤ/n)<sup>2</sup> ⋊ μ<sub>2</sub> with the symmetric group Σ<sub>k</sub>. It acts on the fibre product, and the action extends to the desingularisation. The character ε : Γ<sub>k</sub> → {±1} is trivial on translations, is the product map on the inversions μ<sub>2</sub><sup>k</sup>, and is the sign on Σ<sub>k</sub>.
- The projector Π<sub>ε</sub> lies in the group algebra of Γ<sub>k</sub> with coefficients ℤ[1/(2n·k!)]. Write V(ε) for the image of Π<sub>ε</sub> on V.

**Parabolic cohomology.** The group H<sup>1</sup>(M̄, j<sub>\*</sub> Sym<sup>k</sup> R<sup>1</sup>π<sub>\*</sub>ℚ), in the Betti or l-adic theory, where j : M → M̄ is the inclusion. It is attached to cusp forms of weight k + 2 and level n.

**Chow and Grothendieck motives.** A motive is a pair of a smooth projective variety and a projector made of algebraic cycles. Chow motives use cycles up to rational equivalence. Grothendieck motives use cycles up to homological equivalence, here with coefficients in a number field L.

## Main results

**Theorem (the projector works; Theorem 1.2.1).** For Betti and for l-adic cohomology, the ε-part of the whole cohomology of the Kuga-Sato variety equals the parabolic cohomology group. So the pair (Kuga-Sato variety, Π<sub>ε</sub>) is a Chow motive over ℚ whose realisations are the parabolic cohomology groups.
- Step 1: on the open part, Künneth and Leray show the ε-part is H<sup>1</sup>(M, Sym<sup>k</sup> R<sup>1</sup>π<sub>\*</sub>).
- Step 2: a long exact sequence (Theorem 1.3.3) compares the open part with the compactification. The boundary term has a different weight because k ≠ 0, so the connecting map vanishes.

**Theorem (motive of a newform; Theorem 1.2.4).** Let f = Σ a<sub>m</sub>q<sup>m</sup> be a normalised newform of weight w, level n and character χ, and let L be the field generated by the a<sub>m</sub>. Then there is a Grothendieck motive M(f) over ℚ with coefficients in L such that:
- Case p ∤ nl: for a prime λ of L above l, the λ-adic realisation is unramified at p, and geometric Frobenius has characteristic polynomial X<sup>2</sup> − a<sub>p</sub>X + χ(p)p<sup>w−1</sup>.
- Case p ∤ n and p ≥ w: for a prime of L above p, the realisation is a crystalline representation of the Galois group of ℚ<sub>p</sub>, and the crystalline Frobenius φ has the same characteristic polynomial.

**Corollary (motivic cohomology; Corollary 1.4.1).** The same exact sequence holds for Beilinson's motivic cohomology. It places the motivic cohomology of the motive inside the ε-part for the open variety, as the kernel of a boundary map to the cusps. Scholl says this leads to results on L-values, with details elsewhere.

## Note, then ignore

- Weight 2 is left out. The case k = 0 is the Jacobian of the modular curve, and the weight argument in Step 2 needs k ≠ 0. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Chow against Grothendieck. The whole space of cusp forms gives a Chow motive, but splitting off one newform is done only modulo homological equivalence. Scholl remarks that the finer splitting looks hard without the standard conjectures. Safe to skip.
- The bound p ≥ w. It comes from the range in which the Fontaine-Messing comparison was proved at the time. The paper says later work of Faltings removes it. Safe to skip.
- Level n ≥ 3. This makes the universal elliptic curve exist. It is bookkeeping. Safe to skip.
- Sections 2 and 3. They describe the desingularisation by explicit blow-ups of products of double points. You need them only to check the boundary computation. Safe to skip.
