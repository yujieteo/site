## The story

For elliptic curves a bridge joins analysis and arithmetic. The Tate curve is one equation over ℤ[[q]]. Put in a complex q and you get ℂ/L. Put in a p-adic q and you get K<sup>×</sup>/q<sup>ℤ</sup>. Its q-expansions decide which modular forms are integral. People wanted the same bridge in genus g. The tool is Schottky uniformisation: cut 2g holes in the Riemann sphere and glue them in pairs by Möbius maps. Schottky gave product formulas for the periods. Ichikawa's idea is to treat the fixed points and multipliers of those Möbius maps as formal variables. Then the periods are power series over ℤ, and a curve exists over that power series ring. This "generalised Tate curve" specialises to Riemann surfaces and to Mumford curves. It expands modular forms on M<sub>g</sub>.

## Main definitions

**Schottky group and uniformisation.** Take 2g disjoint closed discs D<sub>±1</sub>, …, D<sub>±g</sub> in ℙ<sup>1</sup>(ℂ) and elements γ<sub>i</sub> of PGL<sub>2</sub>(ℂ) that send the outside of D<sub>−i</sub> onto the inside of D<sub>i</sub>. The group Γ they generate is free of rank g. The quotient Ω<sub>Γ</sub>/Γ of its domain of discontinuity is a Riemann surface of genus g.
- Each γ<sub>i</sub> has an attracting fixed point t<sub>i</sub>, a repelling fixed point t<sub>−i</sub> and a multiplier s<sub>i</sub> with |s<sub>i</sub>| &lt; 1.
- By Koebe's theorem every Riemann surface arises this way.

**Multiplicative periods.** If Z = (z<sub>ij</sub>) is the period matrix, the multiplicative periods are exp(2πi z<sub>ij</sub>). The Jacobian is (ℂ<sup>×</sup>)<sup>g</sup> modulo the group they generate.

**Teichmüller modular form.** Let λ be the Hodge line bundle on the moduli stack M<sub>g</sub> of smooth curves of genus g > 1, that is, the top exterior power of the pushforward of relative differentials. A Teichmüller modular form of degree g and weight h with coefficients in a ℤ-module M is a global section of λ<sup>⊗h</sup> ⊗ M over M<sub>g</sub>.
- Pulling back along the Torelli map τ: M<sub>g</sub> → A<sub>g</sub> turns Siegel modular forms into Teichmüller modular forms.

## Main results

**Theorem (Schottky's formulas; Theorem 3.1).** Assume ∞ lies in Ω<sub>Γ</sub> and the series Σ |γ′(z)| over Γ converges uniformly on compact sets away from the orbit of ∞. Then explicit sums over cosets of Γ give a normalised basis of holomorphic 1-forms, and exp(2πi z<sub>ij</sub>) is an explicit infinite product of cross-ratios of fixed points.

**Theorem (generalised Tate curve; Theorem 4.2).** Fix a stable graph Δ with first Betti number g. Let A<sub>0</sub> be the ring of moduli parameters over ℤ of degenerate curves with dual graph Δ, and let A<sub>Δ</sub> = A<sub>0</sub>[[y<sub>e</sub>]] with one variable per edge e. There is a stable curve C<sub>Δ</sub> of genus g over A<sub>Δ</sub> such that:
- C<sub>Δ</sub> is a universal deformation of the degenerate curve. For small complex values of the y<sub>e</sub> it becomes a Schottky uniformised Riemann surface.
- C<sub>Δ</sub> is smooth once all y<sub>e</sub> are inverted, and it is Mumford uniformised there. Its multiplicative periods p<sub>ij</sub> are computable elements of that ring.

**Theorem (expansion principle; Theorem 6.2).** For g > 1, evaluation on C<sub>Δ</sub> gives an injective map κ<sub>Δ</sub> from Teichmüller modular forms to power series. A form has coefficients in a submodule N exactly when its expansion does. For a Siegel modular form φ, the expansion of τ<sup>\*</sup>φ is the Fourier expansion of φ with q<sub>ij</sub> replaced by p<sub>ij</sub>.
- Consequence: φ vanishes on the Jacobian locus if and only if its Fourier series vanishes after putting q<sub>ij</sub> = p<sub>ij</sub>. This is one answer to the Schottky problem.

**Theorem (integral ring; Theorem 6.3).** For g ≥ 3 the ring of integral Teichmüller modular forms is normal and finitely generated over ℤ. In genus 3 it is generated by integral Siegel modular forms together with one extra form of weight 9, a square root of the product of even theta constants up to a constant.

## Note, then ignore

- Convergence in Theorem 3.1. The formulas need a convergence assumption on the Schottky group. It holds when the multipliers are small. Safe to skip at first.
- Maximally degenerate case. When every vertex of Δ has three branches, A<sub>0</sub> = ℤ. Ihara and Nakamura treated that case earlier. The general statement only adds parameters.
- Stacks versus level structure. The notes switch between the moduli stack and fine moduli schemes with level n ≥ 3. This is bookkeeping. Skip it.
- Genus 2. There M<sub>2</sub> is affine over a field of characteristic not 2, so spaces of forms are infinite-dimensional and negative weights occur. It is a special case to ignore at first.
- Typos. These are informal notes with small slips in wording and references. They do not affect the statements.
