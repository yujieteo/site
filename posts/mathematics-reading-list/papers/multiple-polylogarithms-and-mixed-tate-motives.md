## Before you start

This paper runs to about 80 pages and uses Hodge theory, motives and K-theory in the first paragraph. The likely feeling is shame at not knowing the background. That feeling is allowed. Nobody reads this paper with all of it in hand. If you want to know why relations between zeta values have anything to do with geometry, the introduction alone tells you. Read it for the pleasure of the idea. Finishing can wait.

## The story

Euler studied sums ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, ..., n<sub>m</sub>) over 0 &lt; k<sub>1</sub> &lt; ... &lt; k<sub>m</sub> of 1/(k<sub>1</sub><sup>n<sub>1</sub></sup> ⋯ k<sub>m</sub><sup>n<sub>m</sub></sup>). They satisfy many linear relations over ℚ. In weight 11 there are 512 convergent sums, and the conjectured number of new generators is 2. People wanted to know all relations. The block is transcendence: nobody can prove even that ζ(5) is irrational. Goncharov's idea is to stop working with the numbers. Each multiple zeta value is an iterated integral on ℙ<sup>1</sup> minus {0, 1, ∞}, so it is a period of a mixed Hodge structure built from paths on that space. He replaces the number by that structure with a marked period. These objects form a Hopf algebra. The coproduct cannot be seen on numbers. Bounding the Hopf algebra is algebra, not transcendence, and it bounds the numbers from above.

## Main definitions

**Multiple polylogarithm.** For integers n<sub>i</sub> ≥ 1 and |x<sub>i</sub>| &lt; 1, Li<sub>n<sub>1</sub>,...,n<sub>m</sub></sub>(x<sub>1</sub>, ..., x<sub>m</sub>) is the sum over 0 &lt; k<sub>1</sub> &lt; ... &lt; k<sub>m</sub> of x<sub>1</sub><sup>k<sub>1</sub></sup> ⋯ x<sub>m</sub><sup>k<sub>m</sub></sup> / (k<sub>1</sub><sup>n<sub>1</sub></sup> ⋯ k<sub>m</sub><sup>n<sub>m</sub></sup>). The weight is n<sub>1</sub> + ... + n<sub>m</sub> and the depth is m.
- Case m = 1: the classical polylogarithm Li<sub>n</sub>(x).
- Case all x<sub>i</sub> = 1: the multiple zeta value ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, ..., n<sub>m</sub>).

**Framed Hodge-Tate structure.** A Hodge-Tate structure is a mixed Hodge structure over ℚ with h<sup>p,q</sup> = 0 for p ≠ q. An n-framing is a pair of non-zero maps ℚ(0) → Gr<sup>W</sup><sub>0</sub>H and Gr<sup>W</sup><sub>−2n</sub>H → ℚ(n). It picks out one period of H.
- Equivalence: generated by maps H<sub>1</sub> → H<sub>2</sub> that respect the frames.
- Result: the classes form a commutative graded Hopf algebra H<sub>•</sub> over ℚ.

**Mixed Tate motives over O<sub>F,S</sub>.** For a number field F and a finite set S of primes, MT(O<sub>F,S</sub>) is an abelian category containing Tate objects ℚ(n), built in section 3 from the triangulated categories of Levine and Voevodsky and the Tannakian formalism.

**Torsor of paths.** P(X; x, y) is the pro-object that linearises homotopy classes of paths from x to y in X. On ℙ<sup>1</sup> minus {0, 1, ∞} the base points are tangent vectors at 0 and 1.

## Main results

**Theorem (Ext groups; formulas (11) and (12)).** In MT(O<sub>F,S</sub>), Ext<sup>1</sup>(ℚ(0), ℚ(n)) = K<sub>2n−1</sub>(O<sub>F,S</sub>) ⊗ ℚ, and Ext<sup>i</sup> vanishes for i &gt; 1.
- Step 1: the category is equivalent to graded modules over a graded Lie algebra L<sub>•</sub>(O<sub>F,S</sub>), which is therefore free.
- Step 2: for ℤ, Borel's ranks give one generator in each odd degree 3, 5, 7, ..., so L<sub>•</sub>(ℤ) is the free Lie algebra F(3, 5, ...).

**Theorem (periods of the path torsor; Theorem 1.3).** The Hodge-Tate structure of paths on ℙ<sup>1</sup>(ℂ) minus {0, 1, ∞} from the tangential base point at 0 to that at 1, with any framing, is equivalent to a product of the framed structures ζ<sup>H</sup>(n<sub>1</sub>, ..., n<sub>m</sub>). So all its periods are ℚ-linear combinations of multiple zeta values times powers of 2πi.
- Case of level N: Theorem 1.6 says the same for ℙ<sup>1</sup> minus {0, ∞} and the N-th roots of unity, with multiple polylogarithms at roots of unity.

**Theorem (upper bound; Theorem 1.5).** The elements ζ<sup>H</sup> form a graded Hopf algebra Z<sup>H</sup><sub>•</sub>. It maps onto the weight-graded algebra of the numbers (2πi)<sup>−w</sup>ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, ..., n<sub>m</sub>). So the dimension of each weight-graded piece of the numbers is at most the dimension of Z<sup>H</sup><sub>k</sub>.

**Conjecture (structure of multiple zeta values; Conjecture 1.1).** The weight grades the ℚ-algebra Z of multiple zeta values, and Z ≅ ℚ[π<sup>2</sup>] ⊗ U F(3, 5, ...)<sup>∨</sup>, the graded dual of the enveloping algebra of the free Lie algebra. This predicts d<sub>k</sub> = d<sub>k−2</sub> + d<sub>k−3</sub> for d<sub>k</sub> = dim Z<sub>k</sub>.

## Note, then ignore

- Summation order. Goncharov sums over increasing k<sub>1</sub> &lt; ... &lt; k<sub>m</sub>. Other authors use the reverse order. It only relabels indices. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- No weight grading on the tilde algebra. Dividing by (2πi)<sup>w</sup> makes ζ(2) rational, so that algebra is only filtered. This is why "Gr<sup>W</sup>" appears. Safe to skip.
- Tangential base points. The points 0 and 1 are not in the space, so paths start along tangent vectors. This is a regularisation device. Safe to skip.
- "In the continuation of this paper". The claim that the motivic path torsor at level N lives over the N-cyclotomic integers is announced here and proved later. The introduction says so. Safe to skip.
- Motives over ℤ. No triangulated category of motives over Spec ℤ was available, so MT(O<sub>F,S</sub>) is cut out of motives over F. Safe to skip.
