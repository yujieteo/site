## Before you start

This note is six pages, yet its first page uses supermanifolds, Morita equivalence and moment maps in quick succession. You may feel exposed if one of the three is new to you, as if a short paper ought to be easy. You are allowed to feel that. The proofs are finite-dimensional linear algebra with explicit bases, so nothing is hidden. If you have wondered why Clifford algebras repeat with period eight, here is one hands-on reason. Enjoy the computation with four odd variables first. The rest can wait.

## The story

Real Clifford algebras repeat with period eight up to Morita equivalence: Cliff(8) ≃ ℝ. Along the way Cliff(4) ≃ ℍ. These facts are usually checked by writing matrices, which shows that they hold but not where they come from. Johnson-Freyd looks for a geometric source. A Clifford algebra is the Weyl algebra of a purely odd symplectic vector space ℝ<sup>0|n</sup>, the way the Heisenberg algebra is for an even one. For a split form, a Lagrangian subspace gives a Morita equivalence to ℝ at once. The positive-definite form has no Lagrangian, so this fails. The idea that replaces it: divide by a symmetry group. A Hamiltonian reduction quantises to a bimodule, and for Clifford algebras that bimodule is a Morita equivalence whenever the reduced algebra is non-zero. Three groups, Spin(3), G<sub>2</sub> and Spin(7), give the three classical equivalences.

## Main definitions

**Cliff(n) as a Weyl algebra.** ℝ<sup>0|n</sup> has odd coordinates x<sub>1</sub>, ..., x<sub>n</sub> and the positive-definite symplectic form Σ (dx<sub>i</sub>)<sup>2</sup>/2. Its canonical quantisation is Cliff(n), generated by odd x<sub>i</sub> with x<sub>i</sub><sup>2</sup> = −1 and x<sub>i</sub>x<sub>j</sub> = −x<sub>j</sub>x<sub>i</sub>.
- Cliff(−n) comes from the form with the opposite sign, and has x<sub>i</sub><sup>2</sup> = 1.

**Super Morita equivalence.** Two ℤ/2-graded algebras A and B are Morita equivalent if graded bimodules M and N exist with M ⊗<sub>B</sub> N ≅ A and N ⊗<sub>A</sub> M ≅ B. All statements below are in this graded sense.

**Quantum Hamiltonian reduction.** Let G act on an algebra A with comoment map μ\* : 𝔤 → A, a Lie algebra map whose commutators give the infinitesimal action. Let ⟨μ\*𝔤⟩ be the left ideal generated by its image. Then A//G = (A/⟨μ\*𝔤⟩)<sup>G</sup>.
- It is an algebra because it equals the endomorphisms of the A-module A/⟨μ\*𝔤⟩.
- For compact G it is A<sup>G</sup> modulo A<sup>G</sup> ∩ ⟨μ\*𝔤⟩.

## Main results

**Lemma (non-zero suffices; Lemma 1).** If Cliff(n)//G is not the zero algebra, then the module Cliff(n)/⟨μ\*𝔤⟩ is a Morita equivalence between Cliff(n) and Cliff(n)//G.
- Step 1: after tensoring with ℂ, a linear Lagrangian or coisotropic subspace shows Cliff(n) ⊗ ℂ is Morita equivalent to ℂ or to Cliff(1) ⊗ ℂ.
- Step 2: over those two algebras every non-zero finitely generated module is a projective generator, and these properties are Morita invariant.

**Theorem (quaternions; Theorem 1).** SO(4) contains two commuting copies of Spin(3) acting on ℝ<sup>0|4</sup>. Reduction by either gives Cliff(4)//Spin(3) ≅ ℍ, with ℍ purely even, hence Cliff(4) ≃ ℍ.
- Step 1: the invariants of one copy are spanned by 1, the three quadratic generators a, b, c of the other copy, and the top element θ = wxyz.
- Step 2: the ideal meets the invariants only in the line through θ + 1, which forces a<sup>2</sup> = b<sup>2</sup> = c<sup>2</sup> = −1.

**Theorem (G<sub>2</sub>; Theorem 2).** Let G<sub>2</sub> ⊂ SO(7) act on ℝ<sup>0|7</sup>. Then Cliff(7)//G<sub>2</sub> ≅ Cliff(−1), hence Cliff(7) ≃ Cliff(−1).
- The invariants are spanned by 1, the octonionic 3-form, its dual 4-form and the volume element θ, with θ<sup>2</sup> = 1. The ideal removes the two forms and leaves 1 and θ.

**Theorem (Bott periodicity; Theorem 3).** Let Spin(7) act on ℝ<sup>0|8</sup> by its spin representation. Then Cliff(8)//Spin(7) ≅ ℝ, hence Cliff(8) ≃ ℝ.
- Step 1: the invariants are spanned by 1, the Cayley 4-form φ and θ. The element φ + 14 lies in the ideal, so the reduction is a quotient of ℝ.
- Step 2: every generator α of the ideal kills φ + 1 + θ, so 1 is not in the ideal and the quotient is not zero.

## Note, then ignore

- Sign of the Poisson bracket. The paper has {x<sub>i</sub>, x<sub>j</sub>} = −2δ<sub>ij</sub> and says the sign depends on a convention. It only decides which algebra is called Cliff(n) and which Cliff(−n). Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Odd forms do not anticommute. For odd x the form dx is even, so dx ∧ dx ≠ 0. That is why an odd symplectic form behaves like a metric. Safe to skip.
- Spin(8) fails. The paper notes that Cliff(8)//Spin(8) is zero, because the comoment image contains invertible elements. It explains the choice of Spin(7). Safe to skip.
- The classical limit is singular. Zero is not a regular value of the moment map, so ℝ<sup>0|4</sup>//Spin(3) is only an affine super scheme. The quantum statement does not need it. Safe to skip.
- Even coordinates. With even variables the same module is a Morita equivalence only after analytic completion, which is the Stone–von Neumann theorem. No even variables occur here. Safe to skip.
