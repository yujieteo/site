## Before you start

This is a 43-page thesis whose first sections review complex, symplectic and Kähler geometry, connections, holomorphic bundles and theta functions before the subject begins. The likely feeling is overwhelm at the length of the list: two whole fields, and a category from each. Nothing is wrong with feeling that. If you have wanted to see mirror symmetry as a statement you can check by hand, Section 6 does it with three lines on a square and one theta identity. Go there for fun. The foundations can be looked up as you need them.

## The story

Physicists saw that two different Calabi-Yau manifolds can give the same physics. In 1994 Kontsevich proposed a mathematical meaning. The symplectic geometry of one manifold, packaged as its Fukaya category, should equal the complex geometry of the other, packaged as its derived category of coherent sheaves. The trouble is that both categories are hard to define, let alone compare. The elliptic curve is the one case where everything is explicit, and Polishchuk and Zaslow worked it out. On the symplectic torus the objects are closed geodesics carrying flat bundles. A line of slope n goes to a line bundle of degree n, and a vertical line goes to a skyscraper sheaf. Intersection points go to theta functions. Composition counts triangles weighted by area, and the weights sum to theta constants. Port's thesis rebuilds this dictionary slowly.

## Main definitions

**The mirror pair.** Let τ = b + iA with A > 0. The A-side E<sup>τ</sup> is the 2-torus ℝ<sup>2</sup>/ℤ<sup>2</sup> with symplectic form A dx ∧ dy and B-field b dx ∧ dy. The B-side E<sub>τ</sub> is the complex torus ℂ/Λ for the lattice Λ generated by 1 and τ.

**Objects of the Fukaya category.** Triples (L, α, M). L is a closed geodesic of E<sup>τ</sup>, the image of a line of rational slope. The real number α fixes a grading and an orientation. M is the monodromy of a flat complex vector bundle on L, with eigenvalues of modulus one.
- Shift: (L, α, M)[1] = (L, α + 1, M).

**Morphisms and composition.** For L ≠ L′, Hom is the direct sum over intersection points p of Hom(M<sub>p</sub>, M′<sub>p</sub>). The product m<sub>2</sub> sums over triangles bounded by three geodesics, each weighted by an exponential of its area and by parallel transport along its edges.
- On the elliptic curve the differential m<sub>1</sub> vanishes, so m<sub>2</sub> is associative.

**The category FK<sup>0</sup>(E<sup>τ</sup>).** The degree zero part of the Fukaya category with finite direct sums added formally: objects are tuples of objects and morphisms are matrices. This makes it an additive category.

## Main results

**Theorem (Polishchuk–Zaslow, with Kreussler's refinement; Theorem 8.4).** There is a functor Φ<sub>τ</sub> from D<sup>b</sup>(E<sub>τ</sub>), the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves, to FK<sup>0</sup>(E<sup>τ</sup>). It is an equivalence of additive categories and it commutes with the shift functors.
- Step 1: by Atiyah's classification (Theorem 7.6) every indecomposable bundle is a pushforward, along a covering E<sub>rτ</sub> → E<sub>τ</sub>, of a line bundle tensored with a unipotent bundle. So define Φ on those and on torsion sheaves.
- Step 2: on morphisms, send a basis of theta functions to intersection points with explicit coefficients, and check composition. The thesis refers to the original papers for that check.

**Claim (the dictionary on objects; Section 8.3).** A rank k bundle L(φ) ⊗ F(V, exp N), with L(φ) a line bundle of degree n, goes to a geodesic of slope n carrying a rank k local system. Translating the bundle in the real direction changes the monodromy. Translating it in the τ direction slides the geodesic. An indecomposable torsion sheaf supported at one point goes to a vertical geodesic with grading 1/2.

**Claim (the simplest example; Section 6).** Take τ = iA and lines L<sub>0</sub>, L<sub>1</sub>, L<sub>2</sub> through the origin with slopes 0, 1, 2. They correspond to O, L and L<sup>2</sup> for a fixed degree one line bundle L. Then L<sub>0</sub> ∩ L<sub>1</sub> and L<sub>1</sub> ∩ L<sub>2</sub> are one point each, and L<sub>0</sub> ∩ L<sub>2</sub> is two points. The triangle counts are Σ exp(−2πA n<sup>2</sup>) and Σ exp(−2πA (n + 1/2)<sup>2</sup>) over integers n. These are the coefficients in the addition formula that expands θ<sub>τ</sub>(z)<sup>2</sup> in the two-dimensional space of sections of L<sup>2</sup>.

## Note, then ignore

- Not the full conjecture. The theorem compares additive categories built from degree zero cohomology. It does not treat the A<sub>∞</sub> structure or a triangulated enlargement of the Fukaya category. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The grading α. It supplies an orientation and a shift functor on the A-side. In the simplest example it plays almost no part. Safe to skip.
- The B-field. It makes the area weights complex. Set b = 0 at first and nothing in the story changes.
- Non-transversal pairs. The original proof handled geodesics that meet transversally. A later paper extended it to all pairs and added the formal direct sums. Safe to skip.
- Which translation does what. The formulas in Sections 6 and 8.3 put the τ-direction shift into the position of the geodesic and the real shift into the monodromy. One sentence of prose in Section 8.3 says the reverse. This page follows the formulas. Safe to skip.
