## Before you start

These are 12 pages of terse lecture notes, and from Talk 2 on they lean on condensed mathematics. You may feel lost when "gaseous" and "nuclear" arrive with almost no warm-up. That feeling is fair. The notes are a sketch, and nobody follows every line of a sketch. If you want to know what Deligne cohomology is for, Talk 1 alone gives you that. Enjoy the first three pages, and treat the rest as a trailer.

## The story

A holomorphic vector bundle on a complex manifold M has Chern classes in H<sup>2p</sup>(M; ℤ). These forget the holomorphic structure. Deligne cohomology repairs this. It glues integer cohomology to the part of the holomorphic de Rham complex in degrees ≥ p, and Chern classes lift to it. One hopes that K-theory of vector bundles then computes Deligne cohomology, as motivic cohomology is computed by algebraic K-theory. It does not: the map from K<sub>0</sub> is far from an isomorphism. Clausen and Scholze change the K-theory. They rebuild complex geometry from rings of holomorphic functions with a "gaseous" completeness structure, define nuclear sheaves on M, and take continuous K-theory of those. This K<sup>nuc</sup> maps to a Deligne version of K-theory. The conjecture is that the map is an isomorphism.

## Main definitions

**Deligne complex ℤ(p)<sub>D</sub>.** For p ≥ 0, ℤ(p)<sub>D</sub> is the homotopy pullback of (2πi)<sup>p</sup>ℤ → Ω<sub>dR</sub> ← F<sup>p</sup>Ω<sub>dR</sub>. Here Ω<sub>dR</sub> is the holomorphic de Rham complex and F<sup>p</sup>Ω<sub>dR</sub> is its part Ω<sup>p</sup> → Ω<sup>p+1</sup> → ….
- p = 0: ℤ(0)<sub>D</sub> = ℤ.
- p = 1: ℤ(1)<sub>D</sub> = O<sup>×</sup>[−1], so a line bundle has a tautological class c<sub>1</sub> in H<sup>2</sup>(M; ℤ(1)<sub>D</sub>).

**Gaseous ℂ-vector space.** A light condensed ℂ-module M is gaseous if 1 − T/2 is an isomorphism on the null sequences of M, where T shifts a sequence by one place. This says that for a null sequence m<sub>0</sub>, m<sub>1</sub>, … the sum Σ m<sub>n</sub>(1/2)<sup>n</sup> exists in M.
- Example: every Banach space over ℂ is gaseous.

**Bounded gaseous algebra and its Gelfand spectrum.** A gaseous ℂ-algebra R is bounded if every element f, also in families, becomes (uniformly) topologically nilpotent after scaling by some λ with 0 &lt; |λ| &lt; 1. Its Gelfand spectrum M<sub>B</sub>(R) is the set of ℂ-algebra maps R(\*) → ℂ with the product topology.
- Example: for R the functions holomorphic near the closed polydisc D<sup>n</sup>, M<sub>B</sub>(R) = D<sup>n</sup>.

**Nuclear modules and the three K-theories.** Nuc is the full subcategory generated by sequential colimits along trace-class maps. K<sup>nuc</sup> is continuous K-theory of nuclear modules. K<sup>Del</sup> is the pullback of KU → HP ← HC<sup>−</sup>, with cyclic homology taken relative to the gaseous base.
- K<sup>Del</sup> has a filtration with graded pieces ℤ(p)<sub>D</sub>[2p], and it splits rationally.

## Main results

**Theorem (sheaves from algebra; Theorems 2.13, 3.1 and 3.10).** If R is pointwise bounded, M<sub>B</sub>(R(\*)) is compact Hausdorff and the category D(R) of R-modules in derived gaseous vector spaces localises along it. For bounded R, the functor M ↦ (K ↦ M ⊗<sub>R</sub> O(K)) is an equivalence from D(R) to modules over the structure sheaf.
- Step 1: closed subsets K go to idempotent R-algebras O(K), fixed by their values on the sets {|f| ≤ C} and {|f| ≥ C}.
- Step 2: full faithfulness is easy, and the generators attached to open sets are hit by fibres of R → O(K).

**Theorem (comparison map; Theorem 4.4).** There is a natural map K<sup>nuc</sup> → K<sup>Del</sup> of sheaves of spectra on complex manifolds.
- Input: a trace map K<sup>nuc</sup> → HC<sup>−</sup>, which uses that the nuclear gaseous category is rigid.

**Conjecture (modified Hodge conjecture; Main Conjecture 4.5).** The map K<sup>nuc</sup> → K<sup>Del</sup> is an isomorphism.
- Equivalent form (Corollary 4.11): the fibre of K<sup>nuc</sup> → HC<sup>−</sup> is invariant under the open disc.
- Evidence: the notes say the analogous rigidity statement holds with solid ℚ<sub>p</sub> in place of gaseous ℂ.

**Theorem (homotopy-invariant part; Theorem 4.10).** The homotopification of K<sup>nuc</sup> is RΓ(−; KU), the map from K<sup>nuc</sup> to it is an isomorphism in degrees ≤ 0, and the homotopification of HC<sup>−</sup> is HP.
- Step 1: GAGA for ℙ<sup>1</sup> inverts the Bott class.
- Step 2: Grauert–Oka gives homotopy invariance of K<sub>0</sub>, and Bass delooping pushes it to negative degrees.

## Note, then ignore

- Light condensed sets. The notes use sheaves on countable limits of finite sets to make "topological vector space" behave categorically. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Pointwise bounded versus bounded. The second asks for uniform control in families and is due to Rodriguez-Camargo. Safe to skip.
- D(ℂ<sub>gas</sub>) is not rigid. This is why the notes pass to nuclear objects before taking K-theory. Safe to skip at first.
- Joint work. Talks 2 to 4 report work with Scholze, and most proofs are one line or absent. Safe to skip the proofs.
- Typos and question marks. The title misspells "perspectives", and a few statements carry a "(?)". They are live notes. Safe to ignore.
