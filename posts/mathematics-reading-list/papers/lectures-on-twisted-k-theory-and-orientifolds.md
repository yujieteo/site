## Before you start

These are 66 pages of informal notes whose second and third lectures speak the language of superstrings: B-fields, orientifolds, worldsheets, pfaffians. The likely feeling is the outsider's unease, a sense that the physics is a wall you were never given the key to. You can let that unease be there. Lecture 1 is pure mathematics, built from linear algebra and Clifford algebras, and it stands alone. If you want to know what "twisted" means in twisted K-theory, that lecture answers it with examples. Enjoy the examples first. The string theory is optional.

## The story

K-theory is built from vector spaces. To twist it, replace vector spaces by modules over an algebra A, chosen so that A-modules still look like "rank one" over vector spaces. Such A are exactly the central simple algebras, and in the ℤ/2ℤ-graded world every class is represented by a Clifford algebra. Now let A vary over a space or groupoid X: a bundle of such algebras, glued by bimodules, is a twisting. Three layers appear: the degree, a double cover, and a gerbe. Earlier treatments took them one at a time, but they are linked and do not split apart. Freed packages all three in one spectrum, a small truncation of real K-theory. That link to KO is then the tool for the string theory result: the B-field is a twisting, and its anomaly cancels the fermion anomaly.

## Main definitions

**Invertible super algebra.** Work in the 2-category whose objects are ℤ/2ℤ-graded algebras over k = ℝ or ℂ, whose 1-morphisms are bimodules and whose 2-morphisms are intertwiners. A is invertible if some A′ makes A ⊗ A′ Morita equivalent to k. The classes form the super Brauer group of k.
- Example: every Clifford algebra of a non-degenerate quadratic form.

**Invertible algebra bundle.** Over a topological groupoid X with objects X<sub>0</sub> and arrows X<sub>1</sub>: a bundle of central simple super algebras on X<sub>0</sub>, an invertible bimodule on X<sub>1</sub> relating the algebras at the two ends of each arrow, and an isomorphism on composable pairs with a cocycle condition (Definition 1.59).

**Twisting.** A twisting of K-theory on X is a pair (P, A): a local equivalence of groupoids P → X and a complex invertible algebra bundle A on P (Definition 1.78). Real algebras give twistings of KO.
- Why P: on the 3-sphere every algebra bundle is constant, and the classes in H<sup>3</sup>(S<sup>3</sup>; ℤ) appear only after passing to the groupoid of an open cover.

**Twisted K-theory class.** Finite rank A-twisted vector bundles represent only torsion twistings (Remark 1.87). In general a class is a family of odd skew-adjoint Fredholm operators on graded Hilbert A-modules.

## Main results

**Theorem (invertible means central simple; Theorems 1.28 and 1.30).** A super algebra over k is invertible if and only if it is finite dimensional and central simple. The super Brauer group of ℂ is cyclic of order 2 and that of ℝ is cyclic of order 8. Each is generated by the Clifford algebra of a line with a positive definite form. The notes credit the underlying results to Wall and Deligne.

**Theorem (classification of twistings; Theorems 1.52 and 1.82).** Let R be the truncation of connective ko to homotopy degrees 0 to 4. For a local quotient groupoid X, isomorphism classes of K-theory twistings on X form the group R<sup>−1</sup>(X). The notes do not prove Theorem 1.82 and refer to Freed, Hopkins and Teleman for the case without the degree.
- Step 1: the 2-groupoid of complex invertible algebras has homotopy groups ℤ/2ℤ, ℤ/2ℤ, 0, ℤ in degrees 0 to 3.
- Step 2: its k-invariants match those of Σ<sup>−1</sup>R, and a lemma shows this fixes the spectrum.

**Theorem (the three layers; exact sequence (1.50)).** For a space X there is a short exact sequence 0 → H<sup>3</sup>(X; ℤ) → R<sup>−1</sup>(X) → H<sup>0</sup>(X; ℤ/2ℤ) × H<sup>1</sup>(X; ℤ/2ℤ) → 0. It splits as sets but not as abelian groups.

**Theorem (anomaly cancellation; Theorem 3.1).** For a family of Type II superstring worldsheets over a base S, with orientifold, the Pfaffian line bundle of the fermions and the B-field line bundle are flat, and their tensor product is trivialisable. The notes prove that a trivialisation exists and do not construct one.
- Step 1: a flat line bundle is determined by its holonomies, so take S to be a circle.
- Step 2: an index theorem of Atiyah, Patodi and Singer type equates the two holonomies, using the twisted spin structure on spacetime.

## Note, then ignore

- Koszul sign rule. Every tensor product is graded and swapping two odd elements costs a sign. It makes the Clifford algebras behave. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Local quotient groupoids. This is the class of groupoids for which the classification holds, and it includes quotients of nice spaces by compact Lie groups. Safe to skip.
- Operator topology. Bundles of Hilbert spaces need the compact-open topology, not the norm topology. The notes defer to Atiyah and Segal. Safe to skip.
- KR-theory. A double cover of X twists the twistings themselves (Theorem 1.103). It matters only for orientifolds. Safe to skip.
- Differential twistings. These refine the topology with connection-like data for the physics. The notes then work without choosing a model. Safe to skip.
