## Before you start

This paper is 28 pages in the Annals and has a reputation: it settled two well-known conjectures at once. The likely feeling is intimidation, the sense that a result of this standing is not for a self-taught reader. That feeling makes sense, and you do not have to get rid of it. The statement is about sums you can write down, and the proof is linear algebra on words in two letters once one formula is granted. If you want to know why multiple zeta values have exactly the dimensions people guessed, the answer is here. Read the two-page outline for enjoyment. Finishing is a separate matter.

## The story

Multiple zeta values ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) satisfy many rational linear relations. Hoffman guessed that those with every argument equal to 2 or 3 span all of them. Separately, people believed that the fundamental group of the line minus 0, 1, ∞ generates all mixed Tate motives over ℤ. Both are hard because nobody can prove that real numbers like these are linearly independent. Brown replaces each multiple zeta value by a motivic version, an algebraic object on which a group acts. Independence can then be tested with the coaction of that group. The size of the ambient space is known: its dimension d<sub>k</sub> in weight k satisfies d<sub>k</sub> = d<sub>k−2</sub> + d<sub>k−3</sub>, which is also the number of words in 2 and 3 of weight k. So it is enough to show the Hoffman elements are independent.

## Main definitions

**MT(ℤ).** The category of mixed Tate motives unramified over ℤ. It is equivalent to the representations of a group G<sub>MT</sub>, a semi-direct product of the multiplicative group with a pro-unipotent group G<sub>U</sub>. The Lie algebra of G<sub>U</sub> is free with one generator σ<sub>2n+1</sub> in degree −2n − 1 for each n ≥ 1.

**Motivic multiple zeta value.** Take the ring of functions on the de Rham path torsor of the line minus three points from 0 to 1. It is spanned by words in e<sub>0</sub>, e<sub>1</sub>. Divide by the largest graded ideal of relations between multiple zeta values that is stable under the coaction. The image of the word for (n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) is ζ<sup>m</sup>(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>), with n<sub>r</sub> ≥ 2.
- Period: a map to ℝ sends ζ<sup>m</sup>(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) to ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>).
- Here ζ<sup>m</sup>(2) is not zero, unlike in Goncharov's earlier version.

**Level.** On the span of the ζ<sup>m</sup>(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) with all n<sub>i</sub> in {2, 3}, the level is the number of n<sub>i</sub> equal to 3. It gives an increasing filtration.

## Main results

**Theorem (Hoffman basis; Theorem 1.1).** The elements ζ<sup>m</sup>(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) with every n<sub>i</sub> in {2, 3} form a basis of the ℚ-vector space of motivic multiple zeta values.
- Step 1: G<sub>U</sub> preserves the level filtration and acts trivially on its graded pieces. So infinitesimal coaction maps go from level ℓ to level ℓ − 1, and their matrices have entries built from coefficients c<sub>w</sub> attached to words with a single 3.
- Step 2: Zagier's formula for ζ(2, …, 2, 3, 2, …, 2), lifted to motivic values, gives the c<sub>w</sub>. Their 2-adic valuations make the matrices upper triangular to leading 2-adic order, hence invertible. Induct on the level.

**Theorem (generation; Conjecture 1 of the paper, proved).** The map from G<sub>MT</sub> to the Galois group of the subcategory generated by the motivic fundamental group of the line minus 0, 1, ∞ is an isomorphism. So that fundamental group generates MT(ℤ).
- Why: the Hoffman basis has d<sub>k</sub> elements in weight k, which already equals the dimension of the largest possible space.

**Theorem (Hoffman's conjecture, algebraic part; Conjecture 2 of the paper, proved).** Every multiple zeta value is a ℚ-linear combination of the ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) with all n<sub>i</sub> in {2, 3}. This follows by applying the period map.

**Corollary (periods; Corollary 1.2).** The periods of every mixed Tate motive over ℤ are linear combinations, with coefficients in ℚ[1/(2πi)], of the ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) with all n<sub>i</sub> in {2, 3}.

## Note, then ignore

- Spanning, not independence, for real numbers. The theorem does not show that the real numbers ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) with arguments 2 and 3 are linearly independent. That is still open. Keep this in mind, then move on.
- Weight. The paper's weight is half the motivic weight, to match the usual convention for multiple zeta values. Safe to skip.
- Order of summation. The paper sums over 0 &lt; k<sub>1</sub> &lt; ⋯ &lt; k<sub>r</sub>, so the last argument must be at least 2. Other papers reverse this. Safe to skip.
- The element f<sub>2</sub>. An extra generator in degree 2 with trivial coaction keeps track of ζ<sup>m</sup>(2). It is bookkeeping. Safe to skip.
- Choices. The embedding of motivic values into the model comodule depends on a choice of rational point. The results do not. Safe to skip.
