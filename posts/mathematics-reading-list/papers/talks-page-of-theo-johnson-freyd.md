## Before you start

The feeling here is likely to be overwhelm. The page holds well over a hundred talks, from 2007 to 2026, and many abstracts use words such as "categorical spectra" without defining them. You are allowed to feel lost in that. Nobody reads a talks page from top to bottom. If you want a sense of where topological field theory meets condensed matter, one abstract gives it. Pick whatever title makes you curious and enjoy that one. Coverage comes second, or not at all.

## The story

A research area is hard to enter through its papers. Each paper assumes the last. A talk abstract is different. The speaker has one paragraph to tell a mixed audience what the question is and why they care. This page collects such paragraphs for one mathematician, newest first. Each entry gives a title, a date, a venue, and often links to slides, notes or video. Read in order, the entries tell a story of their own. The early talks at Berkeley are expository: divergent series, the Yoneda embedding for enriched categories, order-of-magnitude physics. The recent ones report new work: gapless boundaries of quantum materials, unitary field theories, and what the author calls "deeper algebra". The page also lists conferences he helped to organise.

## Main definitions

**Talk entry.** One item on the page: a title, a date and a venue, with an abstract that you show or hide by a link. Many entries add slides, notes or a video.
- Repeats: the same title often appears at several venues, with the abstract rewritten for each audience.

**Functorial field theory (as an abstract describes it).** A representation of a category of bordisms. The 2026 Lausanne lecture series uses "dagger" structures on categories to decide which of these representations count as unitary.

**Topological insulator (as an abstract describes it).** A material whose bulk is gapped, meaning insulating, while every possible boundary carries gapless, meaning conducting, modes. A topological invariant protects the boundary behaviour.

**Invertible phase.** A gapped material of a very simple kind. One abstract describes it as having a unique ground state under periodic boundary conditions.

## Main results

**Claim (enforced gaplessness; 2026 colloquium and seminar abstracts).** Following an argument of Kitaev, the invertible phases in all dimensions form an Ω-spectrum. So each n-dimensional invertible phase gives a character on the n-th stable homotopy group of spheres. If that character is nontrivial, and is not one of six exceptions tied to the Kervaire invariants, then every boundary condition is gapless. The abstracts say this depends on semisimplicity assumptions that are not proved analytically, and that it is joint work in progress with David Reutter.
- Case 1: nontrivial character, not an exception. The phase is a topological insulator.
- Case 2: the six Kervaire cases. These do admit gapped topological boundary conditions.

**Claim (McNamara–Wang reconstruction; July 2026 abstract).** Unitary functorial field theories that are compact and unextended, and not necessarily topological, are determined up to isomorphism by their partition functions on closed manifolds. The abstract credits the theorem to Jacob McNamara and Zhencheng Wang. The talk explains their proof.

**Claim ("deeper algebra"; August 2026 abstract).** Higher algebra is the algebra of connective spectra. The abstract proposes a parallel subject, the algebra of coconnective categorical spectra, and says it organises topics from Galois theory to the classification of phases of matter. This is also joint work in progress with Reutter.

## Note, then ignore

- Conditional statements. The gaplessness result rests on hypotheses about gapped materials that the abstracts call reasonable and unproven. This matters for rigour and not for the story. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- "Gappable" or "gapped". The same talk title appears with both words at different venues. They point to the same result. Safe to skip.
- Work in progress. Several recent abstracts report results with no paper linked yet, so details may change. Safe to skip.
- Hidden abstracts. Abstracts sit behind show and hide links, so a page search may miss text until you open them. Safe to skip.
- The organiser list. The page opens with conferences he organised and did not speak at. It is there for the record. Safe to skip.
