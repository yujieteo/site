## Before you start

The paper mixes nineteenth-century invariant theory with Siegel modular forms, and its pages fill up with long Fourier expansions. You may feel a fear of being slow, because the formulas look like they must be checked line by line. They need not be, and it is fine to feel that pull and leave them. If you like seeing an abstract object turned into something a computer can print, this paper does exactly that. Read it for the pleasure of the dictionary. Finish it only if that pleasure lasts.

## The story

A genus 2 curve is y<sup>2</sup> = f(x) with f a sextic. So the moduli space M<sub>2</sub> has two descriptions: binary sextics up to change of variable, and, through the Torelli map, an open part of the moduli space A<sub>2</sub> of principally polarised abelian surfaces. Igusa used this in 1960 to match invariants of sextics with scalar-valued Siegel modular forms. People also want vector-valued forms, whose Fourier expansions are hard to compute. The authors extend Igusa's match: vector-valued forms correspond to covariants. The sextic f itself, the simplest covariant, corresponds to the meromorphic form χ<sub>6,3</sub>/χ<sub>5</sub>. Since every covariant is a polynomial expression in the coefficients of f, every vector-valued form comes from the coordinates of χ<sub>6,3</sub>, whose Fourier series is easy to compute from theta functions.

## Main definitions

**Vector-valued Siegel modular form of weight (j, k).** A holomorphic map F from the Siegel upper half space of degree 2 to Sym<sup>j</sup>(ℂ<sup>2</sup>) with F(γ·τ) = ρ(cτ + d) F(τ) for all γ in Γ<sub>2</sub> = Sp(4, ℤ), where ρ = Sym<sup>j</sup> ⊗ det<sup>k</sup>. Equivalently, a section of Sym<sup>j</sup>(E) ⊗ det(E)<sup>k</sup>, with E the Hodge bundle. The space is M<sub>j,k</sub>, and M is the sum over all (j, k).
- j = 0: scalar-valued forms. Igusa showed their ring R is generated by E<sub>4</sub>, E<sub>6</sub>, χ<sub>10</sub>, χ<sub>12</sub>, χ<sub>35</sub>.

**Covariant of binary sextics.** Let V be the standard 2-dimensional representation of GL(2, ℂ), so Sym<sup>6</sup>(V) is the space of binary sextics with coefficients a<sub>0</sub>, …, a<sub>6</sub>. A covariant is an equivariant copy of an irreducible representation A[λ<sub>1</sub>, λ<sub>2</sub>] inside Sym<sup>d</sup>(Sym<sup>6</sup>(V)). Concretely it is a form of degree d in the a<sub>i</sub> and of degree λ<sub>1</sub> − λ<sub>2</sub> in x<sub>1</sub>, x<sub>2</sub>.
- Degree in x equal to 0: an invariant. The ring I of invariants has five generators A, B, C, D, E.
- The sextic f itself is the covariant with d = 1. The Hessian of f is one with d = 2.

**The forms χ<sub>5</sub>, χ<sub>10</sub>, χ<sub>6,3</sub>, χ<sub>6,8</sub>.** χ<sub>5</sub> is, up to normalisation, the product of the ten even theta constants, a form with a character on Γ<sub>2</sub>, and χ<sub>10</sub> = χ<sub>5</sub><sup>2</sup>. χ<sub>6,3</sub> is a form of weight (6, 3) with character, built from the gradients of the six odd theta functions. χ<sub>6,8</sub> = χ<sub>6,3</sub> χ<sub>5</sub> is a cusp form of weight (6, 8).
- χ<sub>5</sub> vanishes to order 1 along A<sub>1,1</sub>, the locus of products of elliptic curves, and χ<sub>10</sub> to order 2.

## Main results

**Proposition (Hodge bundle on sextics; Corollary 4.1).** On the space of binary sextics with non-zero discriminant, the pullback of the Hodge bundle E is the equivariant bundle V ⊗ det(V).
- Step 1: the curve y<sup>2</sup> = f(x) comes with the basis x dx/y, dx/y of differentials.
- Step 2: a matrix A in GL(2, ℂ) acts on this basis by det(A)·A.

**Theorem (covariants sit between modular forms; Section 4).** There are inclusions M ⊂ C ⊂ M<sub>χ<sub>10</sub></sub>, where C is the ring of covariants and the right-hand side is M with χ<sub>10</sub> inverted. They extend Igusa's R ⊂ I ⊂ R<sub>χ<sub>10</sub></sub>. Every covariant defines a meromorphic Siegel modular form whose only possible poles lie along A<sub>1,1</sub>.

**Theorem (the universal form; Conclusion 6.1).** Under the map from C, the sextic f goes to χ<sub>6,3</sub>/χ<sub>5</sub>, of weight (6, −2), after normalising f. Let h be a covariant of degree d from A[p + q, q]. Substitute the coordinates α<sub>i</sub> of χ<sub>6,3</sub> for the a<sub>i</sub> in h. The result F<sub>h</sub> is a holomorphic form of weight (p, q + 3d). Every vector-valued modular form on Γ<sub>2</sub>, with or without character, arises this way after multiplying or dividing by a power of χ<sub>5</sub>.
- Step 1: the map sends h to F<sub>h</sub>/χ<sub>5</sub><sup>d</sup>, so its pole order along A<sub>1,1</sub> is at most 5d.
- Step 2: the Taylor expansions of χ<sub>6,3</sub> and χ<sub>5</sub> along τ<sub>12</sub> = 0 give the true order of F<sub>h</sub>, and so the largest power of χ<sub>5</sub> you may divide by.

## Note, then ignore

- Characters: χ<sub>5</sub> and χ<sub>6,3</sub> are forms with a character on Γ<sub>2</sub>, or forms on the level 2 subgroup invariant under A<sub>6</sub>. This only tracks a sign. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Degree normalisation: the degree of an invariant is its degree in the roots divided by 3. It matters only when you compare with older tables. Safe to skip.
- M is not finitely generated but C is: this is why covariants help, yet nothing in the story depends on the proof. Safe to skip.
- Quasi-modular forms: the Taylor coefficients of χ<sub>10</sub> along the product locus are quasi-modular, involving e<sub>2</sub>. This is needed only to compute vanishing orders. Safe to skip.
- Stable sextics and nodal curves: the dictionary extends to sextics with roots of multiplicity at most 2. It is there to reach the compactification. Safe to skip.
