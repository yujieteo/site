## Before you start

This is a short letter in particle physics, dense with two-component spinor indices and Standard Model charge tables. You may feel wary: it is a bold claim about the Big Bang, and you cannot easily judge it. That wariness is welcome, and you can keep it the whole way through. If you like symmetry arguments, here one reflection does a lot of work. Read it as a puzzle about what a mirror does to a spinor. You do not have to reach a verdict.

## The story

Quantum chromodynamics allows a term θGG̃ that violates CP symmetry. Experiment says its coefficient is tiny, and nobody knows why. This is the strong CP problem. The usual fix adds a new particle, the axion. Boyle, Teuscher and Turok try something else. In earlier papers they proposed that the radiation-filled universe extends through the Bang to a mirror image on the other side. Here they ask what boundary condition a mirror forces on spinors. A mirror in space can pair a spinor with its own charge conjugate. A mirror in time cannot. It must pair each left-handed spinor with a separate right-handed one. In a left-right symmetric extension of the Standard Model, demanding this mirror symmetry of the whole theory makes the Yukawa matrices Hermitian, and θ vanishes.

## Main definitions

**Dirac-type reflecting boundary condition.** At a mirror with unit normal n, a left-handed spinor φ and an independent right-handed spinor χ̄ satisfy n·σ χ̄ = ξφ, where ξ is a phase. Lorentz covariance fixes this form.

**Majorana-type reflecting boundary condition.** The special case χ̄ = φ̄, where φ̄ is the charge conjugate of φ. The field is reflected into itself.
- Example: massless spinors at the boundary of anti-de Sitter space.

**Left-right symmetric model (LRSM).** A gauge theory with group SU(3) × SU(2)<sub>L</sub> × SU(2)<sub>R</sub> × U(1)<sub>B−L</sub>. Every left-handed doublet has a right-handed partner, and there are two Higgs doublets h<sub>L</sub> and h<sub>R</sub>. When h<sub>R</sub> gets a large vacuum value, the model reduces to the Standard Model.

**Mirror CT symmetry.** An anti-linear transformation that reverses conformal time τ → −τ, applies charge conjugation, and swaps each field with its left-right partner.

## Main results

All results are physics arguments in a short letter. The paper has no numbered theorems, and the authors say they "argue" for the picture.

**Claim (physics argument; time mirrors need Dirac pairs; eqns. (4) to (7)).** For a field with m undotted and n dotted spinor indices, the Majorana-type condition is consistent only if (−n<sup>2</sup>)<sup>m+n</sup> = 1. So for fermions, where m + n is odd, it needs a spacelike normal. At the Bang the normal is timelike, so fermions must obey a Dirac-type condition.
- Step 1: write the Majorana-type condition and its complex conjugate.
- Step 2: compare the two. The phases match only under the stated sign condition.

**Claim (physics argument; the Standard Model allows it; eqns. (8) to (10)).** Contract the left-handed doublets with the unit Higgs doublet and its conjugate. The resulting left-handed fields u<sub>L</sub>, d<sub>L</sub>, ν<sub>L</sub>, e<sub>L</sub> transform under the gauge group exactly like u<sub>R</sub>, d<sub>R</sub>, ν<sub>R</sub>, e<sub>R</sub>. So Dirac-type conditions at the Bang respect gauge symmetry, provided a right-handed neutrino exists.

**Claim (physics argument; strong CP in the LRSM).** If the LRSM action is invariant under the mirror CT symmetry, the Yukawa matrices are Hermitian, Y = Y<sup>†</sup>. The θGG̃ term is forbidden, and with no overall Yukawa phase the chiral anomaly does not bring it back.
- Case T alone: it would also remove θ, but it forces real Yukawa matrices, which conflicts with observations.
- Classical side: solutions invariant under the matching linear time reversal satisfy the Dirac-type condition.

**Claim (physics argument; back to the Standard Model).** Define two-sheeted fields that equal h<sub>L</sub>, h<sub>R</sub> after the Bang and h<sub>R</sub>, h<sub>L</sub> before it. Sending the vacuum value of the second one to infinity respects the reflection and leaves the Standard Model. The authors conclude that the solution carries over to the Standard Model itself.

## Note, then ignore

- Signature and spinor conventions. The paper uses signature (+, −, −, −) and two-component notation from a cited textbook appendix. They fix signs only, so skip them at first.
- Conformal frame. Fields are rescaled by powers of the scale factor a(τ) so that the background looks static. It is bookkeeping, safe to skip.
- Even or odd a(τ). The CT rule for spinors has a γ<sup>5</sup> or a 1 depending on this. The argument works either way, so skip it.
- Weyl lift and Figure 1. A change of field variables shows the Higgs passes smoothly through the Bang and avoids an "antigravity" region. It supports the last claim only. Safe to skip.
- Unit Higgs doublet. It is undefined where h = 0, a set of measure zero. Safe to skip.
