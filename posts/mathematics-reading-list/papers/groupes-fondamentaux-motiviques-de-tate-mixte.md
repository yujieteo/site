## Before you start

This paper is 84 pages, in French, and it leans on Voevodsky's triangulated category of motives and on tannakian categories. The likely feeling is shame at missing the background. It is fine to feel that. The paper takes the hard inputs as black boxes too. If you have wondered why multiple zeta values are counted by 1/(1 − t<sup>2</sup> − t<sup>3</sup>), the reason is here. Enjoy that one idea. The other pages are optional.

## The story

Multiple zeta values satisfy many relations over ℚ. Zagier conjectured that those of weight n span a space of dimension d<sub>n</sub>, with Σ d<sub>n</sub>t<sup>n</sup> = 1/(1 − t<sup>2</sup> − t<sup>3</sup>). These numbers are iterated integrals on ℙ<sup>1</sup> minus 0, 1, ∞, so they are periods of its fundamental group. Deligne had given this group, made unipotent, compatible Hodge, de Rham and ℓ-adic realisations. Missing was one motive behind them, in a category small enough to count in. The paper defines mixed Tate motives over a ring of S-integers and computes the symmetry group of that category. Then it shows the fundamental group is an object there. An object cannot have more periods than the symmetry group allows. For ℤ this gives the upper half of Zagier's conjecture.

## Main definitions

**Mixed Tate motives MT(k).** Let k be a number field. Inside Voevodsky's triangulated category of motives with ℚ-coefficients, take the triangulated subcategory generated by the Tate objects ℚ(n). The Beilinson–Soulé vanishing conjecture holds for k, so this subcategory has a t-structure. Its heart MT(k) is the abelian category of iterated extensions of the ℚ(n). It is tannakian.
- Weight filtration: each object has a unique finite increasing filtration W, indexed by even integers, whose graded piece of weight −2n is a sum of copies of ℚ(n).

**Mixed Tate motives over O<sub>S</sub> (1.4, 1.6).** Let S be a set of finite places and O<sub>S</sub> the ring of S-integers. Recall Ext<sup>1</sup>(ℚ(0), ℚ(1)) = k<sup>×</sup> ⊗ ℚ. MT(O<sub>S</sub>) is the full subcategory of MT(k) of motives M such that every subquotient of M that is an extension of ℚ(n) by ℚ(n + 1) has class in O<sub>S</sub><sup>×</sup> ⊗ ℚ.
- Test (Proposition 1.7): M is unramified at a place v if and only if its ℓ-adic realisation is unramified at v, for ℓ prime to the residue characteristic.

**Motivic fundamental group.** For a variety X over k and base points x, y, Deligne's earlier work gives a system of realisations of the pro-unipotent completion of π<sub>1</sub>(X, x) and of the path torsors P<sub>y,x</sub>. "Motivic" means: this system is the realisation of a (pro-)object of mixed Artin–Tate motives, that is, motives that become mixed Tate over a finite extension of k.

## Main results

**Theorem (Ext groups and the motivic Galois group; 1.6, Propositions 1.9, 2.2, 2.3).** Let k be a number field. In MT(O<sub>S</sub>), Ext<sup>1</sup>(ℚ(0), ℚ(n)) is 0 for n ≤ 0, is O<sub>S</sub><sup>×</sup> ⊗ ℚ for n = 1, and is K<sub>2n−1</sub>(k) ⊗ ℚ for n ≥ 2. All Ext<sup>2</sup> vanish. For the fibre functor ω given by the weight-graded pieces, the tannakian group is 𝔾<sub>m</sub> ⋉ U<sub>ω</sub> with U<sub>ω</sub> pro-unipotent, and the graded Lie algebra of U<sub>ω</sub> is free.
- Step 1: the Ext groups come from Voevodsky's category and Borel's computation of K-theory of number fields.
- Step 2: vanishing of Ext<sup>2</sup> translates into freeness of the Lie algebra.

**Theorem (the fundamental group is motivic; Théorème 4.13, Proposition 4.17).** If X is a unirational variety over a number field k, the realisation system of P<sub>y,x</sub> comes from a mixed Artin–Tate motive over k. If X is the generic fibre of the complement of a relative normal crossings divisor in a smooth proper scheme over O<sub>S</sub>, with components absolutely irreducible on the generic fibre, and x, y extend to O<sub>S</sub>-points, then the motive lies in MT(O<sub>S</sub>).
- Step 1: the realisation functor is fully faithful and reflects subquotients. So it is enough to exhibit the system as a subquotient of something already known to be motivic.
- Step 2: base points "at infinity" (tangent vectors) work the same way (4.14, 4.18).

**Theorem (bound on periods; Théorème 5.24, Corollaire 5.25).** Let X be ℙ<sup>1</sup> minus 0, ∞ and the N-th roots of unity. Let D<sub>n</sub> be the dimension of the ℚ-span of the weight n coefficients of the straight path from 0 to 1. For N = 1 these coefficients are the multiple zeta values. Then Σ D<sub>n</sub>t<sup>n</sup> is bounded term by term by 1/(1 − t<sup>2</sup> − t<sup>3</sup>) for N = 1, and by 1/(1 − t − t<sup>2</sup>) for N = 2. A similar bound for N ≥ 3 depends on the Euler function φ(N) and the number of primes dividing N.
- Step 1: the coefficients are values, at one complex point, of functions on a variety built from U<sub>ω</sub> and one extra line (for π<sup>2</sup> or 2πi).
- Step 2: count graded functions on that variety using freeness of the Lie algebra.

## Note, then ignore

- "Artificial" definition. The authors call their definition of MT(O<sub>S</sub>) as a subcategory of MT(k) perhaps artificial but convenient. No category of motives over O<sub>S</sub> itself is built. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Characteristic zero. The cited results need resolution of singularities, so k has characteristic 0. Safe to skip.
- Upper bound only. The theorem bounds dimensions from above. Lower bounds would need transcendence results, which a version of Grothendieck's period conjecture predicts. Safe to skip.
- Terasoma. For N = 1 Terasoma (2002) also obtained the bound. Safe to skip.
