## Before you start

This is a 51-page paper that leans on p-adic Hodge theory, hyperfields and tropical geometry at once. The likely feeling is shame at not knowing the p-adic side it copies. That is a natural response to a paper built on an analogy with a hard subject, and nothing needs to be done about it. The real side is concrete: real numbers, a strange addition, and Laplace transforms. If you want p-adic period rings to feel less foreign, seeing them rebuilt over ℝ may help. Read the introduction for interest, and count the rest as optional.

## The story

Fontaine builds p-adic period rings in three steps. Take compatible p-th roots in a p-adic field to get a perfect field of characteristic p. Lift back to characteristic zero with Witt vectors. Complete. Connes and Consani ask what the same steps give for ℝ, where Galois theory offers nothing because ℝ has no automorphisms. Step one already breaks. The limit addition lim (x<sup>1/κ<sup>m</sup></sup> + y<sup>1/κ<sup>m</sup></sup>)<sup>κ<sup>m</sup></sup> picks the summand of larger absolute value, and it is not associative. The fix is Krasner's idea of a hyperfield: let x + (−x) be the whole interval [−x, x]. Then the three steps go through. The result is a field in place of the Witt vectors, a thickening of ℝ, and a one-parameter group of automorphisms F<sub>λ</sub> in place of Frobenius.

## Main definitions

**Hyperfield.** A structure like a field, except that the sum of two elements is a non-empty set of elements. It has characteristic one if x + x = x for all x.
- Typical source: a quotient K/G of a field by a subgroup G of its multiplicative group.

**Tropical real hyperfield ℝ<sup>♭</sup>.** The set ℝ with ordinary multiplication and this hyperaddition: x ⌣ y is the summand of larger absolute value, it is x if x = y, and it is the interval [−x, x] if y = −x. It was introduced by Viro.

**W-model.** For a hyperfield H, a W-model is a triple (K, ρ, τ): a field K, a homomorphism of hyperfields ρ : K → H, and a multiplicative section τ : H → K of ρ. A universal W-model is an initial one, written W(H).
- p-adic counterpart: K is the Witt ring, ρ the residue map and τ the Teichmüller lift.

**Universal thickening ℝ<sub>∞</sub> (Definition 4.1).** Let W<sub>ℚ</sub>(ℝ<sup>♭</sup>) be the ℚ-algebra spanned by the lifts [x] = τ(x), and θ : W<sub>ℚ</sub>(ℝ<sup>♭</sup>) → ℝ the ring map with θ([x]) = x. Then ℝ<sub>∞</sub> is the inverse limit of W<sub>ℚ</sub>(ℝ<sup>♭</sup>)/Ker(θ)<sup>n</sup>.

## Main results

**Theorem (perfection of ℝ is tropical; Theorem 2.1).** Fix a rational κ with odd numerator and denominator and |κ| &lt; 1. Let F be the set of real sequences with x<sub>n+1</sub><sup>κ</sup> = x<sub>n</sub>. Then x ↦ x<sub>0</sub> is a multiplicative bijection F → ℝ. The limit of the graphs of the conjugated additions defines a hyperfield structure on F, and it is ℝ<sup>♭</sup>.
- Step 1: the single-valued limit addition exists but fails associativity.
- Step 2: the graphs converge as closed sets, and over the line y = −x the limit fills the interval [−x, x].

**Theorem (properties of ℝ<sup>♭</sup>; introduction).** ℝ<sup>♭</sup> is a perfect hyperfield of characteristic one: x ↦ x<sup>n</sup> is an automorphism for odd n > 0. Its automorphism group is the positive reals, acting by θ<sub>λ</sub>.

**Theorem (universal W-model; Theorem 3.4).** The field W = Frac(ℚ[ℝ<sub>+</sub><sup>×</sup>]), the fraction field of the rational group ring of the positive reals, is the universal W-model of ℝ<sup>♭</sup>. The map ρ reads off the sign of the ratio of leading coefficients, and it identifies ℝ<sup>♭</sup> with W/G for G the kernel of ρ on W<sup>×</sup>.

**Theorem (size of the thickening; Theorems 4.2 and 4.5).** Each group homomorphism ℓ : ℝ<sub>+</sub><sup>×</sup> → ℝ gives a ring map ℝ<sub>∞</sub> → ℝ[[z − 1]], surjective if xℓ(x) + (1 − x)ℓ(1 − x) ≠ 0 for some x. The real vector space Ker(θ)/Ker(θ)<sup>2</sup> is infinite-dimensional, and the classes π<sub>p</sub> = [p] − p for primes p are linearly independent over ℝ.
- Presentation: Ker(θ)/Ker(θ)<sup>2</sup> is generated by symbols ε(x), x ∈ ℝ, with three functional relations, the same ones that govern the entropy function.

## Note, then ignore

- Choice of κ. The construction fixes κ, but the hyperfield does not depend on it. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The analytic half. Sections 5 and 6 build Banach and Fréchet algebras, identify one with finite real Borel measures on [0, ∞) under convolution (Theorem 5.3), and compute a Gelfand spectrum (Theorem 6.4). The story above does not need them. Safe to skip at first.
- The complex case. Section 7 repeats the construction for ℂ and links it to oscillatory integrals such as the Airy function. It is exploratory. Safe to skip.
- Earlier semiring version. Appendix E relates this paper to the authors' earlier Witt construction for semirings of characteristic one by the change of variable z = 1/T. Safe to skip.
- Two versions. This is arXiv version 2 from 2014, with theorems numbered by section. A 2015 version on Connes's site numbers them 1 to 10. Safe to ignore.
