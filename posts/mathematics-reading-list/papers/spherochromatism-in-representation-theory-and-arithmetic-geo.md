## Before you start

This is a Harvard thesis of about 280 pages that joins chromatic homotopy theory, geometric Langlands and prismatic cohomology. The likely feeling is shame at not knowing two or three of those fields. Very few readers know all of them, and you may feel that gap without owing anyone a remedy. If you want to see why a formal group keeps turning up in topology, Chapter 2 shows it in eight pages. Read that chapter, or the introduction, for the pleasure of the idea. The other 270 pages are for another day.

## The story

Cohomology with coefficients in a ring turns spaces into linear algebra. Ring spectra give more general coefficients, such as K-theory or the sphere spectrum S, but algebraic geometry over them is hard to write down. The thesis leans on one device from Hahn, Raksit and Wilson: the even filtration. It sends an E<sub>∞</sub>-ring k to an ordinary stack Spev(k), which carries a one-dimensional formal group. For the sphere, Spev(S) is the moduli stack of formal groups. Part I asks what happens to the derived geometric Satake equivalence when sheaves on the affine Grassmannian take coefficients in k. The proposed answer: the Langlands dual group stays the same and only a space built from the formal group changes. Part II applies the same device to topological Hochschild homology and prismatic cohomology.

## Main definitions

**Even ring.** An E<sub>∞</sub>-ring R is even if its odd homotopy groups π<sub>2n+1</sub>(R) all vanish. A map of even rings is a cover if it is faithfully flat on homotopy groups (Definition 2.1.1).

**Even stack Spev(A).** For an E<sub>∞</sub>-ring A, Spev(A) is the colimit, in fpqc stacks, of the quotients Spec(π<sub>\*</sub>B)/𝔾<sub>m</sub> over all maps A → B with B even. It comes with a map to B𝔾<sub>m</sub>, that is, a line bundle that records the grading (Definition 2.1.1).
- Evenly descendable: when A has a suitable even cover, a spectral sequence runs from the cohomology of Spev(A) to π<sub>\*</sub>(A) (Remark 2.1.6).

**The formal group H and the space G<sub>H</sub>.** The unit S → k gives a map Spev(k) → Spev(S), and so a one-dimensional formal group H over Spev(k). For a group scheme G, the H-loop space of BG is a quotient G<sub>H</sub>/G, and this defines G<sub>H</sub>.
- k an ordinary ring: H is additive and G<sub>H</sub> is the Lie algebra, completed at the nilpotent cone.
- k complex K-theory: H is multiplicative and G<sub>H</sub> is the group G, completed at the unipotent cone.

## Main results

**Claim (sphere; Example 2.2.1).** Spev(S) is the moduli stack M<sub>fg</sub> of one-dimensional formal groups. The map S → MU is an even cover, and the resulting spectral sequence is the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence for the homotopy groups of spheres.

**Conjecture (Satake with general coefficients; Conjecture 1.1.1, made precise as Conjecture 4.3.19).** Let G be a connected simply-laced complex reductive group with torsion-free fundamental group, and let k be a connective E<sub>∞</sub>-ring. Then the category of G(O)-equivariant sheaves of k-modules on the affine Grassmannian has a one-parameter degeneration into graded quasicoherent sheaves on G<sub>H</sub>/Ǧ, where Ǧ is the Langlands dual group acting by conjugation.

**Theorem (evidence; Theorem 1.1.2).** Take G as in the conjecture and k rational cohomology, complex or real K-theory, or an elliptic cohomology theory. Let F be an algebraically closed field with a map π<sub>0</sub>(k) → F. Then the subcategory of locally constant sheaves has a filtration over Spev(k) whose associated graded, after base change to F, is graded quasicoherent sheaves on an open part of G<sub>H</sub>/Ǧ with complement of codimension 2.
- A second part treats sheaves generated by convolutions of minuscule IC-sheaves, for types A, D, E<sub>6</sub> and E<sub>7</sub>, with conditions on F. The author says the proof does not cover E<sub>8</sub>.

**Theorem (THH of ℤ<sub>p</sub>, joint with A. Raksit; Theorem 1.2.1).** Fix a prime p > 2 and let j<sub>p</sub> be the connective cover of the K(1)-local sphere. There is a unique map of cyclotomic E<sub>∞</sub>-rings j<sub>p</sub> → THH(ℤ<sub>p</sub>). It identifies THH(ℤ<sub>p</sub>) with a Frobenius twist of j<sub>p</sub> and induces an equivalence from the S<sup>1</sup>-Tate construction of j<sub>p</sub> to TP(ℤ<sub>p</sub>).

## Note, then ignore

- Genuine equivariance. The theorems use genuine equivariant sheaves, a theory the author treats as a black box because it is not yet in the literature. It decides whether G<sub>H</sub> is completed. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Two gradings. The thesis uses π<sub>\*</sub> in the definition of Spev, while the joint paper with Hahn, Raksit and Yuan uses π<sub>2\*</sub> (Remark 2.1.2). Safe to skip.
- Simply-laced hypothesis. It lets Ǧ act on G by conjugation. The author says other types need folding of Dynkin diagrams and a less simple statement. Safe to skip.
- The prime 2. Theorem 1.2.1 fails for p = 2, for reasons of Nygaard completion. Safe to skip.
- The title. Spherochromatism is a term from optics for a lens that fails to focus all colours at one point. Here it is an analogy for how different chromatic layers of spectra give different answers. Safe to skip.
