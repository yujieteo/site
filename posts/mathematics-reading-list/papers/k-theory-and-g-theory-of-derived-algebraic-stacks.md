## Before you start

These are 53 pages of notes with roughly a hundred numbered statements, set on derived Artin stacks and citing Lurie, Hall and Rydh on almost every page. The likely feeling is boredom mixed with dread: a reference text in the most general setting on offer. Both are reasonable responses to a text like that. The notes are also the one place that lays K, K<sup>B</sup>, KH and G side by side and says when each behaves. If you have ever been unsure which K-theory a paper means, that table is here. Dip in where you are curious. There is no need to read in order.

## The story

There are several "K-theories" of a space, and papers often leave unsaid which one they use. Take perfect complexes and you get K, with pullbacks and products, like cohomology. Take coherent sheaves and you get G, with proper pushforwards and localisation sequences, like Borel–Moore homology. On a regular space the two agree. On a singular one they differ, and K loses homotopy invariance and negative K-groups appear. The notes go through the variants in turn, in the generality of derived stacks: K<sup>B</sup> to handle negative degrees, KH to force homotopy invariance, rational étale versions to gain descent. The payoff is the last section. A quasi-smooth derived stack has a coherent structure sheaf, so its class in G-theory is a fundamental class, and the virtual structure sheaf comes for free.

## Main definitions

**Perfect and coherent complexes.** On a derived Artin stack X, a quasi-coherent complex is perfect if, smooth-locally on affines, it is built from the structure sheaf by finite limits, colimits and summands. On a Noetherian X it is coherent if it has finitely generated homotopy sheaves, only finitely many of them non-zero.
- On a regular classical stack the two classes coincide.

**K and K<sup>B</sup>.** K(X) is the connective K-theory spectrum of the perfect complexes on X, and K<sup>B</sup>(X) is the non-connective Bass–Thomason–Trobaugh spectrum of the same category (Definition 2.6). K is the connective cover of K<sup>B</sup>.

**G-theory.** For Noetherian X, G(X) is the K-theory of the coherent complexes on X. It agrees with Quillen's K-theory of the abelian category of coherent sheaves (Proposition 3.3).

**KH.** For a quasi-compact quasi-separated derived algebraic space X, KH(X) is the geometric realisation of the simplicial spectrum n ↦ K<sup>B</sup>(X × Δ<sup>n</sup>), where Δ<sup>n</sup> is affine n-space arranged as a simplex. It is the universal homotopy invariant theory under K<sup>B</sup>.

## Main results

**Theorem (localisation; Theorems 2.9 and 3.9).** If X is absolutely perfect and Z is a cocompact closed subset, there is an exact triangle K<sup>B</sup>(X on Z) → K<sup>B</sup>(X) → K<sup>B</sup>(X ∖ Z). For a closed immersion Z → X of Noetherian derived Artin stacks with open complement U, there is an exact triangle G(Z) → G(X) → G(U).
- For K<sup>B</sup>: the first term is K-theory with supports, not K-theory of Z.
- For G: this is Quillen's dévissage and localisation, so the first term is G of Z itself.

**Theorem (G ignores derived and nilpotent structure; Corollaries 3.4 and 3.10).** For Noetherian X, pushforward from the classical truncation gives an equivalence G(X<sub>cl</sub>) ≃ G(X). Any surjective closed immersion of Noetherian derived Artin stacks induces an equivalence on G-theory. Neither statement holds for higher K-groups of perfect complexes (Remark 2.25).

**Theorem (comparison; Theorem 3.5 and Remark 4.5).** Let X be Noetherian with bounded structure sheaf. Then perfect complexes are coherent and there are maps K(X) → K<sup>B</sup>(X) → G(X). If X is regular they are equivalences. For a regular algebraic space, K → K<sup>B</sup> → KH → G are all equivalences.
- Step 1: on a regular X every coherent complex is perfect.
- Step 2: K<sup>B</sup> of a category with a bounded t-structure and Noetherian heart is connective.

**Theorem (homotopy invariance and descent; Corollary 3.18, Proposition 4.8, Theorem 5.1).** For a vector bundle E → X, pullback is an equivalence on G-theory (X Noetherian) and on KH (X a derived algebraic space). It can fail for K<sup>B</sup> on singular X. Rationally, on derived algebraic spaces, K<sup>B</sup> satisfies étale descent, and KH and G satisfy fppf descent.

## Note, then ignore

- "Perfect stack". This means the quasi-coherent derived category is compactly generated by perfect complexes. Algebraic spaces and stacks with "nice" stabilisers qualify (Theorem 1.40). Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Finite cohomological dimension. Pushforward along a non-representable map needs this hypothesis, which holds for example for classifying stacks in characteristic zero. Safe to skip.
- Noetherian hypotheses on G. The author does not know whether Proposition 3.1 extends beyond the Noetherian case (Remark 3.2). Safe to skip.
- Étale versions on stacks. For stacks, rational étale K-theory and G-theory do not agree with K and G even rationally, and their pushforwards differ (Warning 5.10). Safe to skip.
- Weibel's conjecture. Negative K-groups vanish below minus the Krull dimension (Theorem 4.1, after Kerz, Strunk and Tamme for schemes). Safe to skip.
