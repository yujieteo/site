## Before you start

This note is eight pages and reads like an announcement. Almost every line leans on something large: Simpson's harmonic bundles, A<sub>∞</sub>-categories, Saito's Hodge modules, Hochschild and cyclic homology. The likely feeling is shame at how much background seems to be assumed, with no proofs to hold on to. Nothing is wrong with you for feeling that. The note is a sketch and it is fine to read it as one. If you have wondered why Hodge theory and Galois theory look so alike, this states one precise guess. Read it as a map, for the view.

## The story

Étale cohomology of a variety over ℚ carries an action of the Galois group of ℚ, and the group acts on the whole category of sheaves, not only on cohomology. Hodge theory has a parallel object, the Hodge Galois group, defined as the symmetry group of the category of real mixed Hodge structures. But there is no "Hodge site", so the group has nothing geometric to act on. Goncharov proposes to fill the gap. A close relative, the twistor Galois group, should act on complexes of sheaves on a smooth projective complex variety X. He builds the action on local systems. The torus part is Simpson's ℂ<sup>\*</sup>-action. The unipotent part is given by numbers called Hodge correlators: integrals of Green currents placed on the edges of plane trivalent trees, as in a Feynman diagram.

## Main definitions

**Hodge Galois group.** The real algebraic group G<sub>Hod</sub> of tensor automorphisms of the fibre functor H ↦ ⊕ gr<sup>W</sup><sub>n</sub>H on real mixed Hodge structures. It is a semidirect product of a unipotent group U<sub>Hod</sub> and the torus ℂ<sup>\*</sup><sub>ℂ/ℝ</sub>, whose complex points are ℂ<sup>\*</sup> × ℂ<sup>\*</sup>.
- Generators: the complexified Lie algebra of U<sub>Hod</sub> has generators G<sub>p,q</sub> for p, q ≥ 1 with the single relation G<sub>p,q</sub> = −G<sub>q,p</sub>. Their sum is one operator G.

**Twistor Galois group.** The subgroup G<sub>T</sub> of G<sub>Hod</sub> that keeps U<sub>Hod</sub> but replaces the torus by the diagonal ℂ<sup>\*</sup>. Its representations are Simpson's mixed twistor structures (Lemma 1.1).

**Category Har<sub>X</sub>.** The graded category whose objects are semisimple local systems on X and their shifts, with Hom(V<sub>1</sub>, V<sub>2</sub>) = H<sup>•</sup>(X, V<sub>1</sub><sup>∨</sup> ⊗ V<sub>2</sub>). Its triangulated envelope is the category of complexes whose cohomology sheaves are local systems.

**Green current.** For a harmonic bundle V on X of complex dimension n, a current G(V; x, y) on X × X of degree 2n − 2 that solves (2πi)<sup>−1</sup> D″D′ G = δ<sub>Δ</sub> − P<sub>Har</sub>. Here δ<sub>Δ</sub> is the delta function of the diagonal and P<sub>Har</sub> is the kernel of the projection onto chosen harmonic forms.

## Main results

**Theorem (action on harmonic bundles; Theorems 2.1 and 2.3).** For a smooth projective complex variety X, the group G<sub>T</sub> acts up to homotopy on Har<sub>X</sub> by A<sub>∞</sub>-equivalences. The subgroup ℂ<sup>\*</sup> acts by Simpson's action on semisimple local systems. The Lie algebra of U<sub>Hod</sub> acts through a map determined by sending G to a class built from the Hodge correlator map. The action is compatible with pull-backs. The note outlines the construction and gives no full proof.
- Step 1: define a linear map Cor from the degree 0 cyclic homology of Har<sub>X</sub>, tensored with H<sub>2n</sub>(X)[−2], to ℂ.
- Step 2: a pairing on Hom spaces turns such a functional into a class in degree 0 Hochschild cohomology, and such classes are A<sub>∞</sub>-functors up to homotopy (Lemma 2.2).

**Theorem (action on smooth complexes; Theorem 1.3).** G<sub>T</sub> acts up to homotopy by A<sub>∞</sub>-equivalences on an A<sub>∞</sub>-enhancement of the derived category of complexes of sheaves on X whose cohomology sheaves are local systems.
- Why: this category is generated by semisimple local systems, so the action on Har<sub>X</sub> extends by functoriality.

**Theorem (correlators are well defined; Theorem 2.5).** Decorate the sides of a polygon with harmonic classes in H<sup>•</sup>(X, V<sub>i</sub><sup>\*</sup> ⊗ V<sub>i+1</sub>). For each plane trivalent tree dual to a triangulation, put Green currents on internal edges and the classes on external edges, apply an operator ξ, take traces at vertices and integrate over one copy of X per internal vertex. The sum over trees depends on choices, but on cycles it does not, and it kills boundaries.

**Conjecture (Conjecture 1.2).** G<sub>T</sub> acts up to homotopy on an A<sub>∞</sub>-enhancement of the derived category of perverse sheaves on X, and the equivariant objects form Saito's category of real mixed Hodge sheaves.

## Note, then ignore

- Two sets of generators. Goncharov's G<sub>p,q</sub> differ from Deligne's generators. He prefers his because they behave well in families. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The ℂ<sup>\*</sup>-action is not algebraic. Simpson's action on local systems is not an algebraic group action. This is stated and not explored. Safe to skip.
- Base point. For the trivial local system the Green current depends on a point a in X, used to represent the fundamental class. Safe to skip.
- Signs. The signs in the tree sums are fixed as in the earlier paper "Hodge correlators II". Safe to skip.
- Arithmetic remark. For the universal modular curve, the simplest correlators are said to be Rankin-Selberg integrals for non-critical L-values of modular forms. The note gives no details. Safe to skip.
