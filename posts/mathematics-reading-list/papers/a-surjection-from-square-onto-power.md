## Before you start

The paper is seven pages and proves one statement. The likely feeling is a quiet worry about the words "permutation model" and "consistent with ZF": the sense that set theory without choice is a foreign country with its own customs. That worry is reasonable, and nothing here asks you to get over it. If you have ever wanted to see what Cantor's diagonal argument does not rule out, this is a clean specimen. Read it as you would a good puzzle. Following every transfinite recursion can wait.

## The story

Cantor proved that no set A maps onto its power set P(A). Specker proved in ZF, without choice, that for infinite A the power set P(A) does not inject into A<sup>2</sup>. With choice this is trivial, since A<sup>2</sup> and A are then in bijection. The dual question stayed open since Truss asked it in 1973: can ZF prove that A<sup>2</sup> never maps onto P(A) for infinite A? Peng and Shen had shown that ω × A never maps onto P(A). Here the three authors answer no to the full question. They build a set A with so much symmetry that it has very few subsets in the model: each subset is determined by a small amount of data. A pairing function h on two-element subsets of A is then rich enough to list them all.

## Main definitions

**Permutation model.** Start with set theory with atoms (ZFA). Let A be the set of atoms, G a group of permutations of A, and F a normal filter of subgroups of G. A set x belongs to the model when all its elements do and its stabiliser sym<sub>G</sub>(x) lies in F. Such a model satisfies ZFA but usually not choice.
- Transfer: the Jech–Sochor theorem turns statements of this kind about a permutation model into consistency with ZF.

**The structure (A, h).** A = ω<sub>ω</sub>, cut into blocks ω<sub>n+1</sub> \ ω<sub>n</sub>. For each n, h<sub>n</sub> is a surjection from the two-element subsets of the n-th block onto P(ω<sub>n</sub>) × P(ω), and h is the union of the h<sub>n</sub>.
- Extension property (Lemma 3.1): for every injection f from an ordinal α &lt; ω<sub>n+1</sub> into the block and every assignment g of values, some new point β of the block has h<sub>n</sub>({β, f(γ)}) = g(γ) for all γ &lt; α.

**The group and filter.** G is the group of permutations of A that preserve every ω<sub>n</sub> and commute with h in the evident sense. F is generated by the subgroups G<sub>n</sub> that fix ω<sub>n</sub> pointwise.

## Main results

**Theorem (pairs onto power set; Theorem 3.6).** It is consistent with ZF that there is an infinite set A and a surjection from [A]<sup>2</sup>, the set of two-element subsets of A, onto P(A).
- Step 1 (Lemmas 3.2 to 3.4): a back-and-forth argument extends every small partial automorphism to a full one. So in the model every subset C of A, from some block m onwards, either contains a whole block or misses it.
- Step 2 (Lemma 3.5): a subset is therefore coded by a subset B of some ω<sub>n</sub> and a set r of natural numbers that says which later blocks are included. The map Φ({ξ, η}) = B ∪ (the blocks named by r), where h({ξ, η}) = (B, r), is onto P(A).

**Corollary (dual Specker problem; Corollary 3.7).** It is consistent with ZF that there is an infinite set A with a surjection from A<sup>2</sup> onto P(A). This follows because A<sup>2</sup> maps onto [A]<sup>2</sup>.

**Theorem (background, quoted in the introduction; no number).** In ZF, for every infinite set A: there is no injection from P(A) into A<sup>2</sup> (Specker), and there is no surjection from ω × A onto P(A) (Peng and Shen). The new result shows the second cannot be improved from ω × A to A × A.

## Note, then ignore

- Atoms. The construction lives in ZFA, not ZF. The Jech–Sochor theorem moves the result to ZF, and the paper cites it without proof. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- GCH below ℵ<sub>ω</sub>. The authors work in ZFA with choice and 2<sup>ℵ<sub>n</sub></sup> = ℵ<sub>n+1</sub> for all n. This keeps the recursive construction of h<sub>n</sub> at size ℵ<sub>n+1</sub>. It is an assumption on the outer universe only. Safe to skip.
- [A]<sup>2</sup> versus A<sup>2</sup>. The theorem is proved for unordered pairs, which is the stronger statement. Safe to skip.
- Consistency, not existence. The result does not exhibit such a set in ordinary mathematics. With choice no such A exists, by Cantor's theorem and the bijection between A<sup>2</sup> and A. Safe to skip once noted.
- Regularity of ω<sub>n+1</sub>. It is used once, so that the range of f lands in an earlier stage of the recursion. Safe to skip.
