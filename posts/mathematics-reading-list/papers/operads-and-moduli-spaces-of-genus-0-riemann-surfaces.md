## Before you start

Two vocabularies meet in 24 pages: operads with their bar constructions, and mixed Hodge theory. You may fear being too slow in one of them. That fear can stay while you read. Getzler recalls the operad definitions himself, and uses Hodge theory mostly as one fact about weights. If you want to know why quantum cohomology has the algebraic structure it has, this is a short answer. Follow the Com and Lie analogy and enjoy it. The symmetric function formulas at the end are optional.

## The story

Commutative and Lie algebras are dual. The Harrison complex of a free commutative algebra and the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of a free Lie algebra both collapse to the generators. Dijkgraaf, Verlinde and Verlinde found a richer structure in physics, with one product for each number n ≥ 2 of inputs. Kontsevich and Manin saw it in quantum cohomology and tied it to the compactified moduli spaces M̄<sub>0,n</sub> of pointed genus 0 curves. Getzler asks what its dual is. The answer sits next door: the open moduli spaces M<sub>0,n</sub>. Gluing curves at marked points makes the homology of M̄<sub>0,n</sub> an operad. Residue maps along boundary strata make the homology of M<sub>0,n</sub> an operad. The boundary stratification, with purity of the Hodge structures, shows that each is the dual of the other.

## Main definitions

**Cyclic operad.** An operad is a collection of chain complexes A(n) with S<sub>n</sub>-actions and composition maps, modelling operations with n inputs and one output. It is cyclic if the S<sub>n</sub>-action on A(n) extends to S<sub>n+1</sub>, so the output can trade places with an input. Write A((n)) = A(n − 1).
- Stable: A((n)) = 0 for n &lt; 3.

**Hypercommutative operad Hycom.** Hycom((n)) = H<sub>•</sub>(M̄<sub>0,n</sub>) for n ≥ 3, with composition induced by gluing two stable curves at a marked point. An algebra over it has graded symmetric products (x<sub>1</sub>, …, x<sub>n</sub>) of degree 2(n − 2) satisfying a generalised associativity relation.
- The product (x<sub>1</sub>, …, x<sub>n</sub>) is the fundamental class of M̄<sub>0,n+1</sub>.

**Gravity operad Grav.** Grav((n)) = Σ<sup>3−n</sup> sgn<sub>n</sub> ⊗ H<sub>•</sub>(M<sub>0,n</sub>) for n ≥ 3. Composition is the adjoint of the Poincaré residue map for a boundary stratum M<sub>0,m+1</sub> × M<sub>0,n+1</sub> of the compactification. An algebra over it has graded antisymmetric brackets [x<sub>1</sub>, …, x<sub>n</sub>] of degree 2 − n satisfying generalised Jacobi relations.
- The bracket is the class of a point in H<sub>0</sub>(M<sub>0,n+1</sub>).

**Cobar construction and Koszul operad.** The cobar construction B sends an operad A to the free operad on the dual of A with a differential built from the compositions of A. A quadratic operad A has a quadratic dual A<sup>!</sup> and a natural map BA → A<sup>!</sup>. A is Koszul if this map is a homotopy equivalence (Definition 4.10).

## Main results

All complexes are over ℂ.

**Theorem (strata span; Theorem 3.3, due to Keel).** The cycles of the closed strata M̄((T)), indexed by trees T with n legs, span H<sub>•</sub>(M̄<sub>0,n</sub>). Getzler gives a new proof by mixed Hodge theory.
- Step 1: H<sup>k</sup>(M<sub>0,n</sub>) is pure of weight 2k (Lemma 3.12), because it injects into the cohomology of a configuration space generated by logarithmic forms.
- Step 2: Deligne's spectral sequence for M<sub>0,n</sub> ⊂ M̄<sub>0,n</sub> then collapses to exact sequences, and odd cohomology of M̄<sub>0,n</sub> vanishes.

**Theorem (duality; Theorem 4.6).** There is a natural homotopy equivalence of operads B Hycom → Grav.
- Step 1: sum the exact sequences from the spectral sequence over all n.
- Step 2: by Poincaré duality, recognise the result as the cobar complex of Hycom with homology Grav.

**Theorem (Koszulness; Theorem 4.13, joint with Kontsevich).** Hycom is Koszul. It is generated by the fundamental classes [M̄<sub>0,n</sub>], and Hycom<sup>!</sup> ≅ Grav. So Hycom-algebras are exactly hypercommutative algebras, and the relations of Kontsevich and Manin follow.
- Step 1: Grav is quadratic, by Getzler's earlier work.
- Step 2: the equivalence of Theorem 4.6 respects Hodge weights, and weights force the homology of B Grav into the right degrees.

**Theorem (characters; Theorem 5.9).** The generating functions for the S<sub>n</sub>-characters of H<sub>•</sub>(M̄<sub>0,n</sub>) and of H<sub>•</sub>(M<sub>0,n</sub>) are Legendre transforms of each other as symmetric functions, up to signs and shifts. Forgetting the group action recovers the Poincaré polynomials found by Fulton and MacPherson.

## Note, then ignore

- Non-unital algebras. Commutative algebras here have no identity element, so Com(0) = 0. This keeps the operads stable. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Suspensions Σ and Λ. The shifts and sign representations in Grav make the brackets land in the stated degrees. Safe to skip.
- Signs ±. All signs follow the Quillen convention and equal +1 on even-degree elements. Safe to skip.
- Characteristic zero. The whole paper works over a field of characteristic zero, in fact over ℂ. Safe to skip.
- Higher genus. Getzler says purity is central and that any extension to higher genus will be subtle. Safe to skip.
