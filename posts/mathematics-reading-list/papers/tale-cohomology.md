## The story

Weil conjectured that the zeta function of a variety over a finite field is rational, has a functional equation, and has zeros and poles of prescribed absolute values. He saw that a cohomology theory with a fixed-point formula would give the first two. The block: the Zariski topology is too coarse to give such a theory. Grothendieck's idea was to change what "open set" means, and allow étale maps as open sets. The course follows that plan in order. It builds sites and sheaves, computes the cohomology of curves, proves the base change and duality theorems, and reaches the Lefschetz fixed point formula. That gives rationality. The last four lectures sketch Deligne's proof of the Riemann hypothesis through Lefschetz pencils. The page states these topics only. The mathematics is in the linked notes.

## Main definitions

The page gives no definitions. These are the standard meanings of the terms in its lecture titles.

**Site.** A category with a chosen notion of covering family, so that sheaves and their cohomology make sense on it (Lectures 2 and 3).
- Étale site of a scheme X: the objects are étale maps U → X, and the coverings are jointly surjective families of étale maps.

**Étale cohomology.** The derived functors of global sections on sheaves on the étale site (Lecture 7 sets up derived functor and Čech cohomology).
- With torsion coefficients ℤ/ℓ, ℓ invertible on X, it behaves like singular cohomology.
- ℤ<sub>ℓ</sub>-sheaves are inverse systems of these (Lecture 19).

**Weil zeta function.** For a variety X over 𝔽<sub>q</sub>, the series exp(Σ |X(𝔽<sub>q<sup>m</sup></sub>)| t<sup>m</sup>/m). The Weil conjectures are statements about it (Lecture 1).

## Main results

The page lists topics, not statements. The items below name what the course reaches, in the page's own order. The hypotheses are the standard ones and were not checked against the lecture notes.

**Claim (cohomology of curves; Lectures 10 to 13).** The course computes the étale cohomology of curves. The listed steps are the Kummer sequence, Hilbert's Theorem 90, Brauer groups and Tsen's theorem.
- Step 1: Hilbert 90 identifies H<sup>1</sup> of the multiplicative group with the Picard group.
- Step 2: Tsen's theorem makes the Brauer group contribution vanish.

**Claim (the main theorems; Lectures 14 to 23).** The course covers proper base change, purity and Gysin sequences, Artin comparison with singular cohomology, smooth base change, the Künneth formula and Poincaré duality.

**Claim (rationality; Lectures 23 and 24).** The Lefschetz fixed point formula gives rationality of the Weil zeta function, and the course then treats integrality.
- Step 1: points over 𝔽<sub>q<sup>m</sup></sub> are fixed points of the m-th power of Frobenius.
- Step 2: the fixed-point formula writes their number as an alternating sum of traces on cohomology.

**Claim (Riemann hypothesis; Lectures 24 to 30).** The course sketches the proof of the Riemann hypothesis for varieties over finite fields. The listed ingredients are the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula, a "Main Lemma", Lefschetz pencils, vanishing cycles and the Kazhdan-Margulis theorem. The last lecture mentions Weil II and applications.

## Note, then ignore

- Archived course. The page says the dates and administrative details are historical, from autumn 2020. They do not affect the content. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Four reference texts. The page lists Milne's lecture notes, Milne's book, Freitag-Kiehl and Katz's lectures. You need none of them to start a video. Safe to skip.
- Fppf descent (Lectures 4 and 5). This is technical groundwork for sheaves and torsors. It supports later proofs but not the story. Safe to skip.
- Lecture 7 has two videos. This is a recording detail. Safe to skip.
- Proofs marked as sketches. Purity and the Riemann hypothesis are listed as sketches, so do not expect every detail in the notes. Safe to skip.
