## Before you start

Sixty-six pages, two parts, and a vocabulary of tt-categories, 2-rings and E<sub>n</sub>-algebras. The feeling this one tends to raise is overwhelm, with a little worry about being too slow for it. Both are fair reactions to the size of the thing. You may want to know what a "point" of a category should be, in the way a field gives a point of a scheme. The introduction answers that in seven pages. Read those for the pleasure of the question. The rest can stay closed.

## The story

Algebraic geometry sees a prime ideal of a ring as a map to a field. Tensor triangular geometry copies the first half: a tt-category C has a Balmer spectrum Spc(C) of prime ideals. The second half is missing. Nobody has a good notion of "tt-field" that realises every prime by a functor C → F. Balmer's substitute is the homological spectrum Spc<sup>h</sup>(C), built from abelian residue fields, with a surjection onto Spc(C). The Nerves of Steel conjecture said this surjection is always a bijection. The authors disprove it with a free example: the free rigid 2-ring on a pointed object. Then they rebuild. Using the Nullstellensatzian objects of Burklund, Schlank and Yuan, they define constructible spectra and show that every homological prime is hit by a functor to a category with one homological point.

## Main definitions

**Balmer spectrum (Definition 2.1).** For an essentially small tt-category C, Spc(C) is the set of prime ⊗-ideals. The closed sets are generated by the supports Supp(x) = {P : x ∉ P}.

**Homological spectrum (Definition 2.9).** Let mod(C) be the abelian category of finitely presented additive presheaves on C. Spc<sup>h</sup>(C) is the set of maximal Serre ⊗-ideals of mod(C). There is a natural continuous surjection φ: Spc<sup>h</sup>(C) → Spc(C).
- Nerves of Steel condition (Definition 2.22): φ is a bijection.

**Nullstellensatzian object (Definition 6.2).** An object R of a presentable category A is Nullstellensatzian if it is non-zero and every compact object under R is either zero or admits a map back to R.

**Constructible spectrum (Definition 6.5).** For a rigid 2-ring C, the points of Spec<sup>cons</sup>(C) are maps C → S with S a Nullstellensatzian rigid 2-ring, where two maps are identified when the pushout of S and S′ over C is non-zero. It is a compact T<sub>1</sub> space.
- E<sub>n</sub> variant: use Nullstellensatzian E<sub>n</sub>-algebras under the unit of Ind(C) instead.

## Main results

**Theorem (Nerves of Steel fails; Theorem A, proved as Theorem 5.7).** Let A<sup>1,+</sup> be the free rigid commutative 2-ring on a pointed object, taken over the rationals. For its localisation at a certain generic point η, the map φ is not injective: the homological spectrum is strictly larger than the Balmer spectrum.
- Step 1: the cobordism hypothesis in dimension one describes A<sup>1</sup> and A<sup>1,+</sup> by generators and relations, and links A<sup>1</sup> to Deligne's Rep(GL<sub>t</sub>).
- Step 2: the comparison functor A<sup>1,+</sup><sub>η</sub> → A<sup>1</sup><sub>η</sub> is conservative with target a semisimple tt-field (Theorem D), so the exact-nilpotence condition fails for the universal fibre sequence Y → 1 → X.

**Theorem (rational case; Theorem E, proved as Theorem 7.1).** For a rational rigid commutative 2-ring C there is a natural bijection Spec<sup>cons</sup>(C) ≃ Spc<sup>h</sup>(C).
- Consequence (Theorem B, Corollary 7.11): for a rational E<sub>∞</sub>-ring R, Perf(R) has enough tt-fields, and every homological point comes from a map from R to a rational 2-periodic field.

**Theorem (E<sub>n</sub>-constructible spectrum; Theorem F, proved as Theorem 8.2).** Let C be a rigid E<sub>m</sub>-2-ring. For every 1 ≤ n &lt; m there is a natural bijection between the E<sub>n</sub>-constructible spectrum of the unit and Spc<sup>h</sup>(C). An example (Theorem 8.17) shows that n &lt; m cannot be relaxed.

**Theorem (geometric points; Theorem C).** Let C be a rigid tt-category that admits an enhancement, and let m be a homological prime. There is a tt-functor C → K to a rigid tt-category K such that Spc<sup>h</sup>(K) is one point and its image in Spc<sup>h</sup>(C) is exactly {m}.
- Step 1: build E<sub>n</sub>-algebra versions of Balmer's weak rings E<sub>m</sub>, using Burklund's work on multiplicative structures on quotients.
- Step 2: show that perfect modules over a high enough such algebra have a one-point homological spectrum (Section 9).

## Note, then ignore

- "Rational" as a standing assumption. In Part I the free 2-rings are taken over D<sup>b</sup>(ℚ) to make life easier. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Two older definitions of tt-field. Proposition 2.31 shows that the definitions of Balmer and of Balmer, Krause and Stevenson differ. Safe to skip.
- The map ψ to Spc(C). In general the E<sub>∞</sub>-constructible spectrum does not surject onto the Balmer spectrum (Corollary 6.11, Example 6.12). This is why E<sub>n</sub> enters. Safe to skip.
- Not yet fields. The authors warn that the categories K in Theorem C are not well understood and do not yet give a full theory of tt-fields. Safe to skip.
- Conventions. From Section 1.D on, "tt-category" means rigid and "2-ring" means commutative. Safe to skip.
