## Before you start

These are 57 talk slides with large type. The likely feeling is suspicion: "ten of this, ten of that" can sound like numerology, and you may worry there is no real mathematics underneath. You can keep that suspicion while you read. If you want one picture that ties Grassmannians, complex structures and Bott periodicity together, the first half of the slides gives it. Enjoy the examples first. The category theory at the end is optional.

## The story

Cartan found ten infinite families of compact symmetric spaces in 1926: three of groups, three of Grassmannians, and four others such as O(2n)/U(n). The number ten also counts the kinds of real and complex Clifford algebras, up to Bott periodicity. Baez says this is no coincidence. Take a real Hilbert space H and ask how many ways make it complex. A complex structure is an orthogonal J with J² = −1. The group O(2n) moves any such J to any other, and those fixing a given J form U(n). So the set of answers is O(2n)/U(n). The general rule: take a representation of the Clifford algebra with n − 1 generators, and form the space of ways to extend it to n generators. You get a symmetric space each time.

## Main definitions

**Symmetric space.** A connected Riemannian manifold M such that each point p has an isometry f : M → M with f(p) = p and derivative −1 at p. Spheres and Grassmannians are examples.

**Clifford algebra.** The real algebra Cliff<sub>n</sub> is freely generated by n anticommuting square roots of −1. The complex one is the same over ℂ. Each is a \*-algebra with e<sub>i</sub>\* = −e<sub>i</sub>.
- Real case: Cliff<sub>n+8</sub> is 16 × 16 matrices over Cliff<sub>n</sub>, which gives 8 kinds.
- Complex case: adding 2 generators gives 2 × 2 matrices, which gives 2 kinds.

**\*-representation.** A unital algebra map ρ from a \*-algebra A to operators on a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space with ρ(a\*) = ρ(a)<sup>†</sup>. Rep(A) is the category of these, with orthogonal equivalences as morphisms.

**Essential fibre.** For a functor F : 𝒳 → ℋ and an object H of ℋ, the category of pairs (X, α) with α an isomorphism F(X) → H. When F is faithful and conservative, it is equivalent to a set, written F<sup>−1</sup>(H).

## Main results

**Claim (fibres are quotients; Theorem 1).** Let F : Rep(Cliff<sub>n</sub>) → Rep(Cliff<sub>n−1</sub>) be restriction. For each H, the set F<sup>−1</sup>(H) is the disjoint union of the quotients Aut(H)/Aut(X), over one X from each isomorphism class with F(X) ≅ H. The same holds for complex Clifford algebras.
- Step 1: F is faithful and conservative, so the essential fibre is a set.
- Step 2: Aut(H) acts on that set, and each orbit is Aut(H) modulo a stabiliser Aut(X).

**Claim (quotients are symmetric; Theorem 2).** Each component Aut(H)/Aut(X) is a compact symmetric space.
- Step 1: let i be the action on X of the last generator e<sub>n</sub>. Then σ(g) = i g i<sup>−1</sup> is an involution of Aut(H).
- Step 2: Aut(X) is exactly the fixed subgroup of σ, and a compact group modulo the fixed points of an involution is a symmetric space.

**Claim (all ten appear).** Every one of Cartan's ten infinite series arises this way. Baez says the only proof so far is to check the list.
- Example: Cliff<sub>0</sub> to Cliff<sub>1</sub> gives O(2n)/U(n), and Cliff<sub>1</sub> to Cliff<sub>2</sub> gives U(2n)/Sp(n).
- Example: Cliff<sub>2</sub> to Cliff<sub>3</sub> gives the disjoint union of quaternionic Grassmannians Sp(n)/(Sp(d) × Sp(n − d)).

## Note, then ignore

- Disconnected fibres. A fibre can be a finite union of symmetric spaces, or empty when the dimension is odd. The definition of symmetric space asks for connected, so read the claim component by component. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Morita equivalence. Matrix algebras over A have the same \*-representations as A. That is why Cliff<sub>4</sub>, 2 × 2 quaternionic matrices, behaves like the quaternions. Safe to skip.
- A slip on the slides. The slide for Theorem 2 writes the quotient upside down, and two examples cite "Theorem 2" where Theorem 1 is meant. Safe to skip.
- The fixed-point fact. The slide quotes it for compact simple groups, while O(n) and U(n) are not simple. Details are left out. Safe to skip.
- Physics. The opening slide mentions Hamiltonians with time reversal and charge conjugation symmetry. Nothing later uses it. Safe to skip.
