## Before you start

These notes run to about seventy pages and touch valuations, topology, Milnor K-theory and schemes. The likely feeling is overwhelm: too many subjects in one file. You can let that be there. The author says plainly that Sections 3, 4 and 5 are largely independent, and that the proofs in Section 7 can be ignored on a first pass. If you want to see what "local" means on a surface, where a point alone is not enough, this is a friendly place. Read the part that amuses you. Finishing is optional.

## The story

On a curve or a number ring, you study a point by completing there. You get a local field such as ℚ<sub>p</sub>, and the adèles glue all of them together. On a surface this fails: completing at a point does not give a field with one valuation. Parshin's idea, from the 1970s, is that the local datum is not a point but a flag: a point on a curve on the surface. Complete along the curve, then at the point, and you get a complete discrete valuation field whose residue field is itself a local field. Iterate n times for dimension n. Kato developed the mixed characteristic case, and Beilinson defined adèles in all dimensions. Class field theory survives, with the multiplicative group replaced by Milnor K-theory. The notes lay out this foundation and point into the literature.

## Main definitions

**n-dimensional local field.** Set cdvdim F = 0 if F is not a complete discrete valuation field, and otherwise one more than cdvdim of the residue field. F is an n-dimensional local field if cdvdim F = n and its n-th residue field is finite (Definition 2.12).
- Dimension 0 is a finite field. Dimension 1 is a usual local field.
- Dimension 2 examples: 𝔽<sub>q</sub>((t<sub>1</sub>))((t<sub>2</sub>)), ℚ<sub>p</sub>((t)), and ℚ<sub>p</sub>{{t}}.

**The field K{{t}}.** For a complete discrete valuation field K, the doubly infinite series Σ a<sub>i</sub>t<sup>i</sup> with coefficients in K whose valuations are bounded below and with a<sub>i</sub> → 0 as i → −∞. Its valuation is the infimum of the valuations of the coefficients, and its residue field is the Laurent series field over the residue field of K.
- So ℚ<sub>p</sub>{{t}} has residue field 𝔽<sub>p</sub>((t)): mixed characteristic at the top level.

**Milnor K-group.** K<sub>m</sub><sup>M</sup>(F) is the m-fold tensor power of F<sup>×</sup> over ℤ, modulo the subgroup generated by tensors a<sub>1</sub> ⊗ ... ⊗ a<sub>m</sub> with a<sub>i</sub> + a<sub>j</sub> = 1 for some i ≠ j. In low degrees, K<sub>0</sub><sup>M</sup> = ℤ and K<sub>1</sub><sup>M</sup> = F<sup>×</sup>.

**Higher adèles.** For a Noetherian scheme X and a quasi-coherent sheaf M, a recursive definition assigns a group A(T, M) to each set T of flags of points. Each is a restricted product of local factors, one per flag. With M the structure sheaf and T all complete flags, it plays the role of the adèle ring of X.

## Main results

**Theorem (classification; Theorem 2.18).** Let F be an n-dimensional local field with last residue field F<sup>(n)</sup>.
- Equal characteristic: if char F ≠ 0, then F ≅ F<sup>(n)</sup>((t<sub>1</sub>))...((t<sub>n</sub>)). If the (n−1)-st residue field has characteristic 0, then F is an iterated Laurent series field in n − 1 variables over a local field of characteristic 0.
- Mixed case: otherwise F is a finite extension of ℚ<sub>q</sub>{{t<sub>1</sub>}}...{{t<sub>r−1</sub>}}((t<sub>r+1</sub>))...((t<sub>n</sub>)) for a suitable r, with ℚ<sub>q</sub> unramified over ℚ<sub>p</sub>.

**Theorem (higher local class field theory, Parshin and Kato; Theorem 5.8).** For every n-dimensional local field F there is a natural reciprocity homomorphism K<sub>n</sub><sup>M</sup>(F) → Gal(F<sup>ab</sup>/F), compatible with norms and with residue fields. For a finite abelian extension L/F it induces an isomorphism K<sub>n</sub><sup>M</sup>(F)/N<sub>L/F</sub>K<sub>n</sub><sup>M</sup>(L) ≅ Gal(L/F).
- The notes state this and sketch four approaches. They do not prove it.

**Theorem (flags give fields; Theorem 7.5 and Corollary 7.6).** A reduced chain A of length n, meaning a local ring with a chain of primes, yields by repeated localisation and completion a finite product of fields of cdvdim ≥ n. If A is essentially of finite type over ℤ, the factors are n-dimensional local fields.
- Section 6 proves the regular case first, where the output is a single field.

**Theorem (adèles compute cohomology, Beilinson; Theorem 8.12).** For a Noetherian scheme X and quasi-coherent M, the adèle groups over all flags form a semi-cosimplicial group whose cohomology is canonically H\*(X, M).
- Stated with a proof sketch only: the sheafified adèles are flasque and resolve M.

## Note, then ignore

- One valuation only. Theorem 1.4 shows that a field is a complete discrete valuation field in at most one way, so cdvdim is intrinsic. You need the fact, not the proof. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Naming clash. Some authors say "n-dimensional local field over k" when the last residue field is k, not finite. Morrow avoids this usage. Safe to skip.
- Topology. The plain valuation topology is poor in dimension 2 and above, and Section 4 replaces it without proofs. It matters for class field theory in detail, not for the outline. Safe to skip.
- Higher rank rings of integers. A higher local field carries a family of them, described in Section 3. Safe to skip.
- Not every inclusion is a morphism. ℚ<sub>p</sub>((t)) over ℚ<sub>p</sub> is not an extension of valuation fields (Example 1.11). It guards against a natural mistake. Safe to skip for now.
