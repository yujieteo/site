## Before you start

A caution first. The thesis PDF would not download, so this page rests on the MIT catalogue record and on the summary of Rector's results in Bousfield and Curtis (1970). It states less than usual. The feeling this thesis may raise is doubt: twelve typed leaves from 1966, in simplicial language nobody taught you. That doubt is allowed. If you want to see where unstable Adams spectral sequences come from, this is one of the first sources. Read it for the pleasure of one clean construction. Finishing can wait.

## The story

Adams built a spectral sequence that computes stable homotopy groups from mod 2 cohomology. People wanted the same for the actual homotopy groups of a space, with no stabilising. Kan had already replaced a space K by a free simplicial group GK, with the homotopy groups of K shifted down by one. A group has a lower central series, and Curtis had used it to filter GK. Rector, a student of Kan, used the mod 2 version of that series instead. Its layers are simplicial vector spaces over 𝔽<sub>2</sub>, built from the mod 2 homology of K by a free restricted Lie algebra functor. The filtration then gives a spectral sequence that starts from homology and converges to the homotopy of K with odd torsion removed.

## Main definitions

**Loop group GK.** For a reduced simplicial set K (one vertex), Kan's loop group GK is a free simplicial group with π<sub>q−1</sub>(GK) ≅ π<sub>q</sub>(K).
- Abelianised mod 2: π<sub>q−1</sub>(GK/Γ<sub>2</sub>GK) is the reduced mod 2 homology of K in degree q.

**Mod 2 lower central series.** For a group G, the subgroup Γ<sub>s</sub>G is generated by all powers c<sup>2<sup>t</sup></sup>, where c is a simple commutator of k elements of G and k·2<sup>t</sup> ≥ s. This gives G = Γ<sub>1</sub>G ⊃ Γ<sub>2</sub>G ⊃ ….
- Each quotient Γ<sub>s</sub>G/Γ<sub>s+1</sub>G is a vector space over 𝔽<sub>2</sub>.

**Rector's spectral sequence.** Apply Γ<sub>s</sub> to GK in each simplicial dimension. The homotopy exact couple of this filtration has E<sup>1</sup><sub>s,q</sub>(K) = π<sub>q</sub>(Γ<sub>s</sub>GK/Γ<sub>s+1</sub>GK), and d<sup>r</sup> maps E<sup>r</sup><sub>s,q</sub> to E<sup>r</sup><sub>s+r,q−1</sub>.

## Main results

These statements follow Bousfield and Curtis, who cite Rector for them. I did not see the theorem numbers or the exact wording in the thesis.

**Theorem (convergence; as reported by Bousfield and Curtis, Section 7).** Let K be simply connected and of finite type. Then Rector's spectral sequence converges to π<sub>∗</sub>(K) modulo odd torsion, that is, to the quotient of π<sub>∗</sub>(K) by its elements of odd finite order.

**Theorem (the first page; same source).** E<sup>1</sup><sub>s,q</sub>(K) ≅ π<sub>q</sub>L<sub>s</sub>(GK/Γ<sub>2</sub>GK), where L<sub>s</sub> is the weight s part of the free restricted Lie algebra functor. So E<sup>1</sup> depends functorially on the graded 𝔽<sub>2</sub>-module H<sub>∗</sub>(K; 𝔽<sub>2</sub>) alone.

**Theorem (link to Adams; Bousfield and Curtis, Theorem 2.1).** Keep only the stages Γ<sub>2<sup>s</sup></sub>GK. For K simply connected with finitely generated homotopy groups, the resulting spectral sequence converges to π<sub>∗</sub>(K) modulo odd torsion. From E<sup>2</sup> on it agrees with the mod 2 Adams spectral sequence in a stable range.
- Bousfield and Curtis credit this jointly to Rector and to the six-author paper on the mod p lower central series. I could not check which part is Rector's.

## Note, then ignore

- Thesis versus article. Rector published a paper with the same title in Topology 5 (1966), pages 343 to 346. I could not compare the two, and you can skip the question on a first reading.
- Other primes. Everything above is at the prime 2, because my source works there. The thesis may treat odd primes too. Safe to skip for now.
- Indexing. Bousfield and Curtis warn that s counts the filtration stage and q the dimension in GK, which is one less than the dimension in K. It matters only when you compare charts, so skip it at first.
- Slow convergence. The same authors say this version converges much more slowly than the one indexed by powers of 2. That is a matter of bookkeeping and can wait.
- Simplicial sets versus spaces. The statements are for simplicial sets. The singular complex and geometric realisation translate them to spaces, so you can ignore the difference.
