## Before you start

The paper is only 26 pages, but it sits where two specialist languages meet: prismatic cohomology and spectral algebraic geometry over the sphere. The likely feeling is the embarrassment of knowing one side and not the other, or neither. That feeling makes sense at a crossroads like this, and you can leave it alone. If you have wondered why Breuil–Kisin twists exist at all, the answer here is short: they are homotopy groups. Read the introduction as a story and enjoy it. Finishing matters less.

## The story

In characteristic zero, Hochschild homology of a smooth algebra is differential forms, and its circle action is the de Rham differential. Periodic and negative cyclic homology then give de Rham cohomology and its Hodge filtration. In mixed characteristic the analogue is a theorem of Bhatt, Morrow and Scholze: for a quasi-regular semiperfectoid ring R, the even homotopy groups of TP(R) and TC<sup>−</sup>(R) are prismatic cohomology and its Nygaard filtration, with Breuil–Kisin twists. But the theorem only sees Nygaard-completed prismatic cohomology, only covers such special rings, and gives rings, not the prismatization stacks. Gregoric removes all three limits at once. He "untwists" Frobenius to undo the completion, following Manam. He then builds spectral stacks from the results and glues them by quasi-syntomic descent. Their underlying classical stacks are exactly X<sup>Δ</sup>, X<sup>N</sup> and X<sup>Syn</sup>.

## Main definitions

**Even periodic spectral stack.** A spectral stack is a functor from E<sub>∞</sub>-rings to anima, of a suitable kind. It is even periodic if it is generated by affines Spec(A) with π<sub>2i</sub>(A) ≅ π<sub>0</sub>(A) and π<sub>2i+1</sub>(A) = 0 for all i. Each spectral stack 𝒳 has an underlying classical stack 𝒳<sup>♡</sup>, with Spec(A)<sup>♡</sup> = Spec(π<sub>0</sub>A).
- Enhancement: an even periodic enhancement of a classical stack X<sub>0</sub> is an even periodic spectral stack 𝒳 with 𝒳<sup>♡</sup> ≃ X<sub>0</sub>.

**Frobenius-untwisted TP and TC<sup>−</sup>.** For a quasi-regular semiperfectoid ring R, TP<sup>(−1)</sup>(R) is a localisation of p-completed TC<sup>−</sup>(R), completed at (p, I). One inverts the elements of π<sub>\*</sub> that the divided Frobenius sends to units.
- Effect: π<sub>2i</sub> of TP<sup>(−1)</sup>(R) is Δ<sub>R</sub>{i} without Nygaard completion, where TP(R) gives the completed version (Theorem 1.1, credited to Manam).

**Spherical Tate-loop space, Nygaard-loop space and spherical prismatization.** Three functors from quasi-syntomic p-adic formal schemes to spectral stacks, defined by quasi-syntomic descent from the affine case.
- Affine case: for X = Spf(R), the first is Spf of TP<sup>(−1)</sup>(R) and the second is the even periodization of Spf of TC<sup>−(−1)</sup>(R).
- Third: the spherical prismatization of Spf(R) is a coequaliser of two maps from the first to the second.

## Main results

**Theorem (Main Theorem; Theorem 2.10).** Let X be a quasi-syntomic p-adic formal scheme. There are canonical even periodic formal spectral stacks whose underlying classical stacks are the prismatization X<sup>Δ</sup>, the filtered prismatization X<sup>N</sup> and the syntomification X<sup>Syn</sup>. Their E<sub>∞</sub>-rings of global functions are TP<sup>(−1)</sup>(X), TC<sup>−(−1)</sup>(X) and p-completed TC(X).
- Step 1: for R quasi-regular semiperfectoid, Spf(R)<sup>Δ</sup> is Spf(Δ<sub>R</sub>) and Spf(R)<sup>N</sup> is Spf of the Rees algebra of the Nygaard filtration modulo 𝔾<sub>m</sub>. The untwisted theories have exactly these as π<sub>0</sub> data.
- Step 2: both sides satisfy quasi-syntomic descent, so the affine identification globalises.

**Theorem (Breuil–Kisin twists; stated in Section I.8 of the introduction).** For each integer i, the homotopy sheaf π<sub>2i</sub> of the structure sheaf of each of the three spectral stacks is the Breuil–Kisin twist O{i} on X<sup>Δ</sup>, X<sup>N</sup> and X<sup>Syn</sup> respectively.

**Theorem (formal groups; part of the Main Theorem).** Every even periodic spectral stack carries a Quillen formal group. On the three stacks above it induces the Drinfeld formal group on X<sup>Δ</sup>, X<sup>N</sup> and X<sup>Syn</sup>.

**Claim (relation to free loops; Section I.9).** The free loop space over the sphere geometrises THH. A coarse quotient by the circle of its even periodization enhances the Nygaard-complete filtered prismatization, by earlier work of the author. It maps canonically to the spherical Nygaard-loop space, and on classical stacks this map is the Nygaard-completion map.

## Note, then ignore

- The superscript (−1). It records that Frobenius has been untwisted once. The untwisted theories map to the usual p-completed ones, and on π<sub>2i</sub> this is the Nygaard completion map. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- TC needs no untwist. Global functions on the third stack are ordinary p-completed TC(X). Safe to skip.
- Even periodic versus even. Independent work of Hahn, Devalapurkar, Raksit, Senger and Yuan uses a different notion of even stacks and covers more objects (Section I.10). Safe to skip.
- No circle action needed. The construction does not use equivariance, a point the acknowledgements credit to Nikolaus. Safe to skip.
- A slip in the text. The introduction once writes "periodization stacks" where it means prismatization stacks. Safe to skip.
