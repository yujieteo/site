## Before you start

This survey runs to 60 journal pages and visits Clifford algebras, spinors, Bott periodicity, Jordan algebras and all five exceptional Lie groups. The likely feeling is overwhelm at the breadth: each section seems to ask for a different course. That feeling makes sense, and you can let it be there. If you want one object that explains why 1, 2, 4 and 8 keep turning up, this is it. The first two sections need only a multiplication table. Read those for fun and stop whenever you like.

## The story

Hamilton spent years looking for a way to multiply triples and found the quaternions in 1843 once he allowed four dimensions. Two months later his friend Graves found an 8-dimensional algebra, the octonions, in which the norm of a product is still the product of the norms. Cayley published first, and the algebra then sat neglected for decades because it is not associative and had no clear use. Baez tells how the use was found. The four algebras ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆 arise by one doubling construction, and each doubling costs one good property. The octonions sit at the last step where division still works. Their symmetry group is the exceptional group G<sub>2</sub>, and the other exceptional groups come from geometry built on them. A seven-point picture, the Fano plane, holds the whole multiplication table.

## Main definitions

**Normed division algebra.** A finite-dimensional real vector space A with a bilinear product, a unit 1 and a norm satisfying ‖ab‖ = ‖a‖‖b‖. Associativity is not assumed. Such an A is a division algebra: ab = 0 forces a = 0 or b = 0.

**Alternative algebra.** An algebra in which any two elements generate an associative subalgebra. By a theorem of Artin this is the same as the identities (aa)b = a(ab), (ab)a = a(ba), (ba)a = b(aa), and any two of the three imply the third.
- Weaker: power-associative, where one element generates an associative subalgebra.
- Measured by: the associator [a, b, c] = (ab)c − a(bc), which is alternating exactly when A is alternative.

**Octonions.** The 8-dimensional real algebra 𝕆 with basis 1, e<sub>1</sub>, …, e<sub>7</sub>, where each e<sub>i</sub> squares to −1, distinct e<sub>i</sub> anticommute, and e<sub>i</sub>e<sub>j</sub> = e<sub>k</sub> when (e<sub>i</sub>, e<sub>j</sub>, e<sub>k</sub>) follow the arrows along one of the seven lines of the Fano plane. For example e<sub>1</sub>e<sub>2</sub> = e<sub>4</sub>.
- Second route: an octonion is a pair of quaternions, multiplied by the Cayley-Dickson rule below.

**Cayley-Dickson construction.** From an algebra A with conjugation \*, form A′ = A × A with (a, b)(c, d) = (ac − db\*, a\*d + cb) and (a, b)\* = (a\*, −b). It takes ℝ to ℂ, ℂ to ℍ and ℍ to 𝕆.

## Main results

**Theorem (Hurwitz; Theorem 1).** ℝ, ℂ, ℍ and 𝕆 are the only normed division algebras.

**Theorem (Zorn; Theorem 2).** ℝ, ℂ, ℍ and 𝕆 are the only alternative division algebras.

**Theorem (Kervaire, Bott-Milnor; Theorem 3).** Every division algebra over ℝ has dimension 1, 2, 4 or 8. This does not say there are only four of them. Baez describes other 4-dimensional examples.

**Theorem (what doubling costs; Propositions 1 to 5 of Section 2.2).** A′ is commutative exactly when A is real, meaning a\* = a for all a. A′ is associative exactly when A is commutative and associative. A′ is alternative and nicely normed exactly when A is associative and nicely normed.
- Case ℝ → ℂ → ℍ → 𝕆: the chain loses, in turn, self-conjugacy, commutativity and associativity. 𝕆 is still alternative and nicely normed, so it is a normed division algebra.
- Case 𝕆 → sedenions: the 16-dimensional algebra is not alternative and has zero divisors, so the chain of division algebras stops.

**Theorem (G<sub>2</sub>; Section 4.1, credited to Cartan, 1914).** The automorphism group of 𝕆 is the exceptional Lie group G<sub>2</sub>. Baez takes this as the definition of G<sub>2</sub> and shows that its dimension is 14.
- Step 1: a basic triple is three unit imaginary octonions, each orthogonal to the subalgebra generated by the other two. Exactly one automorphism carries one basic triple to another.
- Step 2: the choices form spheres S<sup>6</sup>, S<sup>5</sup> and S<sup>3</sup>, and 6 + 5 + 3 = 14.

## Note, then ignore

- Inverses versus division. For non-associative algebras, "every non-zero element has an inverse" and "no zero divisors" are different conditions. The sedenions have the first and not the second. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Index conventions. Baez's table has e<sub>1</sub>e<sub>2</sub> = e<sub>4</sub>, with rules for adding 1 to, or doubling, all indices mod 7. Other authors label the Fano plane differently. Safe to skip.
- Twisted group algebra. Section 2.1 also describes 𝕆 as the group algebra of (ℤ/2)<sup>3</sup> with signs inserted. It is a second view of the same table. Safe to skip.
- Errata. The index page points to published errata (Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 42, 2005). They do not affect the statements above. Safe to skip.
- Physics. The introduction links 𝕆 to superstrings in 10 dimensions and says plainly that there is no proof the octonions describe the real world. Safe to skip.
