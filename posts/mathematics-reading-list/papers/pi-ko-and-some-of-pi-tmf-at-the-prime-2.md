## Before you start

The symbols arrive fast here: h<sub>20</sub>, b<sub>30</sub>, Massey products with four slots. The feeling that comes up is fear of being lost by page 4, and of being too slow to keep the charts in your head. That fear is fine. The author says she went back to the easy case when the hard one confused her. If you want to watch a working topologist find differentials, this is 25 informal pages of it. Enjoy the ko case. The tmf case can wait.

## The story

To compute homotopy groups with the Adams spectral sequence you first need its E<sub>2</sub>-page, an Ext group over the Steenrod algebra A. For ko, the relevant Ext is over the small subalgebra A(1), and a resolution by hand works. For tmf it is over A(2), and a hand resolution is close to impossible. The May spectral sequence breaks the job up. It starts from a polynomial ring on classes h<sub>ij</sub> and converges to Ext, and its differentials are pure algebra. Catherine Ray's thesis runs it twice: fully for A(1), and for A(2) out to about stem 13. Along the way it shows the tricks for finding differentials that papers tend to leave in footnotes. It ends with one Adams differential for tmf, found by comparing four spectral sequences.

## Main definitions

**A(n) and the change of rings.** A(1) is the part of the mod 2 Steenrod algebra generated by Sq<sup>1</sup> and Sq<sup>2</sup>. Since H<sup>\*</sup>(ko) ≅ A//A(1), the Adams spectral sequence for ko starts from Ext over A(1) of (𝔽<sub>2</sub>, 𝔽<sub>2</sub>) and converges to the 2-completed π<sub>\*</sub>(ko).
- For tmf: the same holds with A(2), using the input H<sup>\*</sup>(tmf) ≅ A//A(2).

**May spectral sequence.** A trigraded algebraic spectral sequence with E<sub>1</sub> = 𝔽<sub>2</sub>[h<sub>ij</sub> : i ≥ 1, j ≥ 0] that converges to Ext over A of (𝔽<sub>2</sub>, 𝔽<sub>2</sub>). The class h<sub>ij</sub> has stem 2<sup>j</sup>(2<sup>i</sup> − 1) − 1 and filtration 1. One writes h<sub>n</sub> for h<sub>1n</sub>.
- For A(1): E<sub>1</sub> = 𝔽<sub>2</sub>[h<sub>10</sub>, h<sub>11</sub>, h<sub>20</sub>].
- For A(2): E<sub>1</sub> = 𝔽<sub>2</sub>[h<sub>0</sub>, h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>2</sub>, h<sub>20</sub>, h<sub>21</sub>, h<sub>30</sub>].

**Massey product form of a class.** Classes on later May pages can be written as Massey products, for example b<sub>20</sub> = h<sub>20</sub><sup>2</sup> = ⟨h<sub>0</sub>, h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>0</sub>, h<sub>1</sub>⟩. A higher Leibniz rule then gives the differential of the class from the shorter brackets inside it.

## Main results

**Theorem (the first d<sub>2</sub>; Section 4, the second "Theorem").** Given d<sub>1</sub>(h<sub>20</sub>) = h<sub>0</sub>h<sub>1</sub>, one has d<sub>2</sub>(b<sub>20</sub>) = h<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> + h<sub>0</sub><sup>2</sup>h<sub>2</sub>.
- Proof 1: apply the four-fold Leibniz rule, using ⟨h<sub>0</sub>, h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>0</sub>⟩ = h<sub>1</sub><sup>2</sup> and ⟨h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>0</sub>, h<sub>1</sub>⟩ = h<sub>0</sub>h<sub>2</sub>.
- Proof 2: apply Nakamura's lemma, which turns a d<sub>1</sub> into a d<sub>2</sub> through the operations Sq<sup>0</sup> and Sq<sup>1</sup>.

**Computation (π<sub>\*</sub>(ko); Section 4).** For A(1) the May spectral sequence collapses at E<sub>3</sub>, there are no hidden extensions, and the Adams spectral sequence has no differentials. The 2-completed groups in degrees 0 to 8 are ℤ, ℤ/2, ℤ/2, 0, ℤ, 0, 0, 0, ℤ. As a ring, π<sub>\*</sub>(ko) is the 2-adic integers with generators η, α, β and relations η<sup>3</sup> = 0, 2η = 0, αη = 0, α<sup>2</sup> = 4β. Here η = h<sub>1</sub>, α = h<sub>0</sub>b<sub>20</sub> in degree 4 and β = b<sub>20</sub><sup>2</sup> in degree 8.

**Computation (Ext over A(2) in a range; Section 5).** The E<sub>2</sub>-page has generators h<sub>0</sub>, h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>2</sub>, b<sub>20</sub>, b<sub>21</sub>, b<sub>30</sub> and a new class x<sub>7</sub> = h<sub>1</sub>h<sub>30</sub> + h<sub>21</sub>h<sub>20</sub>. The d<sub>2</sub> differentials are d<sub>2</sub>(x<sub>7</sub>) = h<sub>0</sub>h<sub>2</sub><sup>2</sup>, d<sub>2</sub>(b<sub>21</sub>) = h<sub>2</sub><sup>3</sup>, d<sub>2</sub>(b<sub>30</sub>) = h<sub>1</sub>b<sub>21</sub>, and the d<sub>2</sub> on b<sub>20</sub> above. In the range shown the sequence collapses at E<sub>3</sub>.

**Claim (an Adams differential for tmf; Section 6).** There is an Adams d<sub>2</sub> from the class h<sub>0</sub><sup>3</sup>[12] in stem 12 to Ph<sub>2</sub>[0] in stem 11.
- Step 1: build a finite cell complex X that matches tmf through dimension 12, and get an Atiyah–Hirzebruch differential d<sub>12</sub>(8[12]) = ⟨8, ν, σ⟩[0] from a lemma in Wang and Xu's 61-sphere paper.
- Step 2: identify ⟨8, ν, σ⟩ with Ph<sub>2</sub>, note that only the h<sub>0</sub>-tower in stem 12 can hit it, and push the differential forward to tmf.

## Note, then ignore

- The h<sub>2</sub> term for A(1). The formula d<sub>2</sub>(b<sub>20</sub>) = h<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> + h<sub>0</sub><sup>2</sup>h<sub>2</sub> is written with h<sub>2</sub>, which is not among the three generators for A(1). There the relation reads h<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> = 0, matching η<sup>3</sup> = 0. This is my reading, and it is safe to skip.
- A circle in the logic. The paper says the proof of H<sup>\*</sup>(ko) ≅ A//A(1) uses the homotopy groups of ko. So the ko computation is an exercise, not a first proof. Section 7 gives that proof from a cofibre sequence relating ko and ku. Safe to skip.
- Stem 12 or 13. Different sections give the range as stem 12 or stem 13. Nothing depends on it. Safe to skip.
- Numbering. Two results are both called Theorem 2, and one citation prints as "[?]". This is a thesis draft. Safe to skip.
- Machine help. The d<sub>1</sub> differentials for A(2) are propagated by Eric Peterson's program Ext Chart. Safe to skip.
