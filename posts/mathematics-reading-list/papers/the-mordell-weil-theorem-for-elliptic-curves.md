## Before you start

The proof calls on group cohomology, Kummer theory, the Weil pairing, formal groups, the class group and the unit theorem. If you have not met all of these, you may feel exposed, as if you had turned up without the homework. That feeling is allowed, and it does not need fixing first. The note quotes each tool in one line and moves on, and you can do the same. What you may want from it is the shape of a descent argument. Read for the pleasure of seeing four ideas lock together. Finishing comes second.

## The story

An elliptic curve E over a number field K has a group law, so its K-points form an abelian group E(K). People wanted to know its structure. The trouble is that E(K) can be infinite, and there is no obvious way to list it. Booher follows the standard route in four moves. Reduction at good primes bounds the torsion. Galois cohomology shows that E(K)/mE(K) is finite. A height function measures the size of a point, in the way that max(|a|, |b|) measures the fraction a/b. Then descent: if a point is large, subtract a coset representative and divide by m, and the result is smaller. Repeat, and you land in a finite set of small points. Those generate everything. The last section runs the method on y<sup>2</sup> = x(x − 6)(x + 6).

## Main definitions

**Kummer map.** Assume K contains the m-torsion E[m]. Galois cohomology and the Weil pairing give an injective homomorphism from E(K)/mE(K) into K<sup>×</sup>/(K<sup>×</sup>)<sup>m</sup> × K<sup>×</sup>/(K<sup>×</sup>)<sup>m</sup>.
- Case m = 2, with E given by y<sup>2</sup> = (x − e<sub>1</sub>)(x − e<sub>2</sub>)(x − e<sub>3</sub>): a point (x, y) that is not 2-torsion goes to (x − e<sub>1</sub>, x − e<sub>2</sub>).

**Height of a number.** For α in K, h<sub>K</sub>(α) is the sum of max(0, log |α|<sub>v</sub>) over all normalised absolute values v of K.
- Over ℚ: for a fraction a/b in lowest terms, the height is log max(|a|, |b|).

**Naive height on E.** For a point P = (a, b) on a Weierstrass model, h<sub>E</sub>(P) = h<sub>K</sub>(a). The point at infinity has height 0.

**Canonical height.** ĥ<sub>E</sub>(P) is the limit of h<sub>E</sub>(2<sup>n</sup>P)/4<sup>n</sup>. It is the unique function on E(K) that stays within a bounded distance of h<sub>E</sub> and satisfies ĥ<sub>E</sub>(mP) = m<sup>2</sup> ĥ<sub>E</sub>(P) for m ≥ 1.

## Main results

**Theorem (Mordell-Weil; Theorem 1).** Let E be an elliptic curve over a number field K. Then E(K) is a finitely generated abelian group.
- Step 1: pick representatives P<sub>1</sub>, ..., P<sub>n</sub> of E(K)/mE(K), let C be their largest canonical height, and let S be the finite set of points of canonical height at most C.
- Step 2: if Q is a point of least height outside the group generated by S, write Q = mR + P<sub>i</sub>. The parallelogram law gives m<sup>2</sup> ĥ(R) &lt; 4 ĥ(Q), so R is smaller and lies in the group. Then so does Q, a contradiction.

**Theorem (weak Mordell-Weil; Theorem 2).** Let E be an elliptic curve over a number field K and m ≥ 2. Then E(K)/mE(K) is finite.
- Step 1: enlarge K to contain E[m] (Proposition 3), then embed E(K)/mE(K) by the Kummer map.
- Step 2: at each prime of good reduction not dividing m, the image has valuation divisible by m (Lemma 5). The group of such classes is finite, by finiteness of the class group and the unit theorem (Proposition 6).

**Proposition (canonical height; Proposition 12).** For E over a number field K: only finitely many points of E(K) have canonical height below any bound. ĥ(mP) = m<sup>2</sup> ĥ(P). The parallelogram law ĥ(P + Q) + ĥ(P − Q) = 2ĥ(P) + 2ĥ(Q) holds exactly. And ĥ(P) ≥ 0, with equality exactly when P is torsion.
- Step 1: prove each statement for the naive height up to a bounded error (Proposition 10).
- Step 2: pass to the limit along 2<sup>n</sup>P, which kills the errors.

**Claim (worked example; Section 4).** For y<sup>2</sup> = x(x − 6)(x + 6) over ℚ, the note shows E(ℚ) ≅ ℤ/2 × ℤ/2 × ℤ. The point (−3, 9) has infinite order, and the note says a height search confirms that it generates the free part.

## Note, then ignore

- Heights over general K. The note proves the height properties only over ℚ and puts the general proofs in an appendix. The ℚ case carries the idea. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Group cohomology appendix. Section A builds the needed cohomology from scratch for readers who lack it. You can take the Kummer sequence on trust. Safe to skip.
- The parallelogram law for the naive height. Its proof uses the explicit group law and is cited from Silverman, Theorem VIII.6.2. Safe to skip.
- Formal groups in Lemma 5. They are used once, to show multiplication by m is invertible on the kernel of reduction. Safe to skip.
- Loose ends in the text. The note is dated 2012 and has an unfinished footnote and some typing slips, for example a factor 2<sup>−n</sup> where 4<sup>−n</sup> is meant. None affects the argument. Safe to skip.
