## Before you start

The paper is 21 pages, but it assumes the language of symmetric tensor categories, the Verlinde category Ver<sub>p</sub> and Frobenius exactness from the first section. The likely feeling is a small dread of the citations you have not read. You can have that feeling and still read. If you like the classical fact that Clifford algebras are matrix algebras in disguise, this is the same fact in a wider world. Enjoy the first three pages. Finishing is optional.

## The story

Over a field, a vector space with a symmetric form gives a Clifford algebra, and one with a skew form gives a Weyl algebra. Both are simple when the form is non-degenerate. Etingof asks what survives when vector spaces are replaced by objects of a symmetric tensor category 𝒞 over an algebraically closed field of characteristic not 2. Such categories include super vector spaces and, in characteristic p, the Verlinde category. The first surprise is that the two algebras become one theory. Tensoring with an odd line swaps symmetric and skew forms. The obstacle is positive characteristic, where symmetric powers misbehave. The fix is to ask that the symmetric algebra of V has finite length. Then the Weyl algebra is Azumaya, and its Morita class lands in the Brauer group of 𝒞.

## Main definitions

**Clifford and Weyl algebra A(V).** Let V in 𝒞 carry a bilinear form B: V ⊗ V → 1 that is symmetric or skew with respect to the braiding. Form the Heisenberg Lie (super)algebra V ⊕ 1, with bracket B and 1 central. A(V) is its enveloping algebra with the central 1 identified with the unit.
- Skew B: V is even, and A(V) is the Weyl algebra.
- Symmetric B: V is odd, and A(V) is the Clifford algebra.

**Azumaya algebra and Brauer group.** An algebra A in 𝒞 is Azumaya if the natural map A ⊗ A<sup>op</sup> → End(A) is an isomorphism. Morita classes of Azumaya algebras form the Brauer group Br(𝒞).

**Symplectic Witt group SW(𝒞).** Take 𝒞 Frobenius exact. A symplectic object is a V with a non-degenerate skew form. SW(𝒞) is generated by classes [V] of symplectic objects whose symmetric algebra SV has finite length, added by direct sum, with [V] = 0 when A(V) ≅ End(S) for some object S.

## Main results

**Theorem (parity change and PBW; Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 2.2).** If 𝒞 contains a super-line ψ, then A(V ⊗ ψ) is A(V) with its odd part twisted by ψ, so Weyl and Clifford theories are equivalent. For any form B, the associated graded of A(V) is the symmetric algebra SV in the Weyl case and the exterior algebra ∧V in the Clifford case.
- Step 1: adjoin a super-line if needed and reduce to the Weyl case.
- Step 2: write an explicit Moyal-Weyl star product on SV and check it is associative.

**Theorem (Weyl algebras are Azumaya; Corollary 3.4).** Let 𝒞 be Frobenius exact and V a symplectic object with SV of finite length. Then A(V) is simple and Azumaya. For any W with SW of finite length, A(W ⊕ W\*) → End(SW) is an isomorphism.
- Step 1: finite length forces moderate growth, so there is a fibre functor to super vector spaces in characteristic 0, or to Ver<sub>p</sub> in characteristic p.
- Step 2: in Ver<sub>p</sub>, split V into simple pieces whose p-th symmetric power vanishes, and use a lowest-degree argument on ideals (Proposition 3.1).

**Theorem (Witt group inside Brauer group; Proposition 3.9).** For 𝒞 Frobenius exact, V ↦ [A(V)] gives an injective homomorphism SW(𝒞) → Br<sub>2</sub>(𝒞), the 2-torsion of the Brauer group. Here SW(𝒞) is an 𝔽<sub>2</sub>-vector space.

**Theorem (finite groups; Proposition 3.10 and Corollary 3.14).** Let G be a finite group of order prime to the characteristic and 𝒞 = Rep(G) ⊠ sVec. Then A(X ⊗ ψ) is the classical Clifford superalgebra of an orthogonal representation X, and its class is read from Stiefel-Whitney data of X. Over ℂ, the map to Br<sub>2</sub>(𝒞) is surjective when G is abelian, and is not surjective for every G.

## Note, then ignore

- Factor of 2. The paper uses the relation vw + wv = B(v, w), without the classical factor 2. It only rescales B. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Ind-algebras. A(V) can be infinite, so it lives in the ind-completion of 𝒞. This is bookkeeping. Safe to skip.
- Super-opposite. In the Clifford case, "opposite" and "tensor product" mean the super versions. The signs take care of themselves. Safe to skip.
- Queer algebras Q(V). They appear because a Clifford algebra on an odd number of generators is simple only as a superalgebra. They are needed for Ver<sub>p</sub> in Propositions 3.7 and 3.8. Safe to skip.
- Modified product on cohomology. In Section 3.7 the group law on H<sup>2</sup>(G × ℤ/2) is twisted by a cup product, which is why some statements hold only "as sets". Safe to skip.
