## Before you start

This is a sequel, and it borrows its main tool from representation theory of real Lie groups. You may feel left out before you begin, as if you had walked in on the second half of a talk. Nothing is wrong with that feeling. You do not need the first paper's proof, only its statement, and the tool is used as a black box. If you like proofs where pieces are cut and moved, this one is for you. Read it for that, and stop when it stops being fun.

## The story

The authors had proved that a symmetric body, cut by the mirrors of a reflection group through an inside point, splits into alternating pieces of equal total volume. Their proof was analytic: differentiate, integrate, induct. In the plane, however, Carter and Wagon and later Frederickson had shown the same fact by dissection. They cut the slices into smaller pieces and matched each piece with a congruent piece of the opposite sign. Could the n-dimensional theorem be proved that way? The obstacle is that a general reflection group gives chambers that are hard to match by hand. The unblocking idea is a 2-structure: a sub-arrangement that is a product of single mirrors and dihedral mirror systems. An earlier identity of the authors writes the alternating sum for the whole arrangement as a signed sum over 2-structures. On each product, the plane dissection does the work.

## Main definitions

**Normalised pseudo-root system.** A finite set Φ of unit vectors in a real inner product space V such that the reflection in the hyperplane orthogonal to any β ∈ Φ maps Φ to itself. The reflections generate a finite group W. The hyperplanes orthogonal to the vectors of Φ form a Coxeter arrangement H.

**2-structure (Definition 2.1).** A subset φ ⊂ Φ that is a disjoint union of pairwise orthogonal irreducible pieces, each of type A<sub>1</sub>, B<sub>2</sub> or I<sub>2</sub>(2<sup>k</sup>) with k ≥ 3, and such that every w ∈ W that preserves the positive part of φ has determinant 1.
- W acts transitively on the set of 2-structures.
- φ has the same rank as Φ exactly when −id (on the span of Φ) lies in W.

**Abstract dissection group (Definition 3.1).** Take a family C(V) of subsets of V closed under finite intersections, affine isometries, cutting by half-spaces, and products. For example, all convex subsets. K(V) is the free abelian group on C(V) modulo [∅] = 0, the inclusion-exclusion relation [C ∪ C′] + [C ∩ C′] = [C] + [C′], and [g(C)] = [C] for every affine isometry g.

**Valuation.** A function from C(V) to an abelian group that factors through K(V). Volume and all the intrinsic volumes are examples.

## Main results

**Theorem (basic identity; Theorem 2.5, quoted from an earlier paper).** Let H be a Coxeter arrangement with pseudo-root system Φ. Then the abstract pizza quantity P(H) = Σ<sub>T</sub> (−1)<sup>T</sup> [T] equals Σ<sub>φ</sub> ε(φ) P(H<sub>φ</sub>), a signed sum over all 2-structures φ, where H<sub>φ</sub> is the sub-arrangement given by φ. The identity lives in a group generated by closed convex cones cut out by H.

**Theorem (abstract pizza theorem; Theorem 3.5).** Let H be a Coxeter arrangement in an n-dimensional space V whose group W contains −id. Let K ∈ C(V) be stable under W, and let a ∈ V be a point such that K contains the convex hull of the orbit W·a. If H is not of type A<sub>1</sub><sup>n</sup>, then Σ<sub>T</sub> (−1)<sup>T</sup> [T ∩ (K + a)] = 0 in K(V). If H is of type A<sub>1</sub><sup>n</sup>, the sum equals the class of a product of n half-open segments (0, 2(a, e<sub>i</sub>)e<sub>i</sub>].
- Step 1: expand over 2-structures. Any term with a dihedral factor vanishes by a version of Frederickson's plane dissection.
- Step 2: if every term has type A<sub>1</sub><sup>n</sup>, the terms are non-zero boxes. A refined Bolyai-Gerwien theorem (Section 4) shows that they cancel.

**Corollary (intrinsic volumes; Corollary 3.7).** Under the same hypotheses, and H not of type A<sub>1</sub><sup>n</sup>, the alternating sum of the i-th intrinsic volume of the pieces is zero for all 0 ≤ i ≤ n. Here K must be convex when i ≠ n. For type A<sub>1</sub><sup>n</sup> and convex K, it is zero for 0 ≤ i ≤ n − 1.

## Note, then ignore

- "Pseudo" in pseudo-root. The types H<sub>3</sub> and H<sub>4</sub> do not come from crystallographic root systems, so the authors use unit vectors and drop the integrality condition. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The sign ε(φ). Each 2-structure carries a sign, defined in the earlier paper and in work of Herb. You only need that it exists. Safe to skip.
- Lower-dimensional pieces. The dissection group keeps track of boundaries, which volume ignores. This is what makes the surface-area statement come out. Safe to skip.
- Groemer's extension. It lets the class [C] make sense for non-convex and half-open sets. It is used to write the box in the A<sub>1</sub><sup>n</sup> case. Safe to skip.
- Shape of the pizza. In this proof K need not be measurable or of finite volume. It only needs the symmetry and the convex hull condition. Safe to skip.
