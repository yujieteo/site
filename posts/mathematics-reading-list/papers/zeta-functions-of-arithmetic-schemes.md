## Before you start

These are 45 slides from a lecture series, with no prose between the formulas. Almost every slide names a cohomology theory you may not have met: Weil-étale, Deligne, Weil-Arakelov, prismatic. The likely feeling is fear of being lost by slide three. That is a normal response to slides, which are written for a room with a speaker in it. If you want one statement that contains the class number formula and the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer formula as cases, it is here. Look for the examples you know and enjoy those.

## The story

The Dedekind zeta function of a number field has a residue at s = 1 made of the class number, the regulator, the roots of unity and the discriminant. People want such a formula for every arithmetic scheme X and every integer n. Over a finite field, Lichtenbaum's idea works: replace the Galois group of 𝔽<sub>p</sub> by its subgroup ℤ generated by Frobenius, and the cohomology groups become finitely generated. Then the leading term is an Euler characteristic. Over ℤ the scheme is not compact, because the infinite place is missing. Flach and Morin add it with Arakelov-style cohomology whose values are locally compact groups. The leading term becomes an alternating product of volumes of compact groups. The right measure comes from a variant of topological cyclic homology.

## Main definitions

**Zeta function.** For X regular, connected, of dimension d and proper over Spec ℤ, set ζ(X, s) = ∏ (1 − N(x)<sup>−s</sup>)<sup>−1</sup> over closed points x. It converges for Re(s) &gt; d. Write ζ<sup>\*</sup>(X, n) for its leading Taylor coefficient at s = n.
- X = Spec O<sub>F</sub>: the Dedekind zeta function of F.

**Motivic complex ℤ(n).** A complex of étale sheaves built from Bloch's higher Chow cycles, with ℤ(0) = ℤ and ℤ(1) = 𝔾<sub>m</sub>[−1]. Modulo ℓ<sup>ν</sup>, with ℓ invertible, it is the n-th tensor power of the sheaf of roots of unity.

**Weil-Arakelov cohomology.** Complexes RΓ<sub>ar,c</sub>(X, −) with coefficients ℤ(n), ℝ̃(n) and ℝ̃/ℤ(n), fitting in one exact triangle and taking values in locally compact abelian groups. They are defined only under assumptions: finite generation of étale motivic cohomology in low degrees, the Beilinson conjectures, and Artin-Verdier duality.
- With ℝ̃(n): finite-dimensional real vector spaces, with an acyclic complex given by cup product with a class θ.
- With ℝ̃/ℤ(n): compact commutative Lie groups, so each has a volume once a measure is fixed.

**Fundamental line.** Δ(X/ℤ, n) is the tensor product of the determinant of Weil-étale cohomology with compact support and the determinant of a derived de Rham complex modulo the n-th step of the Hodge filtration, taken relative to the sphere spectrum. It is a free ℤ-module of rank one with a trivialisation λ<sub>∞</sub> over ℝ.

## Main results

**Conjecture (vanishing order and leading term).** Under the assumptions above, for every integer n: the order of ζ(X, s) at s = n is Σ (−1)<sup>i</sup> · i · dim H<sup>i</sup><sub>ar,c</sub>(X, ℝ̃(n)). And |ζ<sup>\*</sup>(X, n)|<sup>−1</sup> is the alternating product of the volumes of the groups H<sup>i</sup><sub>ar,c</sub>(X, ℝ̃/ℤ(n)). In terms of the fundamental line: λ<sub>∞</sub> sends ζ<sup>\*</sup>(X, n)<sup>−1</sup> · ℤ onto Δ(X/ℤ, n).

**Theorem (finite fields; attributed to Milne, Lichtenbaum, Geisser).** Let X be smooth and proper over 𝔽<sub>p</sub>. Assume Weil-étale cohomology of ℤ(n) is a perfect complex, which is known for d ≤ 1. Then the order of ζ(X, s) at s = n is the same alternating sum of ranks, and ζ<sup>\*</sup>(X, n) is, up to sign, the Euler characteristic of that cohomology times an explicit power of p read from Hodge numbers.
- Primes ℓ ≠ p: Grothendieck's formula in ℓ-adic cohomology.
- The prime p: prismatic cohomology and topological cyclic homology.

**Theorem (number rings).** For X = Spec O<sub>F</sub> the vanishing orders hold (Borel). The leading term conjecture holds for n = 0, 1 for every number field F, and for all integers n when F is abelian over ℚ. For n ≤ 0 it reads ζ<sub>F</sub><sup>\*</sup>(n) = ± h<sub>1−n</sub> R<sub>1−n</sub> / w<sub>1−n</sub>, with h, w and R built from K-groups of O<sub>F</sub> and the Beilinson regulator.

**Theorem (Tamagawa numbers, BSD, functional equation).** Three compatibility statements.
- For X smooth and proper over a number ring, the conjecture with an explicit rational correction factor is equivalent to the Tamagawa number conjecture for the motive of the generic fibre. For a regular proper arithmetic surface, the statement for the H<sup>1</sup> part at s = 1 is equivalent to the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer formula for the Jacobian of the generic fibre.
- If ζ(X, s) satisfies the expected functional equation with the Bloch conductor, the conjecture at n holds exactly when it holds at d − n.

## Note, then ignore

- The factor (n − 1)!. With ordinary derived de Rham cohomology, the formula for Spec O<sub>F</sub> needs a correction (n − 1)!<sup>−[F:ℚ]</sup>. Working over the sphere spectrum absorbs it. Safe to skip at first.
- Negative n. ℤ(n) for n &lt; 0 is defined by a projective bundle trick. Safe to skip.
- Brauer group and Sha. On a surface the Brauer group of X stands in for the Tate-Shafarevich group, up to local and global index terms. Safe to skip.
- Signs. Several formulas hold up to ±1. Safe to skip.
- Missing lectures. The file name suggests these are lectures 3 to 5 of a series. The slides still start from the definitions. Safe to skip.
