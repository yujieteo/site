basis: the introduction and the main theorem statements

## Before you start

This survey mentions algebraic K-theory on the first page and then says it will not define it. The likely feeling is unease at using a black box you have not opened. That unease is reasonable, and the authors share the stance: they treat K-groups as a source of ranks and lattices and nothing more. If you like formulas you can check numerically, this text is full of them. Read for the examples. Completing all three parts is optional.

## The story

Dirichlet's class number formula writes the residue of ζ<sub>F</sub>(s) at s = 1 as a determinant of logarithms of units. People wanted the same for ζ<sub>F</sub>(m) with m ≥ 2. For m = 2 geometry gives the answer. A hyperbolic 3-manifold attached to F has volume a multiple of ζ<sub>F</sub>(2). Cut the manifold into ideal tetrahedra. Each has volume D(z), the Bloch-Wigner dilogarithm of a cross-ratio. So ζ<sub>F</sub>(2) is a sum of dilogarithms at algebraic points. Changing the triangulation changes the sum by five-term relations, and this leads to the Bloch group B(F), which is K<sub>3</sub>(F) up to torsion. Zagier's conjecture repeats the pattern for every m with the m-th polylogarithm. Parts II and III carry the idea to partial zeta functions and to elliptic curves.

## Main definitions

**Bloch-Wigner dilogarithm.** D(z) = Im(Li<sub>2</sub>(z)) + arg(1 − z) log|z|, where Li<sub>2</sub>(z) = Σ z<sup>n</sup>/n<sup>2</sup>. It is single-valued and real-analytic on ℂ minus {0, 1}. For general m the survey uses a single-valued L<sub>m</sub> built from Li<sub>m</sub>, ..., Li<sub>1</sub> and Bernoulli numbers.

**Bloch group.** A(F) is the set of Σ n<sub>i</sub>[z<sub>i</sub>] in ℤ[F] with Σ n<sub>i</sub> z<sub>i</sub> ∧ (1 − z<sub>i</sub>) = 0 in Λ<sup>2</sup>F<sup>×</sup>. C(F) is the subgroup generated by the five-term elements [x] + [y] + [(1−x)/(1−xy)] + [1−xy] + [(1−y)/(1−xy)]. Then B(F) = A(F)/C(F).
- D vanishes on C(F), so D is a well-defined map B(ℂ) → ℝ.

**Higher Bloch groups.** B<sub>m</sub>(F) = A<sub>m</sub>(F)/C<sub>m</sub>(F). A<sub>m</sub>(F) is defined by induction: ξ = Σ n<sub>i</sub>[x<sub>i</sub>] lies in A<sub>m</sub>(F) if Σ n<sub>i</sub> φ(x<sub>i</sub>)[x<sub>i</sub>] lies in C<sub>m−1</sub>(F) for every homomorphism φ: F<sup>×</sup> → ℤ.
- Practical C<sub>m</sub>(F), for number fields: the elements of A<sub>m</sub>(F) on which L<sub>m</sub> vanishes under every embedding.
- Intended C<sub>m</sub>(F): the specialisations of all functional equations of L<sub>m</sub>. The two are known to agree only for m = 2.

**Elliptic dilogarithm.** For E(ℂ) = ℂ<sup>×</sup>/q<sup>ℤ</sup> and P the image of x, D<sup>E</sup>(P) is the sum of D(q<sup>l</sup>x) over all integers l. This is Bloch's averaged dilogarithm.

## Main results

**Theorem (ζ<sub>F</sub>(2) and the Bloch group; unnumbered, end of section 1).** Let F be a number field with r<sub>1</sub> real and r<sub>2</sub> complex places. Then B(F) is finitely generated of rank r<sub>2</sub>. For a ℚ-basis ξ<sub>j</sub> of B(F) ⊗ ℚ and one embedding σ<sub>i</sub> from each complex pair, ζ<sub>F</sub>(2) is a non-zero rational multiple of |D<sub>F</sub>|<sup>1/2</sup> times a power of π times det D(σ<sub>i</sub>(ξ<sub>j</sub>)). The survey's text and its display give that power differently, so I leave the exponent out.
- Step 1: Bloch and Suslin identify B(F) with K<sub>3</sub>(F) up to torsion, and D with the Borel regulator.
- Step 2: Borel's theorem gives the rank and the covolume of the regulator lattice.

**Conjecture (polylogarithm conjecture; section 2).** For a number field F and m ≥ 2, the image of A<sub>m</sub>(F) under L<sub>m</sub> and the embeddings of F is commensurable with the Borel regulator lattice of K<sub>2m−1</sub>(F). So B<sub>m</sub>(F) has rank r<sub>2</sub> for m even and r<sub>1</sub> + r<sub>2</sub> for m odd, and ζ<sub>F</sub>(m) is, up to a rational factor, |D<sub>F</sub>|<sup>1/2</sup> times a power of π times a determinant of values of L<sub>m</sub>.
- Known: Beilinson and Deligne, and de Jeu, map the higher Bloch groups to K-theory and show that the regulator is given by polylogarithms.
- Known: Goncharov proved the missing surjectivity for m = 3. The formula also holds for cyclotomic fields.

**Conjecture (elliptic dilogarithm; section 8).** For an elliptic curve E over ℚ, D<sup>E</sup> maps a group A<sub>2</sub>(E/ℚ) of Galois-invariant divisors, cut out by three explicit conditions, onto a lattice commensurable with ℤ · R<sub>E</sub>, where R<sub>E</sub> = N L(E, 2)/(8π) and N is the conductor.

**Theorem (Beilinson; section 9).** If E over ℚ is modular, there is a ℚ-linear combination ξ of torsion points with D<sup>E</sup>(ξ) = R<sub>E</sub>.

## Note, then ignore

- Torsion. The survey ignores torsion in K-groups and Bloch groups throughout, and often tensors with ℚ. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The symbols [0] and [1]. They are declared to lie in A(F) by convention. Safe to skip.
- Deligne's extra condition. For m = 3 and F not totally real, the kernel of the obvious map β<sub>3</sub> is too big, which is why A<sub>m</sub> is defined by induction. Safe to skip.
- The "enhanced" polylogarithm of section 4. It lifts L<sub>m</sub> from ℝ to ℂ modulo rational multiples of (2πi)<sup>m</sup>. It matters for Part II only. Safe to skip.
- Date. The survey is from about 2000. All elliptic curves over ℚ are now known to be modular, so the hypothesis in Beilinson's theorem is automatic. Safe to skip.
