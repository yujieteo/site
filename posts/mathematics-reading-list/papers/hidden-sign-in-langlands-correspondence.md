## Before you start

The likely feeling is doubt about whether you are allowed to read this. It is a note by Bernstein about the foundations of the Langlands correspondence, and that name alone can make you feel under-prepared. The feeling is welcome to stay. The note is 18 pages, informal, and nearly every construction can be tested on PGL(2). If you want to know why half-integer twists and odd signs keep turning up in this subject, here is one clean reason. Read it for the pleasure of the argument.

## The story

The local Langlands correspondence should match irreducible representations of a split p-adic group G with homomorphisms from the Weil group to the dual group G<sup>∨</sup>. Bernstein asks for one modest property: the match should not change when you apply an automorphism of the coefficient field C. The standard correspondence fails this. It rests on the Satake isomorphism, which uses normalised induction, which uses a square root of the modulus character, which needs a choice of √p. Field automorphisms move √p. Bernstein's repair is to enlarge G by a central 𝔾<sub>m</sub> so that a canonical weight "of type ρ" exists. The dual of the enlarged group is a slightly bigger group G<sup>D</sup>, and parameters now land there. A sign appears: G<sup>∨</sup> carries a distinguished central element ε of order 1 or 2.

## Main definitions

**Basic requirement.** Suppose two constructions produce objects S(C) and T(C) functorially in a field C. A correspondence S(C) → T(C) meets the basic requirement if it commutes with all automorphisms of C. Here C is algebraically closed of characteristic 0.

**Weight of type ρ.** A weight r of the Cartan group with σ<sub>α</sub>(r) = r/α for every simple root α, written multiplicatively. For a simply connected semisimple group, half the sum of positive roots is one. In general none exists.

**Extended group and dual data.** Fix a square root L of the anticanonical line bundle on the flag variety. The extended group G̃ consists of pairs (g, α) with g ∈ G and α an isomorphism L → g<sup>\*</sup>L. It is a central extension of G by 𝔾<sub>m</sub> with a canonical weight r of type ρ. Its dual group G<sup>D</sup> sits in an exact sequence 1 → G<sup>∨</sup> → G<sup>D</sup> → 𝔾<sub>m</sub> → 1, with projection p.
- Example: for G = PGL(2), G̃ is GL(2), G<sup>D</sup> is GL(2, ℂ), and p is the determinant.

**Enhanced group.** A group D with a homomorphism ε from μ<sub>2</sub> to its centre. For G<sup>∨</sup>, ε is the restriction to μ<sub>2</sub> of the sum of positive roots of G, read as a cocharacter of the dual torus.

## Main results

**Proposition (structure of G<sup>D</sup>; Section 4.1).** The group G<sup>D</sup> is the quotient of 𝔾<sub>m</sub> × G<sup>∨</sup> by the subgroup of order 2 generated by the central element (−1, ε).
- Step 1: a central map j from 𝔾<sub>m</sub> to G<sup>D</sup> composes with p to give squaring.
- Step 2: so G<sup>∨</sup> and the image of j generate G<sup>D</sup>, and j(−1) is the element ε of G<sup>∨</sup>.

**Conjecture (modified correspondence LLC′; Section 3.6).** There should be a canonical correspondence between irreducible smooth representations of G(F) and homomorphisms ψ from the Weil group to G<sup>D</sup>, up to conjugation by G<sup>∨</sup>, such that p∘ψ is the modulus character ω with ω(Frob) = q. Bernstein constructs ψ for representations of principal series type, where the non-normalised Jacquet module is non-zero.
- Step 1: the characters in the Jacquet module lie on one orbit of the "dot action" of the Weyl group.
- Step 2: multiplying by |r| turns the dot action into the ordinary action, so the characters define one parameter.

**Theorem (absolute Satake isomorphism; Section 5).** Let X<sub>q</sub> be the fibre of p over q, the size of the residue field. The spherical Hecke algebra of G with coefficients in C is canonically isomorphic to the algebra of functions on X<sub>q</sub> invariant under conjugation by G<sup>∨</sup>.
- Remark: X<sub>q</sub> is isomorphic to G<sup>∨</sup>, but not canonically. That is where √p was hiding.

**Proposition (geometric Satake, reread; Section 4.2).** The cohomology functor on perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian is a fibre functor only when it takes values in super vector spaces. The category is then equivalent to super representations of G<sup>∨</sup> on which ε acts as the parity operator. Bernstein says he extracted this from the proofs of Mirković and Vilonen.

## Note, then ignore

- **Split groups only.** The note treats split G over a p-adic field of characteristic 0 to keep the dual group simple. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- **Stacks and pure inner forms.** Bernstein mentions that both sides should really be stacks, and the representation side should include all pure inner forms. He then sets this aside, and so can you.
- **The two-fold cover H.** The kernel of j on G̃ is a double cover H of G, and it is the object of real interest. It may be disconnected, so G̃ is used to define a dual group. Safe to skip.
- **Identifying old and new parameters.** A choice of square root δ of ω identifies the two kinds of parameter by ψ = δ·φ. The point of the note is that this identification is not canonical. Note it and move on.
- **R-functions.** Section 7 renormalises automorphic L-functions so that s = 0 is the natural special point. It is conjectural and separate from the main thread. Safe to skip.
