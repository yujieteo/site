## Before you start

These are eleven pages of talk notes, yet they lean on the plus construction, λ-rings, Bott periodicity and modular representation theory all at once. That can raise a quiet worry that you lack half the background. The worry is reasonable, and you can let it sit there. If you want to see one complete computation of higher K-groups, this is the shortest route to it. Read for the pleasure of the trick. Finishing can wait.

## The story

Quillen defined higher K-groups of a ring R as homotopy groups of a space BGL(R)<sup>+</sup>. Homotopy groups are hard to compute, and for R = ℤ they are still not fully known. For a finite field 𝔽<sub>q</sub>, Quillen found a way in. Topological K-theory has Adams operations ψ<sup>k</sup>, and ψ<sup>q</sup> acts on the classifying space BU. Its homotopy fixed points form a space Fψ<sup>q</sup>, and Bott periodicity gives the homotopy groups of Fψ<sup>q</sup> in two lines. The bridge is the Brauer lift. It turns a representation over 𝔽<sub>q</sub> into a virtual complex representation that ψ<sup>q</sup> fixes. This gives a map BGL(𝔽<sub>q</sub>) → Fψ<sup>q</sup>. Quillen shows that the map is an isomorphism on integral homology, so the plus construction is equivalent to Fψ<sup>q</sup>. The K-groups drop out.

## Main definitions

**Plus construction.** Let X be a pointed connected CW-complex and P a perfect normal subgroup of π<sub>1</sub>(X). A plus construction is an acyclic map X → X<sup>+</sup> whose kernel on π<sub>1</sub> is exactly P. For a commutative ring R one takes X = BGL(R) and P = E(R), the group generated by elementary matrices, and sets K<sub>n</sub>(R) = π<sub>n</sub>(BGL(R)<sup>+</sup>) for n ≥ 1.
- Step 1: attach 2-cells to kill P.
- Step 2: attach 3-cells to restore the homology.

**Adams operations.** An augmented λ-ring, such as K<sup>0</sup>(X) for a finite CW-complex X or the complex representation ring R<sub>ℂ</sub>(G) of a finite group G, carries operations ψ<sup>k</sup> built from the exterior powers. In these two cases each ψ<sup>k</sup> is a ring homomorphism, ψ<sup>k</sup>ψ<sup>l</sup> = ψ<sup>kl</sup>, and ψ<sup>k</sup>(L) = L<sup>⊗k</sup> for a line L. On characters, ψ<sup>k</sup>χ(g) = χ(g<sup>k</sup>).

**Brauer lift.** Fix an embedding ρ of the units of the algebraic closure of 𝔽<sub>q</sub> into ℂ<sup>×</sup>. For a representation V of a finite group G over that algebraic closure, the Brauer character sends g to the sum of ρ(λ) over the eigenvalues λ of g, counted with multiplicity. The Brauer lift of V is the virtual complex representation with this character.

**The space Fψ<sup>q</sup>.** Fψ<sup>q</sup> is the space of pairs (x, γ), where x is a point of BU and γ is a path from x to ψ<sup>q</sup>(x). It is the homotopy fibre of 1 − ψ<sup>q</sup> on BU (Lemma 6.7).

## Main results

**Theorem (Quillen; Theorem 3.1).** Let q be a power of a prime p. For all n ≥ 1, K<sub>2n</sub>(𝔽<sub>q</sub>) = 0 and K<sub>2n−1</sub>(𝔽<sub>q</sub>) ≅ ℤ/(q<sup>n</sup> − 1). A field extension 𝔽<sub>q</sub> → 𝔽<sub>q<sup>m</sup></sub> induces an injection on K-groups. The Frobenius x ↦ x<sup>p</sup> acts on K<sub>2n−1</sub>(𝔽<sub>q</sub>) as multiplication by p<sup>n</sup>.
- Step 1: use the Brauer lift of the standard representation of GL<sub>n</sub>(𝔽<sub>q</sub>) to build a map θ: BGL(𝔽<sub>q</sub>) → Fψ<sup>q</sup>.
- Step 2: show θ is an integral homology isomorphism, so that BGL(𝔽<sub>q</sub>)<sup>+</sup> ≃ Fψ<sup>q</sup>. The notes do not prove this step. They point to Quillen's paper for it.

**Lemma (homotopy of Fψ<sup>q</sup>; Lemma 6.8).** Fψ<sup>q</sup> is a simple space, with π<sub>2n</sub> = 0 and π<sub>2n−1</sub> = ℤ/(q<sup>n</sup> − 1).
- Step 1: by Bott periodicity, π<sub>2n</sub>(BU) ≅ ℤ and the odd groups vanish.
- Step 2: ψ<sup>q</sup> acts on π<sub>2n</sub>(BU) by q<sup>n</sup> (Lemma 4.24), so the long exact sequence of the fibration gives the cokernel of 1 − q<sup>n</sup>.

**Theorem (Green; Theorem 5.8).** For a finite group G and a finite-dimensional representation V over the algebraic closure of 𝔽<sub>q</sub>, the Brauer character of V is the character of a unique virtual complex representation.

**Theorem (Barratt, Priddy, Quillen; Theorem 2.1).** The plus construction of BΣ<sub>∞</sub> with respect to A<sub>∞</sub> is equivalent to Ω<sup>∞</sup>Σ<sup>∞</sup>S<sup>0</sup>. So its homotopy groups are the stable homotopy groups of spheres, and these map to K<sub>n</sub>(R) for every ring R.

## Note, then ignore

- K<sub>0</sub> is left out. The plus construction only gives K<sub>n</sub> for n ≥ 1, and the notes say K<sub>0</sub> needs a separate definition. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- λ-ring terminology. The notes call "λ-ring" what some authors call a "pre-λ-ring", and Remark 4.5 explains the extra axioms. Safe to skip.
- The embedding ρ. Everything after Convention 5.3 depends on this choice, and the notes say the dependence causes no trouble. Safe to skip.
- Atiyah–Segal completion. Lemma 6.10 needs it to pass from maps into BU fixed by ψ<sup>q</sup> to maps into Fψ<sup>q</sup>. The notes take the lemma for granted, and so can you.
- Starred items. Items marked with a star were extra remarks for the talk, including the link between K<sub>4n</sub>(ℤ) and the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture. Safe to skip.
