## Before you start

The main formula has four variables and six theta functions, and it arrives on page 2. The likely feeling is a flinch at the notation, and a worry that you must unpack every symbol before going on. You do not have to, and the flinch is allowed. If you like the idea that a whole infinite family of objects can hide in one closed formula, this paper is a clean case. Look at the formula for pleasure first. Checking it comes later, if ever.

## The story

A cusp form f of weight k has a period polynomial, built from the integrals of f(τ)τ<sup>n</sup> along the imaginary axis. Its even and odd parts each determine f. An earlier paper found that for level 1, all Hecke eigenforms of all weights, each multiplied by its periods, add up to a single generating function. That function is a short product of Jacobi theta functions.

Attempts at higher level failed. People came to think level 1 was an accident, caused by SL<sub>2</sub>(ℤ) having two generators and its ring of modular forms being free on two generators. This paper shows that belief was wrong. For every squarefree N the same kind of identity holds on Γ<sub>0</sub>(N). The right side is a product of a level 1 theta quotient in τ and one in Nτ. For N = 2, 3, 5 the identity even determines all the eigenforms.

## Main definitions

**Period polynomial.** For a cusp form f of weight k, r<sub>f</sub>(X) = ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>∞</sup> f(τ)(X − τ)<sup>k−2</sup> dτ. Write r<sup>ev</sup> and r<sup>od</sup> for its even and odd parts. For Eisenstein series the paper gives a separate definition in Section 2.

**Two-variable period polynomial.** For a Hecke form f (a normalised eigenform), R<sub>f</sub>(X, Y) = [r<sup>ev</sup>(X) r<sup>od</sup>(Y) + r<sup>od</sup>(X) r<sup>ev</sup>(Y)] / [(2i)<sup>k−3</sup>⟨f, f⟩], where ⟨f, f⟩ is the Petersson norm. Its coefficients lie in the number field generated by the Fourier coefficients of f.

**Hecke basis B<sub>k,N</sub>.** A basis of the modular forms of weight k on Γ<sub>0</sub>(N), N squarefree, made of eigenforms of all T<sub>n</sub> with n prime to N.
- Step 1: take newforms of each level N<sub>1</sub> dividing N.
- Step 2: lift them to level N with signed sums of the maps f(τ) ↦ f(dτ), so that each basis element is also an eigenvector of all Atkin-Lehner involutions.

**Jacobi theta function.** θ<sub>τ</sub>(u) = q<sup>1/8</sup> e<sup>u/2</sup> Π<sub>n≥1</sub> (1 − q<sup>n</sup>)(1 − q<sup>n</sup>e<sup>u</sup>)(1 − q<sup>n−1</sup>e<sup>−u</sup>), with q = e<sup>2πiτ</sup>. Write θ′<sub>τ</sub>(0) for its derivative in u at u = 0.

## Main results

**Theorem (level 1, from the earlier paper; formula (5)).** Let C<sub>k</sub>(X, Y, τ) = (1/(k−2)!) Σ R<sub>f</sub>(X, Y) f(τ), summed over Hecke forms of weight k on SL<sub>2</sub>(ℤ), Eisenstein series included. Then
(X+Y)(XY−1)/(X<sup>2</sup>Y<sup>2</sup>T<sup>2</sup>) + Σ<sub>k≥2</sub> C<sub>k</sub> T<sup>k−2</sup> = θ′<sub>τ</sub>(0)<sup>2</sup> θ<sub>τ</sub>((XY−1)T) θ<sub>τ</sub>((X+Y)T) / [θ<sub>τ</sub>(XYT) θ<sub>τ</sub>(XT) θ<sub>τ</sub>(YT) θ<sub>τ</sub>(T)].

**Theorem (squarefree level; Theorem 1).** Let N > 0 be squarefree and C<sub>k,N</sub> = (1/(k−2)!) Σ R<sub>f</sub>(X, Y) f(τ), summed over f in B<sub>k,N</sub>. Then
(X+Y)(NXY−1)/(NX<sup>2</sup>Y<sup>2</sup>T<sup>2</sup>) + Σ<sub>k≥2</sub> C<sub>k,N</sub> T<sup>k−2</sup> = [θ′<sub>τ</sub>(0) θ<sub>τ</sub>((X+Y)T) / (θ<sub>τ</sub>(XT) θ<sub>τ</sub>(YT))] × [θ′<sub>Nτ</sub>(0) θ<sub>Nτ</sub>((NXY−1)T) / (θ<sub>Nτ</sub>(NXYT) θ<sub>Nτ</sub>(T))].
- Step 1: compute the Eisenstein part of the left side and check that both sides agree at every cusp of Γ<sub>0</sub>(N). The difference is then a cusp form.
- Step 2: show both sides have the same Petersson product with each Hecke cusp form. This uses the formula that writes periods as products with Rankin-Cohen brackets of Eisenstein series.

**Theorem (small levels; Theorem 2).** For N = 2, 3 and 5, the identity of Theorem 1 determines the Fourier coefficients and the periods of all Hecke forms on Γ<sub>0</sub>(N).
- Why only small N: in general the space of forms of weight k on Γ<sub>0</sub>(N) has larger dimension than the space of polynomials of degree at most k − 2, so the sum cannot be pulled apart. The paper shows the method failing at level 7.

## Note, then ignore

- Eisenstein terms. The sums include Eisenstein series, with a suitable meaning for their period polynomials and Petersson norms. This makes the identity exact. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The extra rational term. The term with T<sup>−2</sup> on the left has no modular form behind it. It matches the pole of the theta quotient at T = 0. Safe to skip.
- Weight 2. The series G<sub>2</sub> is only quasimodular, so the weight 2 Eisenstein space is one dimension smaller than expected. The paper handles this by hand. Safe to skip.
- Normalisation of the slash operator. The authors work in PGL<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup>(ℝ) with a factor (ad − bc)<sup>k/2</sup>. It lets the Atkin-Lehner matrices of determinant M act directly. Safe to skip.
- History. An earlier 2014 paper by the first two authors proved the prime level case. The reorganised proof here covers all squarefree N. Safe to skip.
