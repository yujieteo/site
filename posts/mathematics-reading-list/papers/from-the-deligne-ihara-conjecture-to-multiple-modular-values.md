## Before you start

This talk passes through Galois groups, mixed Tate motives, Tannakian groups and modular forms in a few pages. You may feel you are too slow, or that everyone else knows these words already. That feeling is allowed. It fits a survey that covers thirty years of work. If you want to see why the number 691 turns up among zeta values, the answer is here with explicit numbers. Enjoy the worked examples first. Finishing is optional.

## The story

The Galois group of ℚ acts on the pro-ℓ fundamental group of the line minus three points, X = ℙ<sup>1</sup> minus {0, 1, ∞}. Ihara turned this action into a graded Lie algebra and found elements σ<sub>3</sub>, σ<sub>5</sub>, σ<sub>7</sub>, … in it. The conjecture says they generate it freely. The obstacle is a near-relation in weight 12: a combination of [σ<sub>3</sub>, σ<sub>9</sub>] and [σ<sub>5</sub>, σ<sub>7</sub>] is almost zero, and the prime 691 appears. The way through is to work with periods, here multiple zeta values, and lift them to motivic versions that carry a coaction. Freeness follows from a basis theorem for them. The weight 12 defect matches the first cusp form. So the second half moves to the moduli of elliptic curves, where the periods are multiple modular values.

## Main definitions

**Ihara's Lie algebra g<sub>ℓ</sub>.** Filter the Galois group of ℚ by how far down the lower central series of the pro-ℓ fundamental group of X an element acts as an inner automorphism. The graded pieces in degrees m ≥ 1 form a Lie algebra g<sub>ℓ</sub> over ℤ<sub>ℓ</sub>. It contains the images of the Soulé characters, written σ<sub>3</sub>, σ<sub>5</sub>, ….

**Multiple zeta value.** For integers n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r−1</sub> ≥ 1 and n<sub>r</sub> ≥ 2, ζ(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) is the sum of 1/(k<sub>1</sub><sup>n<sub>1</sub></sup> ⋯ k<sub>r</sub><sup>n<sub>r</sub></sup>) over integers 1 ≤ k<sub>1</sub> &lt; … &lt; k<sub>r</sub>. The weight is n<sub>1</sub> + … + n<sub>r</sub> and the depth is r.
- As periods: they are the regularised iterated integrals of dz/z and dz/(1 − z) along the straight path from 0 to 1.

**Motivic multiple zeta value.** A formal symbol ζ<sup>m</sup>(n<sub>1</sub>, …, n<sub>r</sub>) cut out of the affine ring of the motivic fundamental groupoid of X. These symbols form a graded ℚ-algebra H with a period map H → ℝ and a coaction. The period map is conjectured to be injective.

**Relative completion and multiple modular values.** The unipotent completion of SL<sub>2</sub>(ℤ) is trivial. Its relative completion is a pro-algebraic group over ℚ that is an extension of SL<sub>2</sub> by a pro-unipotent group. Its periods are the multiple modular values. They include regularised iterated integrals of modular forms along the imaginary axis.

## Main results

**Theorem (motivic Deligne-Ihara, Brown; Theorem 2.1).** The de Rham Tannaka group of mixed Tate motives over ℤ acts faithfully on the de Rham fundamental groupoid of X between the tangential base points at 0 and 1. Taking the ℓ-adic realisation gives the original conjecture: g<sub>ℓ</sub> ⊗ ℚ<sub>ℓ</sub> is freely generated by σ<sub>3</sub>, σ<sub>5</sub>, ….
- Consequence (Corollary 2.2): the fundamental groupoid of X generates all mixed Tate motives over ℤ.
- Consequence (Corollary 2.3): every period of such a motive is a ℚ[(2πi)<sup>−1</sup>]-linear combination of multiple zeta values.

**Theorem (f-alphabet; Theorems 3.1 and 3.3).** There is a non-canonical isomorphism from H to the algebra of words in letters f<sub>3</sub>, f<sub>5</sub>, … with the shuffle product, tensored with ℚ[f<sub>2</sub>]. It sends ζ<sup>m</sup>(2) to f<sub>2</sub> and ζ<sup>m</sup>(2n + 1) to f<sub>2n+1</sub>.
- Step 1: injectivity comes from the coaction and the structure of mixed Tate motives, which rests on Borel's computation of the K-theory of ℚ.
- Step 2: surjectivity comes from showing that the ζ<sup>m</sup> with all arguments 2 or 3 have independent images. This uses a theorem of Zagier.

**Claim (the weight 12 defect; Sections 2.1 and 3.4).** The Ihara-Takao relation says that [σ<sub>3</sub>, σ<sub>9</sub>] − 3[σ<sub>5</sub>, σ<sub>7</sub>] is a rational multiple of an element that vanishes in depth two. On the side of numbers, 28ζ(3, 9) + 150ζ(5, 7) + 168ζ(7, 5) = (5197/691)ζ(12). So depth and the number of odd letters disagree from weight 12 on.

**Claim (genus one; Section 4).** The Lie algebra of the unipotent part of the de Rham relative completion is free. Its generators come from H<sup>1</sup> of the moduli of elliptic curves with coefficients in Sym<sup>n</sup>, which has dimension 1 + 2 dim S<sub>n+2</sub>. That is one Eisenstein class and two classes for each cusp form of weight n + 2.

## Note, then ignore

- Tangential base points. X has no points over ℤ, so paths start at unit tangent vectors at 0 and 1. Pretend they are the points 0 and 1 on a first reading.
- Choice of the map to the f-alphabet. The map depends on choices. Only the part of highest length in the odd letters is canonical. Safe to skip.
- "Motive" in genus one. No bound on extension groups is known there, so the word is used loosely for objects in a category of realisations. You can ignore this at first.
- Signs and normalisations in the Ihara-Takao relation. The exact constant depends on conventions. The plot needs only the 691.
- The sequel. A second talk on multiple L-values is logically independent. Skip it for now.
