## Before you start

The paper is twelve pages, but it leans on three areas at once: hyperbolic 3-manifolds, Bloch groups and Borel's theorem on K-theory of number fields. The likely feeling is shame at missing one of the three. Almost every reader is missing one. You can let that be true and still read on, because each ingredient is used only through one stated fact. If you want to see a number field decide a geometric invariant, this is a clean case. Read for the pleasure of the eigenspace trick.

## The story

A hyperbolic 3-manifold M of finite volume has two real invariants: its volume and its Chern–Simons invariant CS(M), which lives in ℝ/π²ℤ. People wanted to know when CS(M) is a rational multiple of π². There was no handle on this. The idea here is algebraic. Cut M into ideal tetrahedra. Their cross ratios define a class β(M) in the Bloch group of a number field k(M) attached to M. A regulator map sends this class to a complex number whose imaginary part gives the volume and whose real part gives CS(M). Complex conjugation splits the Bloch group into two eigenspaces. Volume sees only the minus part and Chern–Simons sees only the plus part. Borel's theorem counts the ranks. When the plus part is zero, CS(M) is rational.

## Main definitions

**Invariant trace field.** Write M = ℍ³/Γ with Γ a discrete subgroup of PSL<sub>2</sub>(ℂ). Let Γ<sup>(2)</sup> be the subgroup generated by squares. Then k(M) is the field generated by traces of elements of Γ<sup>(2)</sup>. It is a number field with a fixed embedding in ℂ, and it depends only on the commensurability class.

**CM-embedding.** An embedding σ of a number field F in ℂ is a CM-embedding if σ(F) is an imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field. F is a CM-field exactly when all its embeddings are of this kind.

**Bloch group.** Take formal sums Σ n<sub>i</sub>[z<sub>i</sub>] with z<sub>i</sub> ∈ F, z<sub>i</sub> ≠ 0, 1, such that 2 Σ n<sub>i</sub> z<sub>i</sub> ∧ (1 − z<sub>i</sub>) = 0 in the second exterior power of F<sup>×</sup>. Divide by the five-term relation. The result is B(F).
- Geometry: [z] is an ideal tetrahedron with cross ratio z, and the five-term relation is the two ways to cut the hull of five ideal points.
- Number fields: B(F) ⊗ ℚ ≅ K<sub>3</sub>(F) ⊗ ℚ.

**Eigenspaces B<sub>+</sub>, B<sub>−</sub>.** If F ⊂ ℂ is stable under complex conjugation, conjugation acts on B(F) ⊗ ℚ. B<sub>+</sub>(F) and B<sub>−</sub>(F) are its +1 and −1 eigenspaces. They depend on the chosen embedding.

## Main results

**Theorem (Borel, as stated in Section 2).** Let F be a number field with r<sub>2</sub> pairs of complex embeddings. Apply the Bloch–Wigner dilogarithm D<sub>2</sub> at one embedding from each pair. This map B(F) → ℝ<sup>r<sub>2</sub></sup> has kernel the torsion subgroup and image a full lattice. So B(F) has rank r<sub>2</sub>.

**Theorem (ranks of eigenspaces; Theorem B).** Let F ⊂ ℂ be a non-real number field stable under complex conjugation. Let r<sub>2</sub>′ be the number of conjugate pairs of complex embeddings that commute with complex conjugation. Then rank B<sub>−</sub>(F) = (r<sub>2</sub> + r<sub>2</sub>′)/2 and rank B<sub>+</sub>(F) = (r<sub>2</sub> − r<sub>2</sub>′)/2.
- Step 1: pick a basis of B(F) ⊗ ℚ and form its plus and minus combinations under conjugation.
- Step 2: the matrix of D<sub>2</sub>-values takes a block form, and Borel's theorem forces each block to have full rank.

**Theorem (rational Chern–Simons; Theorem A).** If the embedding of the invariant trace field k(M) is a CM-embedding, then CS(M) lies in π²ℚ/π²ℤ.
- Step 1: CS(M) modulo rational multiples of π² is the real part of the regulator of β(M), and β(M) comes from B(k(M)) ⊗ ℚ (Theorems 5.1 and 1.1, proved in a companion paper).
- Step 2: for a CM-embedding, B<sub>+</sub> = 0 (Corollary 3.2), and the real part of the regulator vanishes on B<sub>−</sub>.

**Conjecture (irrationality; Introduction).** If k = k(M) meets its complex conjugate only inside ℝ, then CS(M) is irrational. This would follow from Ramakrishnan's conjecture that the regulator is injective on B(F) ⊗ ℚ. No single case of irrationality is proved.

## Note, then ignore

- Several Bloch groups. The definitions of Dupont–Sah and Suslin differ by torsion of order dividing 6. The paper works after tensoring with ℚ, so the choice does not matter. Safe to skip.
- Two five-term relations. Suslin's and Bloch–Wigner's forms differ by z ↦ 1/z. The paper uses Suslin's because it is killed exactly by the map to the exterior square. Safe to skip.
- The factor 2 in the definition. The kernel is taken of twice the map z ↦ z ∧ (1 − z) for homological reasons. Over an algebraically closed field it changes nothing. Safe to skip.
- Corrected misprint. This preprint fixes a misprint in the five-term relation of the published version. Safe to skip unless you compare the two.
- Fields not stable under conjugation. Theorem 3.1 handles them by passing to F ∩ F̄. It is needed for the conjecture, not for Theorem A. Safe to skip on a first reading.
