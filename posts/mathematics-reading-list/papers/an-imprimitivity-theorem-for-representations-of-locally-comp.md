## Before you start

This is a 30-page paper in functional analysis, full of projective tensor products, approximate identities and Haar measures on cosets. The likely feeling is a small dread that the analysis will bury the idea. That is a fair response to these pages, and you can let it be there. If you want to know what "induced representation" means once the group is not finite, this paper shows where the work goes. Read the introduction and the example with ℝ and ℤ for interest. The estimates can wait.

## The story

Mackey asked how to recognise that a unitary representation of a locally compact group G comes by induction from a closed subgroup H. His answer was a system of imprimitivity: a projection-valued measure on G/H that moves correctly under G. Rieffel later reproved this with Morita equivalence of operator algebras. Both proofs lean on Hilbert space. On a Banach space there are no orthogonal projections and no adjoints. Grønbæk keeps Rieffel's plan and replaces the tools. Induction becomes the tensor product L<sup>1</sup>(G) ⊗ M over L<sup>1</sup>(H). Morita equivalence becomes his own version for Banach algebras. The projection-valued measure becomes a module action of C<sub>0</sub>(G/H), the continuous functions on G/H that vanish at ∞. The recognition theorem then holds on any Banach space.

## Main definitions

**G-module.** A Banach space X with a bounded, strongly continuous action of the locally compact group G. Integrating against a function turns X into a module over the convolution algebra L<sup>1</sup>(G), and the paper passes freely between the two views.

**Induced module.** Let H be a closed subgroup. View L<sup>1</sup>(G) as a right L<sup>1</sup>(H)-module by convolution. A G-module X is essentially induced from an H-module M if there is a G-module map i from the projective tensor product L<sup>1</sup>(G) ⊗<sub>H</sub> M to X, such that every right L<sup>1</sup>(H)-module endomorphism T of L<sup>1</sup>(G) gives a unique bounded operator on X that agrees with T ⊗ id through i (Definition II.1).
- Faithfully induced: i can be chosen injective.
- Strictly induced: i can be chosen to be an isomorphism.

**System of imprimitivity.** An essential Banach module action of C<sub>0</sub>(G/H) on a G-module X that satisfies u.(P.x) = (u.P).(u.x) for u ∈ G, P ∈ C<sub>0</sub>(G/H), x ∈ X, where (u.P)(g) = P(u<sup>-1</sup>g). The action must also be L<sup>1</sup>(G)-continuous, a continuity condition in the strong operator topology (Definition IV.8).

## Main results

**Theorem (imprimitivity; Theorem IV.9).** Let G be a locally compact group, H a closed subgroup and X a G-module. Then X is essentially induced from an H-module via L<sup>1</sup>(G) if and only if X carries a system of imprimitivity in the sense above. In that case the inducing is automatically faithful.
- Step 1: an abstract version for a bimodule P over Banach algebras A and B recognises induced modules through an action of the algebra E derived from P (Theorem II.3).
- Step 2: for P = L<sup>1</sup>(G), the image of E lies in the closure of the crossed product generated by L<sup>1</sup>(G) and C<sub>0</sub>(G/H), so an action of C<sub>0</sub>(G/H) is enough.

**Theorem (flat generator; Theorems III.1 and III.3).** For G locally compact and H closed, L<sup>1</sup>(G) is flat as a right Banach L<sup>1</sup>(H)-module, and it is a generator of the essential right L<sup>1</sup>(H)-modules. So L<sup>1</sup>(H) and the derived algebra E are Morita equivalent in the Banach algebra sense.
- Step 1: flatness comes from extending module maps with the help of a Bruhat approximate cross section for H in G.
- Step 2: the generator property comes from building a bounded approximate identity of L<sup>1</sup>(H) out of module maps L<sup>1</sup>(G) → L<sup>1</sup>(H).

**Theorem (strictly induced; Theorem IV.11).** Suppose X is essentially induced from an H-module Y. Then X is strictly induced from Y if and only if there is a constant C with Σ‖P<sub>i</sub>x‖ ≤ C‖ΣP<sub>i</sub>x‖ for all x ∈ X and all finite families P<sub>1</sub>, …, P<sub>n</sub> in C<sub>0</sub>(G/H) with mutually disjoint supports.

**Theorem (ℝ over ℤ; Theorem V.1).** Let T(t) be a bounded strongly continuous representation of ℝ on X with generator A, and let C(ℝ/ℤ) act L<sup>1</sup>(ℝ)-continuously on X. Then X is essentially induced from a ℤ-module if and only if, for x in the domain of A and differentiable P, the vector P.x lies in the domain of A and A(P.x) = P′.x + P.Ax.

## Note, then ignore

- Three grades of induced. Essentially, faithfully and strictly induced differ because a Banach space allows many completions of an algebraic tensor product. Keep "essentially" in mind and skip the others on a first reading.
- Modular functions. The symbols Δ and δ for G and H appear in most integrals because G/H carries only a quasi-invariant measure. Safe to skip.
- Measurable cross sections. The author calls the choice of these the one technical complication of the proof. Safe to skip.
- The b.s.o. topology. This is the strong operator topology on bounded sets, used to say which approximations are allowed. Safe to skip.
- Link to Mackey's version. For unitary representations the introduction says the strictly induced module is Naimark equivalent to Mackey's induced representation, not literally equal. Safe to skip.
