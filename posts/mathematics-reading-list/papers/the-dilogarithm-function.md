## Before you start

At 63 pages with many formulas, this text may raise overwhelm, the sense that you must check each identity before moving on. That feeling is reasonable, and you can let it be there. The text is two lectures. Chapter I is a reprinted 1988 article of about 16 pages and it stands alone. If you want to see one function tie together a power series, hyperbolic geometry and number fields, Chapter I gives you that. Read it the way Zagier wrote it, for fun. Chapter II can wait.

## The story

The dilogarithm Li<sub>2</sub>(z) = Σ z<sup>n</sup>/n<sup>2</sup> looks like a minor cousin of the logarithm. It has few known closed-form values (Zagier lists eight) but many functional equations, all cluttered with logarithm terms. It is also multivalued. Bloch and Wigner found a fix: add one correction term and take the imaginary part. The new function D(z) is real-valued and continuous on all of ℂ, and its functional equations lose their clutter. Then comes the surprise. D(z) is the volume of an ideal tetrahedron in hyperbolic 3-space with vertices ∞, 0, 1, z. The five-term relation says five tetrahedra on five points add up to zero. Hyperbolic 3-manifolds are glued from such tetrahedra, and some of them have volume given by ζ<sub>F</sub>(2) for a number field F. So ζ<sub>F</sub>(2) is a sum of values of D.

## Main definitions

**Dilogarithm.** Li<sub>2</sub>(z) = Σ<sub>n≥1</sub> z<sup>n</sup>/n<sup>2</sup> for |z| &lt; 1. It extends to ℂ minus the cut [1, ∞) by Li<sub>2</sub>(z) = −∫<sub>0</sub><sup>z</sup> log(1−u) du/u.

**Bloch-Wigner function.** D(z) = Im(Li<sub>2</sub>(z)) + arg(1−z) log|z|, with arg between −π and π. It is real analytic on ℂ except at 0 and 1, where it is continuous but not differentiable.
- Symmetry: D(z) = D(1 − 1/z) = D(1/(1−z)) = −D(1/z) = −D(1−z) = −D(−z/(1−z)).

**Bloch group.** Take formal sums [z<sub>1</sub>] + … + [z<sub>n</sub>] with z<sub>i</sub> ∈ ℂ<sup>×</sup>∖{1} such that Σ z<sub>i</sub> ∧ (1 − z<sub>i</sub>) = 0 in Λ<sup>2</sup>ℂ<sup>×</sup>. Divide by the subgroup generated by [x] + [1/x], [x] + [1−x] and the five-term elements. The quotient is the Bloch group B<sub>ℂ</sub>. The same recipe with z<sub>i</sub> in a field F gives B<sub>F</sub>.
- D extends to a linear map B<sub>ℂ</sub> → ℝ, because the relations divided out are exactly the ones D satisfies.

## Main results

**Theorem (five-term relation; Chapter I, equation (4)).** For x, y with xy ≠ 1: D(x) + D(y) + D((1−x)/(1−xy)) + D(1−xy) + D((1−y)/(1−xy)) = 0. The same relation for Li<sub>2</sub> holds only up to a constant and products of logarithms.
- Step 1: differentiate. All functional equations of Li<sub>2</sub> are proved this way.
- Step 2: or see it as geometry. Five ideal tetrahedra spanned by five points of the boundary sphere add up to the zero 3-cycle.

**Theorem (volume of an ideal tetrahedron; Chapter I, equations (7) and (8)).** An ideal tetrahedron in hyperbolic 3-space with vertices ∞, 0, 1, z has volume D(z). For general vertices z<sub>0</sub>, …, z<sub>3</sub> the volume is D of their cross-ratio. Hence a complete hyperbolic 3-manifold of finite volume, cut into ideal tetrahedra with parameters z<sub>ν</sub>, has volume Σ D(z<sub>ν</sub>), and the z<sub>ν</sub> satisfy Σ z<sub>ν</sub> ∧ (1 − z<sub>ν</sub>) = 0.

**Theorem (zeta values; Chapter I, Section 5, no number given).** Let F be a number field with discriminant d, with r<sub>1</sub> real and r<sub>2</sub> complex places. Then B<sub>F</sub> modulo torsion is isomorphic to ℤ<sup>r<sub>2</sub></sup>. Take r<sub>2</sub> independent elements ξ<sub>j</sub> and apply D after each of the r<sub>2</sub> complex embeddings. The determinant of the resulting r<sub>2</sub> × r<sub>2</sub> matrix is a non-zero rational multiple of |d|<sup>1/2</sup> ζ<sub>F</sub>(2) / π<sup>2(r<sub>1</sub>+r<sub>2</sub>)</sup>. Zagier gives only a sketch and credits many authors.
- Step 1: B<sub>F</sub> ⊗ ℚ equals a quotient of K<sub>3</sub>(F) ⊗ ℚ, called K<sub>3</sub><sup>ind</sup>.
- Step 2: Borel computed the rank of that group and the covolume of its regulator, and the regulator matches D.

**Conjecture (Nahm; Chapter II, Section 3).** Let A be a positive definite symmetric r × r rational matrix. Nahm's q-series f<sub>A,B,C</sub> is a modular function for some B ∈ ℚ<sup>r</sup> and C ∈ ℚ if and only if every solution Q of Nahm's equations for A gives a torsion element of the Bloch group. Zagier proves a classification for r = 1.

## Note, then ignore

- Two chapters, two bibliographies. Chapter I is the unchanged 1988 article and Chapter II is newer. Each has its own reference list. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Boxed letters in the margin. They point to endnotes after Chapter I that update the old text. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Definition of the Bloch group. Zagier says his version differs slightly from the usual one, and the identification with K-theory holds after tensoring with ℚ. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Branches of Li<sub>2</sub>. The function jumps by 2πi log|z| across the cut. D(z) was built to remove this. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Higher polylogarithms. The functions D<sub>m</sub> for m ≥ 3 keep some lower-order terms in their functional equations. They matter for ζ<sub>F</sub>(m), not for this story. Safe to skip on a first reading.
