## Before you start

A 146-page dictionary can raise boredom, or a quiet guilt that you are not reading it from A to Z. Both are fine to feel. Nobody reads a dictionary in order. If you want to follow supersymmetry or super field theories, this is where you check what osp(1|2) or "atypical" means in half a minute. Open it when one word makes you curious. Enjoy that entry first. Finishing is not the point here.

## The story

Physicists met symmetries that mix bosons and fermions. Such a symmetry needs a bracket that acts as a commutator on some pairs and as an anticommutator on others. That structure is a Lie superalgebra. The simple ones have a classification that resembles the Cartan list for Lie algebras, but with new features. One algebra can have several inequivalent Dynkin diagrams. The Killing form can vanish. Some representations, called atypical, do not behave like the ones you know. The proofs sit in scattered papers. The authors collect the statements, without proofs, into 64 alphabetical entries with cross-references, followed by more than thirty pages of tables. They aim to be elementary and easy to use. You get a lookup tool, with pointers to the original papers.

## Main definitions

**Lie superalgebra.** A vector space G = G<sub>0</sub> ⊕ G<sub>1</sub> over a field of characteristic zero, with a bracket that respects the ℤ/2 grading, is graded-antisymmetric and satisfies a graded Jacobi identity (entry 24).
- Even part: G<sub>0</sub> is an ordinary Lie algebra.
- Odd part: G<sub>1</sub> is not an algebra. The bracket of two odd elements is symmetric and lands in G<sub>0</sub>.

**Classical, basic, strange.** A simple Lie superalgebra is classical if the representation of G<sub>0</sub> on G<sub>1</sub> is completely reducible (entry 8). A classical one is basic if it carries a non-degenerate invariant bilinear form, and strange if it does not.

**Typical weight.** Let G be basic and let Λ be an integral dominant weight. Put ρ = ρ<sub>0</sub> − ρ<sub>1</sub>, the half-sum of the positive even roots minus the half-sum of the positive odd roots. Then Λ is typical if (Λ + ρ, α) ≠ 0 for every positive odd root α such that 2α is not an even root (entry 39). Otherwise Λ is atypical.

## Main results

All results are stated in the dictionary without proof. It gives references instead.

**Theorem (classification of simple Lie superalgebras; entry 9).** The simple Lie superalgebras in the classification split into two families, the classical ones and the Cartan type ones.
- Classical: the basic series A(m,n), B(m,n), C(n+1), D(m,n), the exceptional F(4), G(3) and the one-parameter family D(2,1;α), and the strange series P(n) and Q(n).
- Cartan type: four infinite families W(n), S(n), S̃(n) and H(n). For these, G<sub>1</sub> is not completely reducible under G<sub>0</sub>.

**Theorem (classical means reductive even part; entry 8).** A simple Lie superalgebra is classical if and only if G<sub>0</sub> is a reductive Lie algebra. In that case G<sub>1</sub> is either a sum of two irreducible representations of G<sub>0</sub> or irreducible.
- Type I, two pieces: A(m,n), C(n+1) and P(n).
- Type II, one piece: B(m,n), D(m,n), F(4), G(3), D(2,1;α) and Q(n).

**Theorem (invariant forms; entry 23).** An invariant bilinear form on a simple Lie superalgebra is either non-degenerate or identically zero. Any two invariant forms are proportional.
- Consequence: the Killing form, defined with the supertrace, can be zero on a basic superalgebra. This happens for A(n,n), D(n+1,n) and D(2,1;α).

**Theorem (typical means simple Kac module; entry 39).** Let G be basic and Λ integral dominant. The Kac module of highest weight Λ, built in the entry on highest weight representations, is simple if and only if Λ is typical.
- Example: every finite-dimensional representation of B(0,n) is typical.
- Example: every finite-dimensional representation of C(n+1) is typical or singly atypical.

## Note, then ignore

- The double bracket. One symbol stands for a commutator or an anticommutator, depending on the degrees of the two elements. It saves writing two formulas. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Two names for each algebra. A(m,n) is sl(m+1|n+1), B(m,n) is osp(2m+1|2n), and so on. When m = n the algebra A(n,n) is sl(n+1|n+1) modulo a one-dimensional centre. Safe to skip until you need that case.
- A slip in entry 8. One sentence there attaches "type I" to the irreducible case. The gradation theorem and Table II in the same entry show that type I is the two-piece case. Safe to skip once you know it.
- Many Dynkin diagrams. A basic superalgebra has several inequivalent simple root systems. The dictionary picks the distinguished diagram, the one with the fewest odd roots. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- No proofs. Every entry points to references for proofs. This is by design. Safe to skip the bibliography until a statement matters to you.
