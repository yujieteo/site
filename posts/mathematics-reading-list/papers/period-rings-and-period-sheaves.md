## Before you start

These notes are 14 pages, but most of that is 37 problems, several marked "Hard!". The likely feeling is fear of being too slow, or of failing the exercises. You can feel that and still read. The statements between the problems tell the whole story without solving anything. You may want to know what B<sub>dR</sub> really is, as a ring you could write elements of. Read for that pleasure first. Solve a problem only if one tempts you.

## The story

A proper smooth variety over a finite extension K of ℚ<sub>p</sub> has étale cohomology with a Galois action, and de Rham cohomology with a filtration. People wanted an isomorphism between them. None exists over K, or even over ℂ<sub>p</sub>. Fontaine's idea is to enlarge the coefficients to a "period ring" that carries a Galois action and a filtration at once. The notes build these rings in order. First Witt vectors of a perfect ring. Then the tilt of O<sub>ℂ<sub>p</sub></sub>, and a map θ from its Witt vectors back to O<sub>ℂ<sub>p</sub></sub>. Completing along the kernel of θ gives B<sub>dR</sub><sup>+</sup>. Adding divided powers gives B<sub>crys</sub>, which also has a Frobenius. A last section turns the rings into sheaves on the pro-étale site.

## Main definitions

**Strict p-ring and W(R).** A strict p-ring is a ring A that is p-adically separated and complete, with p not a zero divisor and A/p of characteristic p. For a perfect ring R of characteristic p there is a unique strict p-ring W(R) with residue ring R (Theorem 2.2).
- Step: each x in R has a Teichmüller lift [x], and every element of W(R) is uniquely a sum Σ p<sup>n</sup>[a<sub>n</sub>].

**B<sub>dR</sub>.** Let O<sup>♭</sup> be the inverse limit of O<sub>ℂ<sub>p</sub></sub>/p along x ↦ x<sup>p</sup>. The map θ: W(O<sup>♭</sup>) → O<sub>ℂ<sub>p</sub></sub> has kernel generated by ξ = [p<sup>♭</sup>] − p. The ring B<sub>dR</sub><sup>+</sup> is the completion of W(O<sup>♭</sup>)[1/p] along ker θ. It is a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field ℂ<sub>p</sub>.
- Step: t = log[ε] is a uniformiser, where ε is a compatible system of p-power roots of 1. The Galois group acts on t by the cyclotomic character.
- Step: B<sub>dR</sub> = B<sub>dR</sub><sup>+</sup>[1/t], filtered by powers of t.

**B<sub>crys</sub>.** Adjoin the divided powers ξ<sup>m</sup>/m! to W(O<sup>♭</sup>), complete p-adically to get A<sub>crys</sub>, then invert p and t. The result sits inside B<sub>dR</sub>. It inherits the filtration and also has a Frobenius φ with φ(t) = pt.

**De Rham and crystalline representations.** For a p-adic representation V of G<sub>K</sub>, set D<sub>dR</sub>(V) = (V ⊗ B<sub>dR</sub>)<sup>G<sub>K</sub></sup>. Its dimension over K is at most dim V, and V is de Rham when equality holds. Crystalline is defined the same way with B<sub>crys</sub> and K<sub>0</sub> = Frac W(k).

## Main results

**Theorem (comparison theorems; Theorems 1.1 to 1.3).** Let X be proper and smooth over K. The notes quote these as background, without proof.
- Case de Rham: étale H<sup>n</sup> of X over the algebraic closure, tensored with B<sub>dR</sub>, is isomorphic to H<sup>n</sup><sub>dR</sub>(X/K) ⊗ B<sub>dR</sub>, compatibly with Galois actions and filtrations.
- Case crystalline: if X has a proper smooth model over O<sub>K</sub>, the same holds with B<sub>crys</sub> and crystalline cohomology of the special fibre, compatibly with Frobenius as well.

**Theorem (D<sub>dR</sub>; Theorem 4.1).** On de Rham representations, D<sub>dR</sub> is exact and faithful, but not full. It respects sums, tensor products, subobjects, quotients and duals. For de Rham V, the natural map D<sub>dR</sub>(V) ⊗ B<sub>dR</sub> → V ⊗ B<sub>dR</sub> is an isomorphism.

**Theorem (D<sub>crys</sub>; Theorem 6.1).** On crystalline representations, D<sub>crys</sub> is exact and fully faithful into filtered φ-modules. One recovers V as the φ = 1 part of Fil<sup>0</sup> of D<sub>crys</sub>(V) ⊗ B<sub>crys</sub>.
- Step: crystalline implies de Rham (Theorem 6.2), and de Rham implies Hodge-Tate (Theorem 4.2).

**Theorem (fundamental exact sequences; Theorem 5.2).** There are exact sequences 0 → ℚ<sub>p</sub> → B<sub>crys</sub><sup>φ=1</sup> → B<sub>dR</sub>/B<sub>dR</sub><sup>+</sup> → 0 and 0 → ℚ<sub>p</sub> → Fil<sup>0</sup>B<sub>crys</sub> → B<sub>crys</sub> → 0, where the last map is φ − 1. The notes state these and call the proofs difficult.

## Note, then ignore

- Topology on B<sub>dR</sub><sup>+</sup>. The valuation-ring topology is too coarse for log[ε] to behave well, so Problem 11 builds a finer one. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- No Frobenius on B<sub>dR</sub>. The Frobenius of W(O<sup>♭</sup>)[1/p] does not extend to B<sub>dR</sub><sup>+</sup>. This is the reason B<sub>crys</sub> exists, and the proof is safe to skip.
- B<sub>max</sub>. Problem 28 introduces a variant of B<sub>crys</sub> with a better topology. It defines the same class of representations, so skip it.
- Fil<sup>0</sup> versus B<sub>crys</sub><sup>+</sup>. These two rings differ (Problem 25). The difference matters only in exact statements like Theorem 5.2. Safe to skip.
- Period sheaves. Section 7 repeats the constructions for perfectoid affinoid algebras and sheafifies on the pro-étale site. It takes one page and can wait.
