## Before you start

The title joins two subjects with heavy reputations: automorphic forms and perverse sheaves. The feeling it may raise is a worry that you need both before you can start. That worry is understandable, and here it is mostly unfounded. The category at the centre is defined by generators and relations drawn on a picture of Weyl chambers. If you like seeing a hard sheaf category turn into linear algebra, this paper does that. Look at the A<sub>2</sub> picture for fun before anything else.

## The story

Perverse sheaves are hard to hold in the hand, so people look for descriptions as diagrams of vector spaces and maps. Kapranov and Schechtman had one for the Cartan subalgebra 𝔥 with its root hyperplanes: a vector space for each face of the Coxeter complex, with maps along inclusions of faces. The quotient W\𝔥 is harder, since closures of its strata can be singular. For 𝔤𝔩<sub>n</sub> it is the space of monic polynomials stratified by root multiplicities. The idea is to take W-invariants at the level of the indexing category. Objects become standard parabolics. The two kinds of maps behave like parabolic induction (Eisenstein series) and restriction (constant term). Composing a restriction after an induction gives a sum over double cosets of W, the same shape as Langlands' constant term formula.

## Main definitions

**Tits product.** For parabolic subalgebras 𝔭, 𝔮 containing 𝔥, with cones C<sub>𝔭</sub>, C<sub>𝔮</sub> in 𝔥<sub>ℝ</sub>, the parabolic 𝔭 ∘ 𝔮 corresponds to the first chamber, in the span of the two cones, met by a segment from C<sub>𝔭</sub> to C<sub>𝔮</sub>. In Lie terms 𝔭 ∘ 𝔮 = (𝔭 ∩ 𝔮) + 𝔫<sub>𝔭</sub>. It is associative and not commutative.

**Double incidence category Q.** The k-linear category whose objects are the parabolic subalgebras containing 𝔥, with generators Ind : 𝔭 → 𝔭′ and Res : 𝔭′ → 𝔭 for each 𝔭 ⊂ 𝔭′. The relations are transitivity, Res ∘ Ind = Id, and two conditions on the composites τ = Res ∘ Ind through a common larger parabolic.
- Collinear transitivity: τ composes along three parabolics whose cones meet a line segment in order.
- Invertibility: τ is invertible between cones on opposite sides of a wall.

**Perverse Coxeter category C.** The category of W-invariants C = Q<sup>W</sup>. Its objects are W-orbits of parabolics, that is, the standard parabolics 𝔭<sub>I</sub> for subsets I of the simple roots. Its morphisms are W-averages of morphisms of Q. On associated algebras this is the usual invariant subalgebra.

**Perv(W\𝔥).** Perverse sheaves of k-vector spaces on W\𝔥, with k of characteristic 0, smooth along the strata that are images of the generic parts of the complex flats of the root arrangement. Strata are labelled by W-orbits of Levi subalgebras.

## Main results

**Theorem (main equivalence; Theorem 6.1.2).** For a complex reductive group G with Cartan subalgebra 𝔥 and Weyl group W, there is an equivalence Perv(W\𝔥) ≃ Fun(C, Vect<sub>k</sub>). The authors say the same statement and proof hold for any finite Coxeter group.
- Step 1: start from Perv(𝔥) ≃ Fun(Q, Vect<sub>k</sub>), so that W-equivariant perverse sheaves on 𝔥 are functors on the semidirect product Q ⋊ W.
- Step 2: compare the stack quotient with W\𝔥 by pullback and pushforward, and identify the image stratum by stratum.

**Proposition (Langlands formula; Proposition 4.6.1).** In C, for parabolics 𝔭<sub>1</sub>, 𝔭<sub>2</sub> with stabilisers W<sub>1</sub>, W<sub>2</sub>, the composite Res<sup>𝔤</sup><sub>𝔭<sub>2</sub></sub> ∘ Ind<sup>𝔤</sup><sub>𝔭<sub>1</sub></sub> equals the sum over double cosets w ∈ W<sub>1</sub>\W/W<sub>2</sub> of Ind ∘ τ ∘ Res. Each term restricts to 𝔭<sub>1</sub> ∘ w𝔭<sub>2</sub>, applies the braiding operator τ to w𝔭<sub>2</sub> ∘ 𝔭<sub>1</sub>, and induces up to w𝔭<sub>2</sub>.
- Step 1: in Q the same identity holds with a single term (Proposition 4.3.3), by collinearity of four parabolics.
- Step 2: averaging over W turns one term into the double coset sum.

**Theorem (type A; Theorem 5.4.4).** For G = GL<sub>n</sub>, the category Q ⋊ S<sub>n</sub> is equivalent to the degree n block of the universal braided category generated by a set bialgebra (a Hopf monoid in species, in a braided setting). There is also a functor from the degree n block of the universal braided category of graded bialgebras to C for GL<sub>n</sub> (Proposition 5.7.1).

**Conjecture.** That last functor is an equivalence, and C is equivalent to a category given by explicit generators and relations in unpublished notes of J. Tao.

## Note, then ignore

- No functional equations. The operators τ satisfy braid relations only. In automorphic theory they descend to the Weyl group through functional equations, and C drops that step on purpose. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- Flats are not root arrangements. The arrangement induced on a flat need not come from a root system. D<sub>4</sub> gives an example. Safe to skip.
- Infinite-dimensional stalks. The authors do not require finite-dimensional stalks, so functors land in all vector spaces. Safe to skip.
- Shift convention. The perversity normalisation differs by a shift from the earlier Kapranov–Schechtman paper. Safe to skip.
- Averaging and composition. Averaging over W does not respect composition in general. Lemma 4.5.1 lists when it does. Safe to skip at first.
