## Before you start

A survey like this one lists many theorems from many fields: geometry, topology, representation theory. The feeling it tends to raise is a fear of being spread thin, of following none of the threads to the end. You are allowed to feel scattered here. A survey is a map, and nobody walks every road on a map. If you want one clear picture of what the space of abelian varieties looks like, sections 3 and 7 give it. Pick the road you like. Completeness is not the point.

## The story

A<sub>g</sub> is the moduli space of principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension g. It is the quotient of the Siegel upper half space by Sp(2g, ℤ), so its rational cohomology is the cohomology of that group. People want to know this cohomology, and that of the compactifications. Full answers exist only for very small g. Two ideas give order. First, the Hodge bundle has Chern classes λ<sub>i</sub>, and these generate a "tautological" ring with one simple relation. Second, Borel showed that in degree below g the cohomology no longer changes with g, and there it is a polynomial ring on the odd λ-classes. The survey follows both ideas through the Satake and toroidal compactifications, where L<sup>2</sup> and intersection cohomology enter. An appendix by Olivier Taïbi explains trace formula computations.

## Main definitions

**A<sub>g</sub> and the Hodge bundle.** A<sub>g</sub> = Sp(2g, ℤ)\\ℍ<sub>g</sub>, where ℍ<sub>g</sub> is the Siegel upper half space. The Hodge bundle 𝔼 is the pushforward of relative 1-forms of the universal abelian variety. It has rank g, and λ<sub>i</sub> = c<sub>i</sub>(𝔼).

**The ring R<sub>g</sub> (Definition 1).** The graded ring generated by u<sub>1</sub>, …, u<sub>g</sub> with the one relation (1 + u<sub>1</sub> + … + u<sub>g</sub>)(1 − u<sub>1</sub> + … + (−1)<sup>g</sup>u<sub>g</sub>) = 1. It has dimension 2<sup>g</sup> as a vector space. It is the cohomology ring of the compact dual of ℍ<sub>g</sub> (Theorem 1).

**Compactifications.** The spaces that close up A<sub>g</sub>.
- Satake (Baily-Borel): the smallest. As a set it is the disjoint union of A<sub>g</sub>, A<sub>g−1</sub>, …, A<sub>0</sub>. It is singular.
- Toroidal: depends on a choice of fans. The Hodge bundle and the λ-classes extend to it.

**Stabilisation (Question 1).** A natural map H<sup>k</sup>(A<sub>g+1</sub>, ℚ) → H<sup>k</sup>(A<sub>g</sub>, ℚ) exists. Cohomology stabilises if this map is an isomorphism for g large compared with k.

## Main results

**Theorem (tautological ring; Theorem 2, due to van der Geer).** In the rational Chow ring and in the rational cohomology of A<sub>g</sub>, the λ-classes satisfy the relation above, λ<sub>g</sub> = 0, and there are no other relations. So the tautological ring of A<sub>g</sub> is R<sub>g−1</sub>.
- Case: on a smooth toroidal compactification λ<sub>g</sub> ≠ 0 and the tautological ring is R<sub>g</sub> (Theorems 3 and 4).

**Theorem (Zucker conjecture; Theorem 7, proved by Looijenga and by Saper-Stern).** For a locally symmetric variety X of Hermitian type, the L<sup>2</sup>-cohomology of X is naturally isomorphic to the intersection cohomology of its Baily-Borel compactification, in every degree.

**Theorem (Borel stability; Theorem 19).** The rational cohomology of A<sub>g</sub> stabilises in degree k &lt; g. In that range it is freely generated by λ<sub>1</sub>, λ<sub>3</sub>, λ<sub>5</sub>, …. Cohomology with values in a local system vanishes in degree k &lt; g unless the local system has a non-trivial constant summand.
- Step 1: identify cohomology in the stable range with Sp(2g, ℤ)-invariant harmonic forms.
- Step 2: control growth of forms near the boundary on the Borel-Serre compactification.

**Theorem (stability for Satake; Theorems 22 and 25).** The rational cohomology of the Satake compactification stabilises in degree k &lt; g to a polynomial ring on classes x<sub>4i+2</sub> (i ≥ 0) and y<sub>4j+2</sub> (j ≥ 1), by Charney and Lee. The x-classes restrict to odd λ-classes on A<sub>g</sub> and the y-classes restrict to zero. Its intersection cohomology stabilises in degree k &lt; g to ℚ[λ<sub>1</sub>, λ<sub>3</sub>, …].

## Note, then ignore

- Rational coefficients. Sp(2g, ℤ) does not act freely, so A<sub>g</sub> is an orbifold and the survey works with ℚ-coefficients almost everywhere. Skip the worry.
- Mixed Hodge structures. The low-genus results record Hodge weights, for example an extension of ℚ(−6) and ℚ(−3) in H<sup>6</sup>(A<sub>3</sub>) (Theorem 10). The Betti numbers tell the story. Skip the weights at first.
- Which toroidal compactification. Stability for toroidal compactifications depends on the choice of fans and is partly open. Skip section 7's second half on a first reading.
- g ≤ 5. The intersection cohomology of the Satake compactification equals R<sub>g</sub> as a graded vector space only for g ≤ 5 (Theorem 17). For g ≥ 6 it is strictly larger. Note it and skip it.
- The appendix. Taïbi's appendix uses Arthur's classification of automorphic representations. It is a separate text with its own prerequisites. Skip it.
