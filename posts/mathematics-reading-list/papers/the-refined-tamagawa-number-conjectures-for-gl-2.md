## Before you start

This paper is about 40 pages of Iwasawa theory, and its introduction cites dozens of earlier results. You may feel behind before you reach page 3. It is fine to feel that. The main theorem reads like a Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer formula, and you can follow its statement with only a rough idea of a Selmer group. If exact formulas for ranks appeal to you, this is one. Read the statement for pleasure first, and take the proof later or never.

## The story

For a newform f, the Bloch–Kato Selmer group at the central point should mirror the L-function: its corank should be the order of vanishing, and its finite part should have the size given by the leading term. Known proofs of exact formulas need analytic rank at most 1, a full Iwasawa main conjecture, and good behaviour at p. Kim replaces derivatives of L(f, s) in the variable s by a different kind of variation. He twists modular symbols by products of discrete logarithms at auxiliary primes. These "Kolyvagin derivatives" are the Kurihara numbers. Their first non-zero level gives the corank, and their valuations give every elementary divisor. The only inputs are a large image condition and a small piece of the main conjecture, which is known for ordinary primes.

## Main definitions

**Setting.** Let f be a newform of weight k for Γ<sub>0</sub>(N), p ≥ 3 a prime, O the ring of integers of a p-adic field containing the coefficients of f, and π a uniformiser. W<sub>f</sub><sup>†</sup> is the discrete Galois module V/T of the self-dual twist by k/2, and Sel(ℚ, W<sub>f</sub><sup>†</sup>) is its Bloch–Kato Selmer group.
- Large image: the image of Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ(ζ<sub>p<sup>∞</sup></sub>)) contains a conjugate of SL<sub>2</sub>(ℤ<sub>p</sub>).

**Kurihara numbers.** Let n be a square-free product of primes ℓ not dividing Np with ℓ ≡ 1 and a<sub>ℓ</sub>(f) ≡ ℓ<sup>k−1</sup> + 1 mod π. The Kurihara number δ<sub>n</sub> is the sum over a in (ℤ/nℤ)<sup>×</sup> of a modular symbol value at a/n times the product over ℓ | n of the discrete logarithm of a mod ℓ. It lives in O/I<sub>n</sub>, where I<sub>n</sub> is generated by the ℓ − 1 and the 1 − a<sub>ℓ</sub> + ℓ<sup>k−1</sup>.
- n = 1: δ<sub>1</sub> is the central L-value divided by a period and a power of 2πi.

**Order and derivatives.** ord(δ) is the least number of prime factors of an n with δ<sub>n</sub> ≠ 0. ∂<sup>(i)</sup>(δ) is the least π-adic valuation of δ<sub>n</sub> over n with exactly i prime factors, and ∂<sup>(∞)</sup>(δ) is the least over all n.
- Analogy: ord(δ) stands for the order of vanishing of L(f, s) at s = k/2, and ∂<sup>(i)</sup> for the i-th derivative.

## Main results

**Theorem (structure; Theorem 1.1).** Let ρ<sub>f</sub> have large image and suppose some Kurihara number at s = k/2 is non-zero. Then Kato's Kolyvagin system is non-trivial, and Sel(ℚ, W<sub>f</sub><sup>†</sup>) ≅ (F/O)<sup>ord(δ)</sup> ⊕ ⊕<sub>i≥0</sub> (O/π<sup>e<sub>i</sub></sup>)<sup>2</sup>, where 2e<sub>i</sub> = ∂<sup>(ord(δ)+2i)</sup>(δ) − ∂<sup>(ord(δ)+2i+2)</sup>(δ). So the corank is ord(δ), and the length of the non-divisible part is ∂<sup>(ord(δ))</sup>(δ) − ∂<sup>(∞)</sup>(δ).
- Step 1: an explicit reciprocity law with torsion coefficients relates Kato's Kolyvagin system to the Kurihara numbers.
- Step 2: a "relative" Kolyvagin system argument, with repeated global duality and self-duality, upgrades the usual upper bound to an exact structure.

**Theorem (non-vanishing; Theorem 1.2).** With large image, the Kurihara numbers do not all vanish in each of these cases: L(f, k/2) ≠ 0, or the Iwasawa main conjecture localised at the augmentation ideal holds, or p ≥ 5 is good ordinary and f is p-distinguished. Conversely, non-vanishing implies that localised main conjecture.
- A fourth case covers analytic rank 1 under extra hypotheses, including an injectivity assumption on a p-adic Abel–Jacobi map.

**Corollary (ordinary case; Corollary 1.5).** For p ≥ 5 good ordinary and p-distinguished with large image, ord(δ) equals the corank of the Selmer group, and the p-parity conjecture holds for f.
- Parity comes from a functional equation for Kurihara numbers.

**Conjecture (Conjecture 1.6).** With large image, the order of vanishing of L(f, s) at s = k/2 equals ord(δ).

## Note, then ignore

- Minimal integral periods. The numbers are normalised by a choice of period. The paper says the choice does not affect the main theorems. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- The dagger. † marks the twist by k/2, which makes the representation self-dual. Safe to treat as notation.
- Squares in the formula. Each finite summand appears twice, and the method uses the self-duality of the Galois representation. Safe to accept.
- The p = 3 case. Section 3 does extra work to allow p = 3 in the Kolyvagin system theory. Safe to skip.
- Other critical points. Theorem 1.8 gives a length formula at any critical integer r with non-zero L-value, also with nebentypus. Safe to leave for later.
