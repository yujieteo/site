## Before you start

The page is a bare list of about thirty schools, and each link opens several lecture courses. The likely feeling is overwhelm, with a touch of greed: so much material, and none of it started. You can feel that and still pick only one thing. If you want notes written for graduate students meeting a topic for the first time, this archive is full of them. Choose a year by its title because it appeals to you. Nothing here needs completing.

## The story

The Southwest Center for Arithmetic Geometry runs the Arizona Winter School (AWS) each year. Every school takes one theme in arithmetic geometry. This page is the index of past schools. It runs from the 1998 workshop on Diophantine geometry and the ABC conjecture up to AWS 2026 on computational arithmetic geometry and cryptography. Each entry gives the year, the theme and a few links: general information, course descriptions with notes, and usually a schedule. From 2008 on the link text also promises video. From 2022 a second series, PAWS, appears beside the main school. The page itself holds no mathematics. Its use is as a map. You scan the titles, find the theme you care about, and go one click down to that year's notes.

## Main definitions

**AWS.** The Arizona Winter School, the annual school of the Southwest Center for Arithmetic Geometry. Each year has one title, such as "Perfectoid Spaces" (2017) or "Iwasawa Theory" (2018).
- Older entries (1998 to 2007) link to "Course descriptions and notes" and a participants list.
- Newer entries link to "General information, course descriptions, video and notes".

**PAWS.** A second series listed on the same page from 2022 to 2025, with its own themes, for example "Elliptic Curves and Abelian Varieties" (2023). The index page does not expand the name or say who it is for.

**Theme.** The single topic of a school. All lecture courses of that year sit under it, so the title tells you what the notes cover.

## Main results

**Claim (coverage).** The index lists a school for every year from 1998 to 2026. The themes move across arithmetic geometry and number theory.
- Early examples: local-to-global principles (1999), function fields (2000), modular forms (2001), periods (2002), logic and number theory (2003).
- Later examples: p-adic geometry (2007), arithmetic statistics (2014), perfectoid spaces (2017), nonabelian Chabauty (2020), abelian varieties (2024).

**Claim (what each entry holds).** Every entry links to a page for that year with course descriptions and notes. Entries from 2008 on also advertise video. I read only the index, so I did not check that each year's notes and videos are all present.

**Claim (link to this reading list).** Bhatt's notes on the Hodge-Tate decomposition, listed elsewhere in this reading list, are hosted under the 2017 school on this site. This follows from the address of those notes, which sits in the aws/2017 folder.

## Note, then ignore

- AWS 2021: this year is titled "Virtual School in Number Theory" and has no single theme in its title. Safe to skip.
- Schedule and local directions: these links are for people who attended. They hold no mathematics. Safe to skip.
- Sidebar links: "This Year's AWS", "Distinguished Lecture Series" and "Photo Galleries" lead away from the archive. Safe to skip on a first visit.
- Exercises and recordings: the index does not name them for each year. You find out what exists only on the page for that year. Safe to skip until you choose one.
