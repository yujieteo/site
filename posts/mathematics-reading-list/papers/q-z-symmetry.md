## Before you start

This is a physics paper about a group you already know well. The words around it (higher-form symmetry, gauging, invertible TQFT) may raise the feeling of being an outsider at someone else's seminar. That feeling is reasonable and you can leave it be. Section 2 is pure algebra: characters of ℚ/ℤ and the ring of profinite integers. If you like seeing a limit argument pay off in an unexpected place, start there and read for fun. The physics can come later or never.

## The story

Physicists classify symmetries of quantum field theories, and the usual groups are Lie groups or finite groups. What about a discrete group that is not finitely generated? The test case is ℚ/ℤ, the rotations by rational angles, with the discrete topology. The one idea is that ℚ/ℤ is the union of the cyclic groups ℤ/n. Symmetry by ℤ/n is well understood. So every question about ℚ/ℤ becomes a limit of answers for ℤ/n. Charges, which are characters, turn the direct limit into an inverse limit, and that inverse limit is the ring ℤ̂ of profinite integers. Anomalies behave the same way once a possible error term, called lim-one, is shown to vanish. The result is a classification where ℤ̂ replaces the integers, and a gauge theory whose "level" is a profinite integer.

## Main definitions

**ℚ/ℤ as a direct limit.** ℚ/ℤ is the direct limit of the groups ℤ/n under the inclusions ℤ/m → ℤ/n for m dividing n. It is also the direct sum over primes p of the Prüfer groups ℤ/p<sup>∞</sup>.
- The paper gives it the discrete topology, not the one induced from U(1).

**Profinite integers ℤ̂.** The inverse limit of the rings ℤ/n under reduction maps. An element is a compatible choice of residue modulo every n.
- By the Chinese remainder theorem ℤ̂ is the product over primes p of the p-adic integers. It has zero divisors.

**Anomaly group A<sup>d+1</sup>(G).** The abelian group of invertible topological field theories in dimension d+1 with symmetry G. It classifies anomalies of d-dimensional theories with that symmetry. In low dimensions and to first approximation it is the group cohomology H<sup>d+1</sup>(G, U(1)). The full answer uses bordism groups.

## Main results

**Theorem (charges; equations 2.2 and 2.10).** Hom(ℚ/ℤ, U(1)) ≅ ℤ̂, and this is also the endomorphism ring of ℚ/ℤ. The paper adds that ℚ/ℤ has no indecomposable complex representations other than these one-dimensional ones.
- Step 1: Hom turns a direct limit in the first slot into an inverse limit.
- Step 2: Hom(ℤ/n, U(1)) ≅ ℤ/n, and the inverse limit of these is ℤ̂.

**Theorem (group cohomology; equation 3.6).** H<sup>d+1</sup>(ℚ/ℤ, U(1)) is ℤ̂ for even d and 0 for odd d, for d ≥ 0.
- Step 1: the Milnor exact sequence writes the answer as an inverse limit over ℤ/n plus a lim-one term.
- Step 2: the restriction maps are surjective, so the Mittag-Leffler condition holds and lim-one vanishes.

**Claim (bordism classification; equations 3.12 and 3.16).** With no time-reversal symmetry, the bosonic anomaly groups for d = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 are ℤ̂, 0, ℤ̂ × ℤ, 0, ℤ̂ × ℤ̂. The fermionic groups are ℤ/2 × ℤ̂, ℤ/2, ℤ̂ × ℤ, 0, ℤ̂ × ℤ̂. The paper obtains these as inverse limits of cyclic-group classifications taken from the literature.

**Claim (physics argument; section 4.1).** The 3-dimensional Dijkgraaf-Witten theory with gauge group ℚ/ℤ has an action labelled by k̂ in ℤ̂. Its group of line operators is an extension of ℚ/ℤ ('t Hooft lines) by ℤ̂ (Wilson lines), namely (ℤ̂ × ℚ) modulo the subgroup generated by (−2k̂, 1). The paper derives this as a limit of ℤ/n gauge theories.

## Note, then ignore

- Topology on ℚ/ℤ. With the topology induced from U(1), the representation theory would be that of U(1) itself. The discrete topology is what makes ℤ̂ appear. Safe to skip once noted.
- The extra ℤ at d = 2. It is the gravitational anomaly, tied to the Pontryagin class of spacetime and not to ℚ/ℤ. Safe to skip on a first reading.
- 3-torsion at d = 4. The simple formulas for the two actions hold only when the background field has no 3-torsion. Safe to skip.
- A lost ℤ/2. For ℤ/n with 4 dividing n the fermionic group at d = 2 has a ℤ/2 factor. It dies in the limit because the restriction maps kill it. Safe to skip.
- Notation. The paper writes ℤ̂<sub>p</sub> for p-adic integers and ℤ/n for cyclic groups, to avoid the physics habit of writing ℤ<sub>n</sub>. Safe to skip.
