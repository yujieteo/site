### Einstein synchronisation makes equal one-way light speeds a convention

**Remember:** The far clock reads midway, so out and back take equally long.

*True by definition.* MSC 83A05 (Special relativity). arXiv physics.class-ph. Level 2: later undergraduate.

**Definition.** For two clocks at rest a fixed distance apart in an inertial frame, Einstein synchronisation gives a reflection the time midway between sending and return. If light leaves the first clock at τ₁ and comes back at τ₂, the second clock reads (τ₁ + τ₂)/2 when it reflects the light.

**Problem.** With Einstein synchronisation of distant clocks, the one-way speed of light is the same in both directions.

**How the definition helps.** The midpoint rule gives each leg duration (τ₂ − τ₁)/2 (our own). Both legs cover the same distance, so dividing by that duration gives equal one-way speeds (our own).

It says: “Since it is meaningless to measure a one-way velocity prior to the synchronisation of distant clocks, experiments claiming a measure of the one-way speed of light can often be reinterpreted as verifying the Laue–Weyl's round-trip condition.”

*True by definition.* MSC 83A05 (Special relativity). arXiv physics.class-ph, math-ph. Level 2: later undergraduate.

**Problem.** Let frame B move relative to A with rapidity w₁, and C relative to B with rapidity w₂, along one line. Then C moves relative to A with rapidity w₁ + w₂, which is Einstein’s formula (u + v)/(1 + uv/c²) in terms of velocities.

### Killing tensors conserve the Carter quantity

**Remember:** Feed it three equal velocities and its derivative vanishes.

*True by definition.* MSC 83C57 (Black holes), 83C40. arXiv gr-qc, math.DG. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** A rank-two Killing tensor is a symmetric tensor field K whose covariant derivative vanishes after symmetrizing over all three inputs. Equivalently, (∇ᵤK)(u,u) = 0 for every vector u (our own).

**Problem.** Along an affinely parametrized geodesic with velocity u, the value K(u,u) stays constant. In the rotating Kerr black hole, a special Killing tensor gives the Carter constant this way, up to combinations of known constants.

**How the definition helps.** Along the geodesic ∇ᵤu = 0, so the derivative of K(u,u) is (∇ᵤK)(u,u), which the definition makes zero (our own). For Kerr, the work is constructing the special Killing tensor (our own).

**nLab:** [Killing tensor](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Killing+tensor). It says: “For every Killing tensor K on (X,g) the dynamics of the relativistic particle on X has a further conserved quantity.”

**Wikipedia:** [Killing tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_tensor). It says: “Every Killing tensor corresponds to a constant of motion on geodesics.”

**Sources:**

- [Martin Walker and Roger Penrose (1970), On quadratic first integrals of the geodesic equations for type {22} spacetimes](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01649445)
- [Brandon Carter (1968), Global Structure of the Kerr Family of Gravitational Fields](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.174.1559)

### Kruskal coordinates remove the horizon singularity

**Remember:** Exponentiate the light coordinates and the horizon becomes an ordinary place.

*True by definition.* MSC 83C57 (Black holes), 83C15. arXiv gr-qc, math-ph. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** Kruskal coordinates replace the Schwarzschild light coordinates t ∓ r\* by exponentials, U = −exp(−(t − r\*)/(4M)) and V = exp((t + r\*)/(4M)), where r\* = r + 2M ln(r/(2M) − 1), G = c = 1 and angles are unchanged. In them UV = (1 − r/(2M))exp(r/(2M)), and the dU dV coefficient −32M³exp(−r/(2M))/r stays smooth and nonzero at r = 2M (our own).

**Problem.** In units G = c = 1, the Schwarzschild metric extends smoothly across its horizon at r = 2M. This radius is not a curvature singularity.

**How the definition helps.** The metric coefficient that blew up at r = 2M becomes smooth and nonzero, so the metric extends across the horizon (our own). Since UV has nonzero derivative in r at r = 2M, r is a smooth function of UV there, and the sphere part r² dΩ² stays regular (our own).

**nLab:** [Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Kruskal%E2%80%93Szekeres+coordinates). It says: “for maximaly extended Schwarzschild spacetime.”

**Wikipedia:** [Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruskal%E2%80%93Szekeres_coordinates). It says: “Note that the metric is perfectly well defined and non-singular at the event horizon.”

**Sources:**

- [Martin Kruskal (1960), Maximal Extension of Schwarzschild Metric](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.119.1743)

### Radial potential locates photon spheres

**Remember:** Light circles at the potential's peak, so it falls off.

*True by definition.* MSC 83C57 (Black holes). arXiv gr-qc, astro-ph.HE. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** Outside a Schwarzschild black hole of mass M, with G = c = 1, the radial potential of a light ray is V(r) = L²(1 − 2M/r)/r². Here L is the ray's angular momentum and r is the areal radius, with sphere area 4πr². The ray moves radially like a particle with (dr/dλ)² = E² − V(r), so circular orbits sit at stationary points of V (our own).

**Problem.** Outside a Schwarzschild black hole of mass M, circular light orbits have areal radius 3M. They are unstable under radial perturbations.

**How the definition helps.** A circular orbit needs V′(r) = 0, and for L ≠ 0 this holds outside the horizon only at r = 3M (our own). There V has a maximum, so a small radial push grows (our own).

**Wikipedia:** [Schwarzschild geodesics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzschild_geodesics). It says: “the unstable orbit &lt;math&gt;r<sub>crit</sub> ={3\\over 2}r<sub>s</sub>&lt;/math&gt;.”

### Surface gravity detects affine flow

**Remember:** Surface gravity is how much the horizon's Killing flow accelerates along itself.

*True by definition.* MSC 83C57 (Black holes). arXiv gr-qc. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** On a Killing horizon, a null surface to which a Killing field K is normal, the surface gravity κ is the factor in ∇ₖK = κK wherever K ≠ 0. It measures how far the flow parameter of K is from an affine one, which has zero covariant acceleration.

**Problem.** Along a horizon orbit where K ≠ 0, the flow parameter is affine exactly when κ vanishes along the orbit. At each such point, κ vanishes exactly when the gradient of the squared length g(K,K) vanishes.

**How the definition helps.** The first claim reads the definition directly: zero acceleration means ∇ₖK = 0 (our own). The Killing equation makes the gradient of g(K,K) equal to −2∇ₖK = −2κK, which gives the second claim. This is the full spacetime gradient, so it can be nonzero although g(K,K) = 0 on the horizon.

**Wikipedia:** [Surface gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_gravity). It says: “kᵃ ∇ₐ kᵇ = κ kᵇ implies −kᵃ ∇ᵇ kₐ = κ kᵇ”

**Sources:**

- [Bardeen, Carter, Hawking (1973), The four laws of black hole mechanics](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf01645742)

### Null frame coefficients detect geodesics

**Remember:** No sideways acceleration means a light ray follows a geodesic.

*True by definition.* MSC 83C60 (Spinor and twistor methods in general relativity and gravitational theory; Newman-Penrose formalism). arXiv gr-qc, math-ph. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** Pick real null fields ℓ, n with g(ℓ,n) = −1 and orthonormal spacelike fields e₁, e₂ perpendicular to both, and set m = (e₁ + i e₂)/√2. The spin coefficient κ = −g(m, ∇ₗℓ) records the two sideways components of the acceleration ∇ₗℓ, in one sign convention (our own).

**Problem.** The curves tangent to ℓ can be reparametrized as geodesics exactly when κ vanishes along them.

**How the definition helps.** Since g(ℓ,ℓ) = 0, the acceleration ∇ₗℓ is perpendicular to ℓ, so it has no n part (our own). Thus κ = 0 exactly when the acceleration is a multiple of ℓ, the test for a reparametrization to be a geodesic (our own).

**Wikipedia:** [Newman–Penrose formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newman%E2%80%93Penrose_formalism). It says: “shows that ℓᵃ is tangent to a geodesic if and only if κ = 0”

**Sources:**

- [Newman, Penrose (1962), An Approach to Gravitational Radiation by a Method of Spin Coefficients](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1724257)

### Incomplete geodesics need not encounter curvature

**Remember:** A geodesic can just stop, with no curvature blowing up.

*Makes the theorem clean to state or prove.* MSC 83C75 (Space-time singularities, cosmic censorship, etc.), 53C50. arXiv gr-qc, math.DG. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** A spacetime is geodesically incomplete if some geodesic, a path with parallel velocity in an affine parameter, stops at a finite parameter value and cannot be continued. The definition asks only that a path end, not that curvature blow up.

**Problem.** Assume a spacetime has a noncompact Cauchy surface, a closed future trapped surface, and Ric(k,k) ≥ 0 for every lightlike k. Then Penrose's theorem gives a future light ray that ends at finite affine parameter, and this alone does not require any curvature to diverge.

**How the definition helps.** Incompleteness only asks for a ray that stops, so curvature may stay bounded. For example, delete one event from flat spacetime: a straight light ray toward it stops at finite parameter, yet curvature is zero (our own). Penrose's theorem does the separate work of deriving incompleteness from trapping.

**nLab:** [Penrose singularity theorem](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Penrose+singularity+theorem). It says: “the singularity theorem makes statements about non-extendible curves but not explicitly about "singularities" in the sense of points of diverging curvature”

**Sources:**

- [Roger Penrose (1965), Gravitational Collapse and Space-Time Singularities](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.14.57)

### Null convergence bounds the distance to focusing

**Remember:** Converging light only converges faster, so it soon focuses.

*Makes the theorem clean to state or prove.* MSC 83C75 (Space-time singularities, cosmic censorship, etc.). arXiv gr-qc. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** The expansion θ of a family of light rays in four dimensions is the rate of change of log cross-sectional area density along each ray, in an affine parameter. Null convergence asks Ric(k,k) ≥ 0 for every null k, and then for rays leaving a surface orthogonally, which have no rotation, the Raychaudhuri equation gives θ′ ≤ −θ²/2 (our own).

**Problem.** Light rays leave a spacelike two-surface orthogonally, and one ray starts with expansion θ₀ &lt; 0. Under null convergence, that ray reaches a focal point, where the area density vanishes, within affine length 2/\|θ₀\| if it extends that far.

**How the definition helps.** From θ′ ≤ −θ²/2 we get (1/θ)′ ≥ 1/2, so 1/θ climbs from −1/\|θ₀\| to 0 within affine length 2/\|θ₀\| (our own). Then θ tends to −∞, so the area density collapses there or earlier (our own).

**Wikipedia:** [Raychaudhuri equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raychaudhuri_equation). It says: “As the expansion gives the rate of change of the logarithm of the area density”

**Sources:**

- [Raychaudhuri (1955), Relativistic Cosmology. I](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrev.98.1123)

### Conformal time measures light travel

**Remember:** Conformal time: light crosses one comoving unit per tick.

*True by definition.* MSC 83F05 (Relativistic cosmology), 53C50. arXiv gr-qc, math.DG. Level 3: graduate.

**Definition.** In a homogeneous, isotropic universe with scale factor a(t), conformal time η is defined by dη = dt/a(t), with light speed one. In a flat universe it makes the metric a²(−dη² + dx²), so time and fixed spatial coordinates carry the same factor (our own).

**Problem.** In a spatially flat homogeneous universe, a radial light ray travels one unit of fixed-coordinate distance per unit of conformal time. The particle horizon radius in those coordinates is the conformal time elapsed since the start of the model, if finite.

**How the definition helps.** For a radial light ray ds = 0, so \|dx/dη\| = 1 (our own). Integrating from the start gives the particle horizon radius as the elapsed conformal time, when it is finite (our own).

**Wikipedia:** [Cosmological horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_horizon). It says: “the particle horizon is equal to the conformal time that has passed since the Big Bang, times the speed of light.”

**Paper or page:** [Bonnet-Myers rigidity theorem for globally hyperbolic Lorentzian length spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17017v3). It says: “γ(t) = (t, ± ∫ₜ₀ᵗ 1/f(λ) dλ)”

**Sources:**

### Bondi news controls energy loss

**Remember:** Energy leaves at the rate of the news squared.

*Makes the theorem clean to state or prove.* MSC 83C30 (Asymptotic procedures (radiation, news functions, \\(\\mathcal \\)-spaces, etc.) in general relativity and gravitational theory), 83C40. arXiv gr-qc, math-ph. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** Near outgoing light infinity in Bondi coordinates, write the angular metric as r²q + rC + O(1), with q the unit sphere metric and C the trace-free shear. The Bondi news N = ∂ᵤC is the rate of change of that shear in retarded time u, and Bondi energy is the sphere average of the Bondi mass aspect (our own).

**Problem.** In four-dimensional vacuum general relativity with a smooth asymptotically flat Bondi expansion, Bondi energy never increases with retarded time. It has zero instantaneous loss exactly when the news vanishes in every direction.

**How the definition helps.** The vacuum Bondi mass-loss law makes the energy derivative a negative constant times the sphere integral of \|N\|², because the angular divergence term integrates to zero (our own). A continuous nonnegative integrand has zero integral exactly when it vanishes everywhere (our own).

### Bulk symmetries act conformally at infinity

**Remember:** A bulk symmetry preserves the boundary metric up to scale.

*Makes the theorem clean to state or prove.* MSC 83C30 (Asymptotic procedures (radiation, news functions, \\(\\mathcal \\)-spaces, etc.) in general relativity and gravitational theory). arXiv gr-qc. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** A smooth conformal compactification adds a boundary on which the rescaled metric h = Ω²g extends smoothly and nondegenerately, where Ω &gt; 0 inside, Ω = 0 on the boundary and dΩ ≠ 0 there. The boundary keeps only a conformal metric, the induced metric up to smooth positive rescaling.

**Problem.** Take a smooth conformal compactification with timelike boundary, whose induced metric has one negative direction. Any interior Killing field that extends smoothly to the boundary generates local maps that preserve the boundary metric up to positive rescaling.

**How the definition helps.** A Killing field preserves g, so it changes h = Ω²g only by a factor: ℒₖh = 2(KΩ/Ω)h (our own). Taking the h-trace shows KΩ/Ω is smooth up to the boundary, so KΩ = 0 there and K is tangent to it (our own). Restricted to the boundary, the identity says the flow changes the induced metric only by scale (our own).

### Cotton curvature supplies boundary stress

**Remember:** Cotton–York curvature is built to obey the boundary constraints.

*Makes the theorem clean to state or prove.* MSC 83C30 (Asymptotic procedures (radiation, news functions, \\(\\mathcal \\)-spaces, etc.) in general relativity and gravitational theory). arXiv gr-qc. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** On an oriented three-dimensional Lorentzian manifold, the Cotton–York tensor is the curl of Ric − (R/4)h: take its covariant derivative and contract the derivative index and first index with the volume form. It measures how curvature varies, and it is symmetric, trace-free and divergence-free for every metric h.

**Problem.** On a three-dimensional conformal boundary, whose metric h is fixed only up to a positive factor, take a constant multiple of the Cotton–York tensor as stress data. It is trace-free and divergence-free for each h, and changing h to Ω²h multiplies it by Ω⁻¹, as boundary stress data require.

**How the definition helps.** Because the identities hold for every metric, the curvature-built tensor meets the stress constraints for all representatives at once (our own). Its rescaling law is the one required of boundary stress, so the choice is consistent across the conformal class. The work lies in proving those curvature identities (our own assessment).

### Dominant energy bounds total momentum

**Remember:** Keep local energy large enough to pay for local momentum.

*Rules out counterexamples.* MSC 83C40 (Gravitational energy and conservation laws; groups of motions). arXiv gr-qc. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** Initial data, a spatial metric with its bending in spacetime, give an energy density μ and a momentum density J through the constraint equations. The dominant energy condition requires μ ≥ \|J\| everywhere, so local energy always covers local momentum.

**Problem.** Take smooth complete three-dimensional initial data without boundary, with integrable μ and J satisfying the dominant energy condition. If the metric error decays as 1/r and the bending as 1/r², each derivative gaining one inverse power through orders two and one, then total energy E is at least total momentum \|P\|.

**How the definition helps.** The condition removes negative-mass examples such as zero bending with metric (1 − a/√(1+r²))⁴ times the Euclidean metric, 0 &lt; a &lt; 1 (our own). This metric is complete with energy −2a and zero momentum, but its scalar curvature is negative, so μ &lt; 0 breaks the bound (our own). The spacetime positive mass theorem supplies the global estimate.

**Paper or page:** [The case of equality for the spacetime positive mass theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01984v3). It says: “Suppose (M,g,k) is a complete asymptotically flat initial data set satisfying the dominant energy condition (DEC) μ ≥ \|J\|. Then E ≥ \|P\|.”

**Sources:**

- [Sven Hirsch and Yiyue Zhang, The case of equality for the spacetime positive mass theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01984v3)

### Nearly round spheres recover total mass

**Remember:** Escaping spheres must even out their bending faster than 1/r.

*Rules out counterexamples.* MSC 83C40 (Gravitational energy and conservation laws; groups of motions), 53C21. arXiv gr-qc, math.DG. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** Nearly round spheres are surrounding spheres whose size is comparable to r, the least distance to a fixed point, and whose bending becomes equal in all directions faster than 1/r. Precisely, max distance ≤ Cr, intrinsic diameter ≤ Cr, area ≤ Cr², and the trace-free shape operator B obeys r¹⁺ᵗ sup\|B\| + r²⁺ᵗ sup\|∇B\| ≤ C, for the end's decay exponent t and one constant C.

**Problem.** Assume a three-dimensional end approaches Euclidean space with two derivatives at decay rate t &gt; ½ and has integrable scalar curvature. Along nearly round spheres escaping to infinity, the Hawking mass calculated from area and mean curvature tends to the total mass at infinity.

**How the definition helps.** The bound on B makes the error in the Hawking mass vanish as the spheres grow (our own). Without it, dilated copies of a fixed nonspherical Euclidean ellipsoid keep ∫H² a fixed amount above 16π by Gauss–Bonnet, so their Hawking mass tends to −∞ although Euclidean space has zero total mass (our own). The nearly round surface convergence theorem identifies the limit with total mass.

### Rescaling makes horizon inversion isometric

**Remember:** Divide by radius squared to turn horizon inversion into an isometry.

*True by definition.* MSC 83C57 (Black holes). arXiv gr-qc. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** In the extremal Reissner–Nordström exterior with mass M &gt; 0 and areal radius r &gt; M, in geometric units, inversion replaces r − M by M²/(r − M) and keeps time and angles. It multiplies the metric g by the square of new radius over old radius, so the rescaled metric g/r² is the natural one to compare (our own).

**Problem.** The inversion preserves the rescaled metric. On the extended exterior it exchanges the future horizon with the boundary reached by outgoing light, and similarly for the past.

**How the definition helps.** Dividing by r² cancels exactly the factor that inversion puts on g (our own). Since inversion sends r − M → 0 to r − M → ∞, it swaps the horizon with the region reached by outgoing light (our own). A separate boundary argument identifies where incoming and outgoing rays end.

**Paper or page:** [Peeling at extreme black hole horizons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14574v1). It says: “It fixes the photon sphere and exchanges the future event horizon ℋ⁺ and the future null infinity ℐ⁺”

### A quadratic test detects type D

**Remember:** Type D means W solves (W − λ)(W + 2λ) = 0.

*True by definition.* MSC 83C60 (Spinor and twistor methods in general relativity and gravitational theory; Newman-Penrose formalism). arXiv gr-qc, math-ph. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** At a point of an oriented four-dimensional spacetime, let W be the trace-free part of curvature acting on the three-dimensional complex space of self-dual two-forms, those the Hodge star sends to i times themselves. Petrov type D means W is diagonalizable with eigenvalues λ, λ and −2λ for some λ ≠ 0, so (W − λI)(W + 2λI) = 0.

**Problem.** Suppose tr(W²) ≠ 0 and set λ = −tr(W³)/tr(W²). The curvature has Petrov type D exactly when W² + λW − 2λ²I = 0, where I is the identity.

**How the definition helps.** For type D, tr(W²) = 6λ² and tr(W³) = −6λ³ recover λ, and the identity is the factored quadratic (our own). Conversely, λ = 0 would make the identity W² = 0, against tr(W²) ≠ 0 (our own). The distinct roots λ and −2λ then give diagonalizability, and zero trace in dimension three forces multiplicities two and one (our own).

### Curvature blocks twice differentiable extensions

**Remember:** Curvature can diverge where first derivatives still extend continuously.

*Rules out counterexamples.* MSC 83C75 (Space-time singularities, cosmic censorship, etc.). arXiv gr-qc. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** A C² extension identifies a spacetime with a proper open subset of a larger spacetime, keeping its metric, where the metric has continuous second derivatives in smooth coordinates. Then curvature is continuous in every smooth frame, while a C¹ extension, with only continuous first derivatives, allows curvature to blow up.

**Problem.** Each interior Lorentzian Hawking–Page metric in the cited family extends across its specified lightlike boundary with continuous first derivatives. No extension across that boundary has continuous second derivatives.

**How the definition helps.** A C² metric has continuous curvature in every smooth local frame, so curvature that blows up at the boundary rules out any C² extension (our own explanation). The cited paper proves that the sectional curvature blows up along the boundary for every extension. Constructing the C¹ extension and proving that blow-up are the substantive results.

### Strict trapping rules out flat cylinders

**Remember:** Strictly shrinking light fronts must focus; merely parallel ones need not.

*Rules out counterexamples.* MSC 83C75 (Space-time singularities, cosmic censorship, etc.), 83C57. arXiv gr-qc, math.DG. Level 4: research.

**Definition.** In a spacelike Cauchy slice, take a smooth compact separating surface without boundary with a chosen outer side. It is outer trapped if its outgoing orthogonal light rays start with strictly shrinking area, that is, future outward null expansion θ⁺ &lt; 0 everywhere.

**Problem.** Assume a spacetime has a spacelike Cauchy surface and Ric(k,k) ≥ 0 for every lightlike k. If a connected outer trapped surface separates the slice and its outer side has noncompact closure, then some future light ray ends at finite affine parameter.

**How the definition helps.** Strict negativity excludes surfaces whose outgoing rays keep constant area (our own). An example is a fixed-time torus in flat spacetime with two directions made periodic: its expansion is zero and every ray extends forever (our own). The outer trapped surface singularity theorem turns strict focusing and the noncompact outside into null incompleteness.
