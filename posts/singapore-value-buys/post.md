Most money spent on goods goes on small things bought again and again. This post covers those goods. It says what they cost, how the price has moved, what it may do next, what quality to look for, and how to tell a real deal.

## The short version

1. **Trust the unit price.** Compare per 100 g, per 100 sheets or per wash. Do not compare packs.
2. **Start with the house brand** for goods that are close to the same in any pack: sugar, salt, rice, oil, flour, paper goods and plain paracetamol.
3. **Pay more only for a thing you can check.** Toothpaste with 1,450 ppm fluoride is worth it. A shampoo with a nicer scent may not be.
4. **Buy fish in November.** Pomfret, prawns and threadfin usually peak in January, ahead of Chinese New Year on 6 February 2027.
5. **A good deal is 10% under the average.** The tables below give that price for each good.
6. **Do not wait for a price to fall.** Most goods drift up. If you need it and the price is fair, buy it.

Three things stand out. Eggs and coffee rose much faster than food in general. Petrol and electricity rose sharply in the past year. Phones, internet and clothes cost less than they did in 2015.

Some of the 2026 rise has a known cause. The rise in fuel is the clearest effect in this data. GST also went from 7% to 9% over 2023 and 2024.

## How to spot a real deal

A sale tag tells you what a shop wants you to feel. These checks tell you what you are getting.

1. **Work out the unit price.** 3 x 200 g of toothpaste at S$8.50 is S$1.42 per 100 g. A 250 g tube at S$6.10 is S$2.44.
2. **Compare with the average.** The tables below give the current SingStat average and a good deal price for each good.
3. **Compare the same quality.** A house brand under the average is not a deal. It is a cheaper product. For brands, compare with that brand's usual price.
4. **Ignore the "was" price.** Some offers on shop pages run for about a year. A price that is always on offer is the normal price.
5. **Count what you will use.** Buy 3 for S$5.60 is only a deal if you use three. Fresh food on offer that you throw away costs more.
6. **Stock up only on what keeps.** Rice, oil, canned food, paper goods and detergent keep. Stock up at 20% under the average.
7. **Add the deposit.** Since the Beverage Container Return Scheme began, most drink cans and bottles carry a [10-cent deposit added at checkout ↗](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/waste-management/beverage-container-return-scheme). Shop prices usually leave it out.

The good deal price is the current average less 10%, or less twice the good's normal monthly swing if that is larger. It is a rule of thumb, and it was set by judgment. Here it is at work on shelf prices seen on 8 November 2026.

## How the forecasts work

Each method was tested by standing at each month from 2020 on, using only the data known then, and predicting ahead. This covers the 2022 price surge and 64 goods with long records.

| Method | 1 month | 3 months | 6 months | 12 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Same as today | 2.06% | 3.52% | 4.59% | 6.12% |
| Same change as last year | 2.98% | 4.62% | 6.08% | 8.58% |
| Carry on last year's trend | 2.18% | 3.83% | 5.13% | 6.96% |
| Trend plus month pattern | 2.30% | 3.87% | 5.23% | 6.96% |
| Today plus month pattern | 2.12% | 3.51% | 4.61% | 6.12% |
| Today plus half the usual drift | 2.06% | 3.51% | 4.51% | 5.85% |
| Half the usual drift plus month pattern | 2.12% | 3.50% | 4.53% | 5.85% |

The table shows the average miss. Lower is better.

The result is humbling. Next year's price is mostly this year's price, plus a little. Trend-following did worse than doing nothing. The winner adds half of the good's usual drift, and it only beats "same as today" by 0.27 points at 12 months.

The tables use that winner. The range is where 80% of past misses fell for that good, widened if needed to include the middle guess. Goods with under three years of data use the pooled misses of all goods. Ranges lean upward because prices have risen more often than they have fallen. The forecast cannot see a new tax, a war or a farm disease.

The reasons are well reported. The northeast monsoon runs from about November to March and [cuts wild catch more than farmed supply ↗](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2023/01/19/higher-than-usual-surge-in-seafood-prices-in-singapore-due-to-weather-high-demand-for-cny/50993). Chinese New Year adds demand. In one January, [some vegetables about doubled and pomfret rose 20% or more ↗](https://mothership.sg/2021/01/vegetables-wet-market-price-increase).

Chinese New Year moves each year, so a month average blurs it. In 2027 the dates to plan around are Chinese New Year on 6 and 7 February, Hari Raya Puasa on 10 March and Hari Raya Haji on 17 May.

Big retailers can blunt the peak. FairPrice [froze chilled pork and some seafood and vegetable prices ↗](https://www.fairpricegroup.com.sg/fairprice-group-freezes-prices-on-all-chilled-pork-and-select-seafood-and-vegetables-to-celebrate-cny/) from 29 January to 3 March 2026.

## How to read the tables

- **Sep 2026** is the SingStat average, in the pack size shown.
- **1 year** is the change from September 2025.
- **Long run a year** is the yearly change from 2015 to 2025, adjusted for the sample changes. "Sample changed" means a sample change moved the price by over 5% in one month, so the long view is not safe. Use the CPI table for those. "Too new" means the series began in 2024.
- **Sep 2027 forecast** is the middle guess, with the 80% range in brackets.
- **Month pattern** gives the usual low and high month and the gap.
- **Shelf tables** are real products. Each link goes to a page opened on 8 November 2026, except rows marked listing. A date in brackets is when the offer ends.

Cigarettes are in the SingStat list. They are left out here because tax sets the price.

### What to check on the pack

- **Rice:** the Thai standard requires [at least 92% Thai Hom Mali ↗](https://thaihommaliricecertificationmark.dft.go.th/). "Fragrant" alone means less. No regulator defines "AAA" or "new crop".
- **Wholemeal:** [multi-grain and brown colour do not mean wholegrain ↗](https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/food-diet-and-nutrition/whole-grains). Look for "whole" in the name and a wholegrain early in the list.
- **Eggs:** [you cannot see Salmonella ↗](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-safety-tips/food-risk-concerns/risk-at-a-glance/salmonella-and-eggs). Keep them chilled. Nobody in Singapore defines "AA grade".
- **Milk:** "pasteurised" and "keep refrigerated" mean chilled milk. "Fresh" in a UHT carton does not.
- **Oil:** check the oil type. "Vegetable oil" at FairPrice is palm olein and soya bean oil. [HealthHub advises ↗](https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/food-diet-and-nutrition/the-oily-truth) at most 35% saturated fat.
- **Instant coffee:** in a [UK blind test ↗](https://www.which.co.uk/reviews/food-and-drink/article/best-instant-coffee-aOhpG1a1LpLR), own brands scored about the same as Nescafe Gold. These were UK products. "Freeze-dried" and "arabica" are facts on a label, not proof of taste.
- **Instant noodles:** read sodium per 100 g. FairPrice Curry works out at about 1,900 mg.
- **Soy sauce:** "naturally brewed", and water and soya beans first.
- **Luncheon meat:** sodium varies widely, from about 630 mg per 100 g for Tulip Reduced Sodium to about 1,400 mg for Spam Classic. [Processed meat is a Group 1 carcinogen ↗](https://publications.iarc.who.int/Book-And-Report-Series/Iarc-Monographs-On-The-Identification-Of-Carcinogenic-Hazards-To-Humans/Red-Meat-And-Processed-Meat-2018).
- **Drinks:** the [Nutri-Grade mark ↗](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/nutrition-hub/nutri-grade-mark) ranks sugar and saturated fat from A to D.
- **Sugar:** no quality test was found that sets one apart. Buy on price.

Small trays cost far more per kg than the SingStat average. A 300 g pack of Malaysian lean pork worked out at S$21.50/kg, against the S$15.60 average. Buy the larger pack if you will use it, or try the wet market.

Wet markets are not always cheaper, though. FairPrice's own survey of four markets in January 2026 put its own fish 12% to 35% below wet market prices around Chinese New Year. That is one retailer checking its rivals, so read it with care.

### What to check

- **Whole fish:** [clear eyes, red or pink gills, firm flesh that springs back ↗](https://www.fao.org/4/x5955e/x5955e01.htm). Yellow slime means stale. Thawed fish can fool the eye test.
- **Frozen fish:** reject packs that feel soft or show white patches or big ice crystals.
- **Chilled or frozen:** the [SFA says ↗](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-information/food-safety-education/good-food-safety-practices) chillers should hold 4°C or below and freezers -18°C or below. Do not buy cold food shown at room temperature.
- **Getting it home:** pick chilled goods last and use an insulated bag. Do not wash raw meat, as it spreads bacteria.
- **How long:** US guidance gives raw poultry and fresh fish 1 to 2 days in the fridge, and raw steaks and chops 3 to 5 days.
- **"Was" prices:** many Aw's Market offers at FairPrice run to October 2027. Treat those as normal prices.

Chicken shows how a shock works. Malaysia [banned chicken exports ↗](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2022/06/01/malaysia-suspends-chicken-exports-amid-rising-food-prices/) from June to October 2022, when it supplied about a third of Singapore's poultry. The forecast range for chicken is wide for that reason.

### What to check on the pack

- **Toothpaste:** look for sodium fluoride, sodium monofluorophosphate or stannous fluoride. A [Cochrane review ↗](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD007868.pub3) found 1,450 to 1,500 ppm prevents more decay than 1,000 to 1,250 ppm. Singapore caps it at 1,500 ppm.
- **Sunscreen:** SPF 30 or more, broad spectrum or PA+++ and above, and water resistance if you sweat. [Budget creams did well ↗](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/which-test-results-cheap-sunscreens-that-work-aOdTO2w4RTpi) in a UK test, but these were UK products.
- **Repellent:** [NEA registered ↗](https://www.nea.gov.sg/dengue-zika/stop-dengue-now/use-of-mosquito-repellents) repellents use DEET, picaridin or IR3535. Citronella sprays may have none of these.
- **Deodorant:** "anti-perspirant" and an aluminium salt if you want less sweat.
- **Face and skin:** gentle and alcohol-free. "Fragrance free" is not the same as "unscented".
- **Surface cleaner:** decide if you need to clean or to disinfect. [NEA lists ↗](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/public-cleanliness/environmental-cleaning-guidelines/guidelines/list-of-household-products-and-active-ingredients-for-disinfection-of-covid-19) the active ingredients and contact times. Never mix products.
- **Paper goods:** compare per 100 sheets within the same ply. No source proves more ply is better, but it is the best sign on the pack.
- **Paracetamol:** do not double up with a cold medicine that also contains it.
- **Razors, tissues, plasters and bin liners:** no independent test was found.

Fuel has no good deal price, because card and loyalty discounts matter more than the time you buy. SingStat prices are pump prices, so your discount comes off them. Diesel rose 50.9% in the year to September 2026, and petrol about 18%. The forecast ranges are wide, and they lean up after a shock like this one.

Electricity rose 9.1% in the same year, and it is 36.7% above September 2021. Water is 12.3% above its 2024 level. These prices are set by tariffs, so the saving is in using less.

These are grouped like Shopee's menu, plus the things Shopee does not sell, such as bus fares, bills and clinic visits. SingStat publishes no dollar average for most of them, so the tables use the CPI index, where 2024 = 100. An index tells you how fast a price is moving. It does not tell you the price. The shelf tables give real prices.

Most shelf prices here are search listings rather than opened pages, because many shops block automated reading. Rows without a shop page are marked listing. Treat those as a rough guide.

- **ERP:** expressway charges peak between about 7:35am and 8:55am. Leaving before that is the simplest saving.

## Limits

- **Sample changes.** SingStat changed its brands and shops in January 2019 and January 2024. Average prices are not pure price changes across those dates. The CPI is.
- **Averages are not your shop.** They mix brands and outlets. Promotions may be partly hidden.
- **Shops covered.** Shelf prices are mostly FairPrice, with some Cold Storage, IKEA, Courts, Harvey Norman and official fee pages. Sheng Siong, Giant and Don Don Donki showed no readable online prices. Shopee, Amazon.sg, Watsons, Popular, Challenger and several fashion sites blocked automated reading. Of 721 shelf prices, 352 were read from an opened page.
- **Shopee categories.** Shopee's site could not be read, so the category names follow its menu from memory and may not match exactly.
- **Indexes are not prices.** A word like "sofa" maps to the nearest official series, here Furniture & Furnishings. That series covers many products, so your sofa may move differently.
- **Offers end.** Many shelf prices are offers. Dates are shown where a page gave one.
- **Quality.** Many quality points come from agency pages. Some come only from search summaries of them. UK and US tests do not cover Singapore products. No named Singapore product has a test result here.
- **Data end.** SingStat data ends at September 2026, and the shelf prices are from 8 November 2026.
- **Toothpaste and shampoo** have no official item price in Singapore's list.
