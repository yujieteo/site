Tuning in to information flows

15 October 2022

This post was meant to be a response to Venkatesh Rao's article "Against Waldenponding" but instead I want to describe my own new information flows.

As for the basic premise of why even bother with anything but books, this is 2022. Everybody basically knows you cannot get away with just reading books to be an educated person. There is music, art, speeches, blogs, (social) media. Like it or not, one has to attain some mastery over all mediums of consumption in order to truly be educated (in the liberal arts sense) in this day and age. Of course, books are obviously a lot harder to enjoy. The hardest books to enjoy are definitely mathematics books (in the style of Rudin papa, grandpa and baby) but yet there are many people who claim to be totally literate without even struggling with the madness that is mathematical literature. And hence this discussion would be about the extremities of the consumed media (with low and high OODA latencies) so as to inform a multi-pronged strategy for reading.

The problem with staying plugged in all the time especially at lower information response latency is simply the Nyquist limit. Obviously if you want a holistic view of what people are doing day to day it is practically impossible unless you live two days in a day. This is also the same criticism I had on diaries. I want a complete view of what is going on and I am too stupid to extract useful insight based on a partial view of my day.

To give yourself enough time to digest it all, I think that the plugged in turnpike at lower OODA latencies is practically impossible. I also believe that this is the main issue people had with plugging in, and that the accusations of plugging out of this miss this point. These people are definitely absolutely okay with books.

Further, not everybody appreciates that people change based on the information medium, and not all latencies match up to an appropriate medium. A stronger claim would be to say that to manage information flow, one must manage different categories of information at different latencies. For example, some sitcoms like Yes Minister represent a whole generation of experience and insider stories from the British Civil Service, and yet the medium is that of a so-called low latency TV. I do not think the graph is accurate.

One additional point. It is indeed the case that there is a lot of noise, ripples and distortion when it comes to the high frequency spectrum of Twitter and talk radio.

But I will admit that there is merit to staying current with live events since you do need an effectiveness to handle it properly. I have yet to find a satisfactory platform, the most satisfactory being Richard Stallman's political notes (collection of news so it's in between hourly and daily frequency). There is the politically incorrect board on 4chan, but that I only treat as practice to keep myself sharp for high frequency low OODA loop latency news. I don't know of any reliable sources for high frequency events that are worth a lot of my headspace. This is ultimately what I think I should tune out of. For me, I eventually tuned out of modern music despite being brain food for ideas in our modern world at an exceptionally high OODA latency. Modern music now is absolutely fabulous, but I cannot keep up so easily since I love silence.

Wait a minute! I noticed that if you sample across a larger spectrum, of course you will have a diminished information sampling frequency that is incompatible with life. Perhaps it is better to push events and discussions about people all the way to the larger latency end of the spectrum if you want to learn anything useful and consolidate it well.

I will consider news blogs and TV (talk shows). But TV has gotten so bad recently. It is a problem with the medium, not the content. I hate irrelevant segments being put in in between talk shows. It is unclear whether fiction, dramas, or sitcoms fall under events or under ideas. My strategy right now is to do a mix of whatever there is when I have time to watch. I think news blogs are totally fine, and I have consulted many news blogs that carefully look at events.

Newspapers and magazines, their writing is not very relevant to me. What do I mean by this? I mean that if I can predict the stance of the editors and the journalists and prove I am right after reading the article, what is the point? I now rely on people to tell me of controversial articles instead. I wish there was a source that gave insider view towards the category of premium mediocre minds.

As for macro/long investing intelligence, these are what Warren Buffett reads most of the time. It is hard to get into, and I have yet to get into it but I am fine. In particular you can start getting into it with reference to this page by the US government on the form 10-K. College level education is great stuff. I love my textbooks without any pressure to do too well to learn about the world.

And lastly, historically informed books and non-news blogs already form the bulk of my reading apart from textbooks since they have the highest information response latency and of course, who could forget that they are ideas! Ideas are not always great. In addition, I am adding fiction to this list. Too many people of my kind have been stunted by not reading enough fiction. To me, fiction is like vegetables.

Another criticism of the turnpike which I have alluded to is that there are of course mediums like music (read: singing poems) releases which are early warning zeitgeist alarms but at an extremely low latency. Fortunately, fiction also falls under this category, but at least it is not too bad since there is a high information response latency.

In summary, here is my ideal strategy if I could stomach it and stick to it with discipline: (1) real life people for day to day, (2) news blogs, read lots of them, and dramas/variety shows/animes on a very very limited selection (1 per type), (3) read interesting newspaper articles, skip boring ones, read scholarly journals, (4) read what Warren Buffett is reading, read textbooks, (5) compile a list of historically informed books on a reading shelf. I still have my anthology of literary theory to wade through to make sense of leftist activism (which I found very interesting, I am not leftist though), as well as compiling interesting non-news blogs.

The one thing I can give credence to is if the stimulus is not interesting or different, then it is absolutely not worth reading. I think it is healthy to forget the irrelevant, I always go back to useful ideas that I picked up before to re-read. Pretty sure I am one of the few who likes to consolidate their few ideas more than to be a fox and pick up more and more ideas.

I am also kind of suspicious of people who read too many books and not enough of anything else. The world's knowledge is too vast to cram into books, and what I found is that people are far more important than books. For example, I could not have possibly learnt parameter-concept based thinking without the help of my vibrations lecturer, or the concepts of using graphs to accelerate intuitions for a consolidated understanding from my aerodynamics lecturer. So, it is essential to prioritise people, and I do think in a place full of scholars even if they are terrible speakers and teachers, it is easier to learn from a professor terrible at teaching than it is to learn from a great teacher without as much subject knowledge. I do believe it is the rambling that helps consolidate these ideas.
